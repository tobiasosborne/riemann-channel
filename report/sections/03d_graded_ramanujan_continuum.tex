\section{The Graded Ramanujan Property in the Continuum}
\label{sec:graded-ramanujan-continuum}

This section continues \cref{sec:graded-ramanujan}: the definitions are those of
\cref{sec:definitions-h}, the drafted statements are the orchestrator's (author claude:fable-5.1,
one codex prover lane, \texttt{notes/ramanujan-graded/}), and nothing here has had an independent
review.

\subsection{The continuum limit}

\begin{proposition}[Exactness under the continuum limit]
\label{prop:graded-continuum-exact}
\claimstatus{prop:graded-continuum-exact}{sketched-conditional}
For a graded cMPS generator $\Tcm$ ($Q$ even, $R_a$ homogeneous) the lattice channel ${\Edb}_\epsilon
= e^{\epsilon\Tcm}$ is a graded transfer channel with periodic ring norms $\str{\Edb}_\epsilon^n =
\str e^{n\epsilon\Tcm}$ for every $\epsilon$, and its divisor is the push-forward
$\nu_\epsilon(w) = \sum_{e^{\epsilon\lambda} = w}\nu(\lambda)$. Since $|e^{\epsilon\lambda}| =
e^{\epsilon c}$ iff $\re\lambda = c$, a line statement on all retained sector eigenvalues is
$\epsilon$-independent; the net-divisor statements (RH), (FE), (Ram) are $\epsilon$-independent for
$\epsilon$ small enough that no two divisor points alias ($\epsilon(\lambda-\lambda')\notin2\pi i
\mathbb Z$) and none aliases a trivial mode; at $\epsilon = 1$ a CP example whose rates all alias to
$e$ has a lattice divisor that cancels completely while $\str e^{L\Tcm}$ does not vanish between the
samples (prover, D7, ledger L10).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\str e^{L\Tcm} = \sum_\lambda\nu(\lambda)e^{\lambda L}$ (\cref{thm:cmps-sdet}); the exponential map
pushes forward signed multiplicities and sends vertical lines to circles. Corrected and proved by
the prover lane (D7).
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Graded Chernoff limit: the grading lives in the jumps]
\label{prop:graded-chernoff}
\claimstatus{prop:graded-chernoff}{sketched}
On a fixed finite bond let $\chan_\epsilon$ be parity-covariant CPTP maps with $\|\chan_\epsilon-1-
\epsilon\Tcm\| = \epsilon\,\delta(\epsilon)$, $\delta(\epsilon)\to0$, in an induced norm. Then
$\chan_\epsilon^{\lfloor L/\epsilon\rfloor}\to e^{L\Tcm}$ in norm uniformly on $[0,L_0]$, both ring
norms converge, $\Tcm$ commutes with $\Gdb$, and $\Tcm$ has a GKSL presentation with $[H,P] = 0$ and
homogeneous jumps $R_a$ (reached by a canonical change of noise basis, not necessarily by an even
unitary remix alone; ledger L38), i.e.\ $Q = -iH-\tfrac12\sum R_a^\dagger R_a$ is even. For any
homogeneous Kraus representation of $\chan_\epsilon$ the odd letters satisfy
$\sum_{a\ \mathrm{odd}}\|\Kr{a}(\epsilon)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2 = O(\epsilon)$ (Choi odd-block estimate): odd
letters are collectively of order $\sqrt\epsilon$ and can never be a drift letter close to the
identity ($PAP = -A$, $A = 1+B$ give $\|B\|\ge1$); individual limits $\Kr{a}/\sqrt\epsilon\to R_a$
require a choice of Kraus gauge (ledger L12). In the continuum, parity-changing dynamics is
dissipative and there is no Hamiltonian odd letter.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Chernoff's product estimate on a fixed finite bond; $\Gdb$-invariance passes to the limit; a
$\Gdb$-commuting GKSL generator has a homogeneous canonical presentation. Checked in
\cref{num:graded-ramanujan}(d) and by the prover's CPTP Euler family with first-order norm
convergence under mesh halving. Corrected and proved by the prover lane (D8, ledger L11--L12,
L38). This is why \cref{obs:jump-operator-guidance} needed an even--odd Hamiltonian on the
\emph{doubled} bond rather than an odd letter.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Jump Lindbladians: the band is affine]
\label{prop:jump-lindbladian-band}
\claimstatus{prop:jump-lindbladian-band}{sketched}
For $\Tcm = \sum_sg_s(\Ad U_s-1)$ with homogeneous unitaries closed under adjoint, $e^{t\Tcm} =
e^{-\gamma t}e^{\gamma t\chan}$ with $\gamma = \sum_sg_s$ and $\chan$ the weighted channel, so
$\operatorname{spec}\Tcm = \gamma(\operatorname{spec}\chan-1)$ sector by sector, and the graded
Ramanujan band for equal rates is $[-\gamma(1+\lamH),-\gamma(1-\lamH)]$ on both sectors; the
non-backtracking lift exists since the words are discrete.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\Tcm = \gamma(\chan-1)$ with $\chan$ and $1$ commuting. Proved by the prover lane (D9).
\cref{num:graded-ramanujan}(d).
\end{proof}

\begin{observation}[The divisor when there is no trace]
\label{obs:divisor-regular-data}
\claimstatus{obs:divisor-regular-data}{sketched-conditional}
For an infinite-dimensional graded bond the ring norms are distributions
(\cref{def:distributional-trace}) or correlation functions of regular data. The prover lane (D10)
makes this a \emph{relative resonance divisor}: given specified regular spaces, a meromorphic
continuation of the resolvent between them beyond $\re z = \omega/2$ (an assumption of the kind of
\cref{asm:explicit-formula-trace}), and finite-rank Riesz projections at its poles, the divisor is
the signed count of algebraic multiplicities of those projections, sector by sector; the order of
a scalar correlation pole does not measure multiplicity, and a general non-normal generator has no
scalar spectral measure, so with continuous spectrum one retains vector-dependent spectral measures
or supplies a spectral theorem with weights (ledger L13--L14). The definition is deliberately not the
$L^2$ spectrum of $\Tcm$: for the compressed Riemann semigroup $\|Z(t)\| = 1$ for all $t$ (two
readers, 2026-09-14, unreviewed), so no statement on $K_S$ sees the rate $1/4$; (HP) must then be
stated on a weighted or anisotropic space with domain and norm-equivalence qualifications, as
resonances are for Anosov flows. For the Riemann graded generator of \cref{prop:riemann-graded-generator}
the reference rates are $0$ and $-\tfrac12$, the archimedean ladder $-(k+\tfrac12)$ must be
designated trivial, and the regular nontrivial modes have centre $-\tfrac14$ iff RH; the realisation
of that generator as a doubled-bond cMPS is not supplied (it is a graded spectral transfer,
\cref{def:graded-rh-fe-ramanujan}).
\end{observation}

\subsection{Harrow's continuum limit, ungraded and graded}

\begin{observation}[Continuous Harrow: Ramanujan is temperedness]
\label{obs:continuous-harrow-tempered}
\claimstatus{obs:continuous-harrow-tempered}{sketched}
Replacing the finite group by a Lie group and the letters by $\exp(\pm\sqrt\epsilon X_j)$ gives
$\chan_\epsilon = 1+(\epsilon/2d)\sum_jB_j^2+O(\epsilon^2)$, the representation Lindbladian of
\cref{prop:quantum-lindblad-tensor} divided by $d$; with $\Tcm_* = \tfrac12(B_H^2+B_E^2) = 2\Cas+
\tfrac12B_W^2$ (shard 09b) the equally averaged two-generator walk has generator $\Tcm_*/2$ and
threshold $\tfrac14$, and $\Tcm_*$ itself (steps $\exp(\pm\sqrt{d\epsilon}X_j)$) has threshold
$\tfrac12$ (ledger L15). For an irreducible tempered $\pi$ of $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb R)$,
$\pi\otimes\bar\pi$ is tempered (Fell absorption) and on the $K$-invariant sector
$\operatorname{spec}(-\Tcm_*)\subset[\tfrac12,\infty)$, with the exact rate $2s(1-s)+m^2/2$ on a
constituent of parameter $s$ and $K$-type $m$; the bound is sharp for every $D_k^\pm$ (spectrum
exactly $[\tfrac12,\infty)$, endpoint not an eigenvector), there is no HS fixed vector, and for a
unitary representation of $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb R)$ the type-zero bound $-\Tcm_*\ge\tfrac12$ is
equivalent to temperedness: this is the precise diffusion meaning of ``continuous Ramanujan''
(prover, D11). It is not a flow-divisor line theorem: principal constituents have leading
matrix-coefficient exponents $-\tfrac12\pm ir$ along $a_t$, but discrete series are tempered with
faster exponents $-n/2$ and descendants, and discrete and continuous constituents coexist
(ledger L16--L18). Harrow's transfer becomes ``tempered implies tempered tensor square''; the
discrete zeros appear only on a lattice quotient (\cref{sec:selberg-tower}). For the complementary
series $\pi_s$ the tensor square is reported (from memory, unverified, H-repka in the prover's
report) to contain a complementary series iff $s>\tfrac34$, which would make the Lindbladian of
$\pi_s$ Ramanujan for $\tfrac12<s\le\tfrac34$ although $\pi_s$ is not tempered, the threshold being
Selberg's $\tfrac3{16} = \tfrac34\cdot\tfrac14$; not to be cited until byte-checked.
\end{observation}

\begin{observation}[Graded continuous Harrow: the $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb R)$ grading is the archimedean ladder]
\label{obs:graded-continuous-harrow}
\claimstatus{obs:graded-continuous-harrow}{sketched}
For $G = \mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb R)\supset\mathrm{PSL}_2(\mathbb R)$ the index-two graded genuine
irreducibles are the discrete series pairs $D_k^+\oplus D_k^-$ with $k\ge2$ \emph{even} (the centre
$-1$ acts by $(-1)^k$; odd $k$ needs a covering group or a projective bond, ledger L19), swapped by
the reflection $r = \operatorname{diag}(-1,1)$; even letters are the $\mathfrak{sl}_2$ generators
(diffusion $\kappa\Tcm_*$), the odd letter is the reflection, a jump at rate $\gamma$
(\cref{prop:graded-chernoff}). Exact decompositions (prover, D12): the even sector is
$D_k^+\otimes D_k^-\oplus\mathrm{c.c.}\simeq2\int^\oplus I_{1/2+ir}\,dr$, principal series only, with
$K$-invariant rates $\kappa(\tfrac12+2r^2)$ and $\kappa(\tfrac12+2r^2)+2\gamma$ for the two
reflection eigencombinations; the odd sector is $\bigoplus_{l\ge0}(D_{2k+2l}^+\oplus
D_{2k+2l}^-)$, purely discrete, with rates $\kappa(n+2nj+2j^2)$ and the same plus $2\gamma$
($n = 2k+2l$, $j\ge0$), odd minimum $2\kappa k$, and leading $a_t$-exponents $-n/2 = -(k+l)$:
an arithmetic ladder at and beyond the functional-equation partner, positionally the
$\Gamma$-factor ladder of \cref{prop:riemann-graded-generator} (the zero rung, multiplicities and
descendants differ; ledger L24), carrying no critical-line modes. $P$ is a bounded eigenoperator,
not Hilbert--Schmidt, so it supplies no HS divisor point (L21); the heat supertrace is not an
ordinary integral against $|\chi_+-\chi_-|^2$ (the even heat operator is not trace class), but the
localised regular-character functional is well defined and vanishes on hyperbolic elements because
$\chi_{D_k^+} = \chi_{D_k^-}$ there: the regular even and odd hyperbolic contributions cancel in
the supertrace (L22--L23), so the reflection grading assigns the closed geodesics to the even
sector and the archimedean ladder to the odd one, the opposite of the Riemann assignment. For
$k = 1$ (projective) $D_1\otimes D_1 = \bigoplus D_{2+2m}$ gives exponents $-(1+m)$ and its symmetric
part every other rung, matching the $\Gamma_{\mathbb C}$ and $\Gamma_{\mathbb R}$ pole lists
positionally.
\end{observation}

\begin{observation}[Which grading makes the Selberg zeros odd]
\label{obs:selberg-grading-choice}
\claimstatus{obs:selberg-grading-choice}{sketched-conditional}
The grading that makes the Selberg zeros odd is not the reflection grading but the transverse
form-degree grading of the geodesic flow: Dyatlov--Zworski write the Ruelle zeta as
$\zeta_1/(\zeta_0\zeta_2)$ over form degrees (\cref{cit:dz-order-chi}), using the Lefschetz
identity (\texttt{prov:dz13-lefschetz}, \texttt{1306.4203:zeta.tex:525-526})
{\small\texttt{\detokenize{\det ( I - \mathcal P_\gamma ) = \sum_{ k=0}^{ n-1} ( - 1 )^k \tr \wedge^k \mathcal P_\gamma .}}}
so the Selberg zeta $Z(s) = \det(1-L_s)$ is the odd-degree factor, its zeros $\tfrac12\pm ir_j$ are
net-odd divisor points on the critical line exactly when Selberg's $\tfrac14$ holds (the Ramanujan
row of \cref{obs:continuous-dictionary}), and its trivial zeros form the ladder. This is the
form-degree grading found for curves in \cref{sec:ring-norm-genus-one-tier-a}. \emph{Corrected
2026-09-16} (\cref{sec:selberg-letters}): the precise statement is that $\Zsel$ is the ring zeta
of the \emph{stable} two-term transverse complex (even $-\gflow$, odd $-\gflow+1$,
\cref{thm:selberg-two-term-ring-zeta}), while the full exterior algebra gives the Ruelle zeta,
whose even band is shifted and fails (FE); and the $K$-type grading proposed here as the doubled-bond
candidate is \emph{not} a flow grading (it makes $\gflow$ odd) and does not supercancel
(\cref{prop:ktype-not-flow-grading}). The $K$-type parity survives only as a grading of the
diffusion letters.
\end{observation}

\subsection{Where this leaves the graded Ramanujan property}

\begin{observation}[Status]
\label{obs:graded-ramanujan-status}
\claimstatus{obs:graded-ramanujan-status}{sketched-conditional}
Understood: the definition (on the retained net divisor, with a signed trivial divisor and two
reference rates) and its stability under non-aliasing continuum limits; graded quantum expanders
with a mechanism (index-two gradings, band form, net zeros after the non-backtracking lift; the odd
gap has no Alon--Boppana floor, \cref{obs:odd-gap-no-alon-boppana}), Hecke-eigenvalue zeros for
graded Weil--LPS, an elliptic curve over $\mathbb F_5$ from a qubit; curve-type channels Ramanujan by a second mechanism
(the odd block is $\sqrt\qs$ times a unitary, the $P$-mode is $H^0$ by parity-twisted unitality); Harrow's continuum limit as temperedness, its graded version as the archimedean
ladder. Not understood: a graded channel of \emph{circle type} with a group-theoretic mechanism (a
non-Hermitian graded channel whose odd block is $\sqrt\qs$-unitary because of a representation, as
in Artin--Schreier by Parseval); a Kraus (doubled-bond) realisation of the Artin--Schreier, Riemann
and Selberg graded spectral transfers, none of which is supplied (a doubled bond has $n_0-n_1 =
(D_+-D_-)^2\ge0$ while a cohomological grading may have more odd than even dimensions); a continuum example with an infinite discrete divisor on
the critical line other than Riemann and Selberg. \emph{Settled on 2026-09-16}
(\cref{sec:selberg-letters}): the principle relating ``Hermitian channel plus non-backtracking
lift'' to ``odd block unitary'' is \cref{thm:hashimoto-lift-metric} (letter-derived metric,
positive exactly on the strict band, Jordan obstruction at the endpoints); the Selberg graded
spectral transfer is the stable transverse complex, whose first-band form is derived from the
letters with the $\tfrac14$ coercivity as the one extra bound
(\cref{thm:letters-derived-selberg}); and the obstacle on the Riemann side is now the
non-compact bridge of \cref{obs:modular-scattering-sector}. The Riemann case needs the
second mechanism in the continuum, on regular data (\cref{obs:divisor-regular-data}).
\end{observation}
