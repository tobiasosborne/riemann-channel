\section{CCM in Tensor-Network Terms VI: Smeared Transfers}
\label{sec:ccm-tn-continuous}

The continuous analogue of $\sum_jc_jE^j$ is a coherent integral of
open transfer propagators. This gives a natural tensor network when
the propagator and its positive metric have been supplied. Constructing
those objects arithmetically is a separate question.

\begin{proposition}[Finite smeared transfer Gram]
\label{prop:ccm-tn-continuous-smeared-gram}
\claimstatus{prop:ccm-tn-continuous-smeared-gram}{proved}
For a finite-dimensional self-adjoint $H$, set $U(t)=e^{-itH}$ and
$F_f$ as in \cref{def:ccm-tn-continuous}. For integrable compactly
supported $f,g$,
\[
 \iint\overline{f(x)}g(y)\Tr U(y-x)\,dx\,dy
       =\Tr(F_f^*F_g).
\]
In a finite test basis the unshifted Gram nullspace consists exactly
of superpositions with $\sum_jc_jF_{f_j}=0$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[$\langle1\rangle$1.] The group law gives $U(x)^*U(y)=U(y-x)$.
Expand the two operator integrals; finite-dimensional Fubini gives
the identity.
\item[$\langle1\rangle$2.] The contraction is explicitly
$\sum_{a,b}\overline{(F_f)_{ab}}(F_g)_{ab}$, joining the two open
transfer indices. Its Gram norm vanishes exactly when the operator
superposition vanishes.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

For a cMPS, the propagators initially act on the doubled bond:
\[
 \mathcal L=K\otimes I+I\otimes\bar K+\sum_a R_a\otimes\bar R_a.
\]
The existing Dyson expansion of \cref{thm:cmps-ring-norms} supplies
its positive-length ring norms from local propagators and jumps.
A centred retained part $\mathcal L-aI=-iH$ supplies the proposition
only if it is skew-adjoint in the chosen positive metric. The
half-plane bound, line duality and semisimplicity remain separate
inputs (\cref{thm:weil-positivity-continuous,thm:weil-line-duality,prop:hp-inner-product-continuous}).
The smeared operators have another pair of open transfer indices;
their Gram is not the physical cMPS overlap at two different lengths.

\begin{proposition}[Infinite smearing and the raw-trace obstruction]
\label{prop:ccm-tn-infinite-smeared-gram}
\claimstatus{prop:ccm-tn-infinite-smeared-gram}{proved}
Suppose a self-adjoint $H$ has an orthonormal eigenbasis with real
eigenvalues $\gamma_r$, repeated with multiplicity, and its locally
finite counting measure $\TNmeasure=\sum_r\delta_{\gamma_r}$ has polynomial
growth. For $f,g\in C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$, the operators
$F_f=\widehat f(H),F_g=\widehat g(H)$ are Hilbert--Schmidt and
\[
 W(f,g)=\sum_r\overline{\widehat f(\gamma_r)}\widehat g(\gamma_r)
       =\Tr(F_f^*F_g).
\]
This defines a distributional correlation form. On an infinite
Hilbert space the unsmeared unitary $U(t)$ is never Hilbert--Schmidt
or trace class, so its proposed raw Gram has infinite diagonal.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[$\langle1\rangle$1.] Repeated integration by parts gives rapid
Fourier decay. Polynomial counting growth makes
$\sum_r|\widehat f(\gamma_r)|^2$ finite, and similarly for $g$.
Spectral calculus gives Hilbert--Schmidt operators.
\item[$\langle1\rangle$2.] Cauchy--Schwarz makes the cross sum
absolutely convergent. The product of the two Hilbert--Schmidt
operators is trace class and its diagonal trace is the displayed sum.
\item[$\langle1\rangle$3.] Define the tempered distribution
$W(h)=\sum_r\widehat h(\gamma_r)$.
The Fourier identity
$\widehat{f^**g}=\overline{\widehat f}\widehat g$ proves the
correlation interpretation, without an intermediate pointwise trace.
\item[$\langle1\rangle$4.] For an infinite-dimensional unitary,
$\|U(t)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=\Tr I=\infty$. Trace class would imply
Hilbert--Schmidt, so that too fails.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

A general positive spectral measure gives features in $L^2(\TNmeasure)$,
but need not give an operator trace on a specified physical bond.
When its total mass is infinite, the constant feature is not a
normalisable cyclic vector. The zero-extended Fourier-window functions
also are not smooth compactly supported tests: applying the proposition
to them needs a separate summability/domain argument. For counting
growth $O(R\log R)$, their $O((1+|\gamma|)^{-1})$ transforms do give
square summability, with the endpoint form still specified separately.

\begin{proposition}[Exact intertwining when an unshifted model is given]
\label{prop:ccm-tn-exact-continuous-intertwiner}
\claimstatus{prop:ccm-tn-exact-continuous-intertwiner}{proved}
In addition to the hypotheses of
\cref{thm:ccm-tn-loewner-quotient}, suppose a finite self-adjoint $H$
realises the \emph{unshifted} window form by the preceding finite
Gram construction and $\epsilon=0$. Then the feature map descends
isometrically from the CCM quotient onto its range in operator space,
and intertwines $(2\pi/L)D''$ with left multiplication by $H$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[$\langle1\rangle$1.] In $[0,L]$ coordinates let
$\TNnull_b=\sqrt L\TNnull$, so $\TNnull_b(0)=\TNnull_b(L)=1$.
For periodic $f$, integration by parts gives
\[
 F_{-if'}=HF_f-if(0)(e^{-iLH}-I).
\]
\item[$\langle1\rangle$2.] The zero unshifted Gram norm gives
$F_{\TNnull_b}=0$. Apply Step 1 to $\TNnull_b$ and subtract $f(0)$
times that identity. The boundary terms cancel and yield
\[
 F_{-if'-(-i\TNnull_b')f(0)}=HF_f.
\]
The input on the left is precisely the physically scaled $D'f$.
\item[$\langle1\rangle$3.] The Gram identity identifies the radical
with the feature kernel and makes the quotient map isometric onto
its range. Step 2 makes this range invariant under self-adjoint
left multiplication by $H$, proving the claim.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

This is the exact finite bridge between the two descriptions. It
recovers a cyclic space of functions of $H$, not all physical
multiplicity vectors. For $\epsilon>0$ the original feature map
satisfies $\|F_{\TNnull}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=\epsilon\|\TNnull\|^2$ and does
not even descend to the CCM quotient. The shifted matrix has its own
Gram factor $T^{1/2}$, which is not thereby an arithmetic transfer flow.

\subsection{What is still missing for the Riemann tensor network}

The explicit formula supplies the correlation distribution without
constructing a bond. Under RH its zero-side pairing becomes a positive
spectral Gram; this is a conditional realisation, not a construction
from the physical letters. In general it pairs each zero with its
functional-equation partner, as in
\cref{obs:weil-zeta-dictionary,cit:weil-criterion}.
For a graded transfer, the corresponding expression is the difference
of even and odd smeared Gram norms. One must know the sector to be
moved across before obtaining a positive odd Gram.

\begin{observation}[The remaining bond identification]
\label{obs:ccm-tn-riemann-bond-open}
\claimstatus{obs:ccm-tn-riemann-bond-open}{open}
The missing construction is an arithmetic retained transfer flow with
a specified positive metric and distributional trace, together with
an isometry/intertwiner from the CCM window quotients and control of
their increasing-window limit. Neither the finite quotient theorem
nor the clock-history Hamiltonian supplies such a Riemann cMPS bond,
its local letters, its stationary density, or its physical grading.
\end{observation}

The fixed-window real-zero theorem of
\cite{connes2025quadratic} concerns a semibounded closed form with a
simple isolated even minimum. Convergence of suitably normalised
entire approximants to the completed zeta function as the window also
grows is a further step. It would exclude off-line zeros by a complex
analytic limit argument; finite real spectra and accurate matches to
known zeros alone do not provide that step.

\begin{observation}[Rejected: a finite raw Gram of an infinite unitary flow]
\label{obs:ccm-tn-raw-infinite-gram}
\claimstatus{obs:ccm-tn-raw-infinite-gram}{refuted}
Rejected assertion: on an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space,
$\Tr(U(s)^*U(t))$ is a finite Hilbert--Schmidt Gram kernel for a
unitary flow. Its diagonal is $\Tr I=\infty$.
\Cref{prop:ccm-tn-infinite-smeared-gram} gives the surviving smeared
form under explicit summability hypotheses.
\end{observation}
