## Morning: Ihara zeta functions of simplicial complexes (TJO's sidequest)

TJO's question: the fermion--zeros connection for graphs seems increasingly
central, the zeros may carry "surface-like" qualities, so is there a natural
Ihara-type result for simplicial complexes generalising the finite-graph
result, and what is its natural quantum generalisation? Answered in four

**Literature** (`notes/extract/complex-zeta-sources.md`, 1539 lines, and
`notes/extract/cohomological-zeta-sources.md`, 1880 lines, 87 quotes
machine-verified; `refs/fetch_sources.sh` now lists 99 sources). The result
exists for buildings and only there. Kang--Li (0804.2305) for the $\mathrm{PGL}_3$
building: $Z = (1-u^3)^{\chi}/[\det(I - A_1u + qA_2u^2 - q^3u^3)\det(I+L_Bu)]
= 1/[\det(I-L_Eu)\det(I-L_E^tu^2)]$, and Ramanujan iff the cubic's zeros are on
$|u| = q^{-1}$, $L_E$'s on $\{q^{-1}, q^{-1/2}\}$, $L_B$'s on $\{1, q^{-1/2},
q^{-1/4}\}$. Fang--Li--Wang for $\mathrm{Sp}_4$ (quartic vertex polynomial,
band conditions). Kang--Yu (2607.21262, July 2026) for $\mathrm{PGL}_n$:
$(1-u^n)^{\chi}L(\Gamma, q^{(n-1)/2}u) = \prod_{k=1}^{n-1}Z_k^{\epsilon}(u)^{(-1)^{k+1}}$,
odd $k$ upstairs, even $k$ downstairs, with the Euler factor derived as a
graded determinant over the cochain complex (a torsion; they credit Hoffman
2003 for reading Bass's proof that way). Lubetzky--Lubotzky--Parzanchevski:
the higher Hashimoto operator is the geodesic flow on pointed cells, a
Ramanujan complex gives a Ramanujan digraph, poles at $u = k^{-s}$ with
$|s| = 1$ or $\operatorname{Re}s \le 1/2$, and interior poles occur, so in
higher rank "RH" is a one-sided band (Kamber: $L_p$-expander iff
$|\lambda| \le q^{(p-1)/p}$). Storm's hypergraph zeta is the Ihara zeta of the
incidence bipartite graph at $\sqrt u$ (no new object). No Ihara-type zeta
for a general complex exists, and Deitmar--Hoffman 2004, Lubotzky's ICM 2018
survey, Hong--Kwon 2024 and Kang--Yu 2026 all say so; the only
general-triangulation object is Bénard--Chaubet--Dang--Schick's combinatorial
Ruelle zeta (polynomial, vanishing order $b_1$, torsion-type, no RH).
Cohomological side: Deitmar 1995 (alternating product over $\wedge^l\mathfrak n$,
vanishing order the degree-WEIGHTED supertrace $-\sum p(-1)^p\dim H^p$, the plain
Euler sum vanishing), Dyatlov--Zworski (order $-\chi$ at $0$ on surfaces,
$\zeta_R = \zeta_1/\zeta_0\zeta_2$), the placement flipping with dimension;
Matsuura--Ohta 2025: Bass's identity as a Berezin integral with a Dirac
operator block off-diagonal between vertex and edge fermions, the exponent
$E - V$ an index (chirality, not Grassmann parity); Knill's graph torsion
$\mathrm{SDet}(D) = \prod\det(D_k)^{(-1)^k}$ ("fermionic" versus "bosonic"),
never joined to Ihara. Quantum side: nothing. Iyer--Jain--Jordan--Somma's
"Ramanujan quantum expander" is the Hastings bound with no zeta; the one
quantum walk on simplicial complexes has no zeta.

**What this gives TJO's question.** The natural object is the graded
geodesic determinant with specified geodesic data, and "natural" means
functorial under isomorphisms of that data; the building data (opposition,
algebraic lengths) is what makes the vertex-level identity and the RH exist.
The surface-like feature that survives is an odd chamber contribution to a
graded divisor; it is not surface cohomology, and on the building vertex side
it cancels completely. The quantum generalisation is the voltage-quotient
Artin block, and Ramanujan complexes give Ramanujan quantum expanders of type
$\widetilde A_{d-1}$ for every representation by Harrow's argument. None of
this touches the Riemann side; it sharpens the graph/complex half of the
fermionic-zeros analogy.

## BC stationary state and arithmetic-covariant generator cones (Codex)

TJO authorised the next investigation after clarifying that the BC state is
the prescribed stationary state of an unknown Lindbladian, with Galois
symmetry and adelic symplectic structure. Both the no-event generator and
the jumps must be allowed to vary. No subagents or independent reviewers
were used for this mathematical investigation; a later export-tool
installation was delegated to Luna at TJO's explicit request.

Completed the finite phase-residue constraint calculation in
`notes/bc-symmetry-generators.md` (arguments B0–B6), with definitions in
shard 02e and results in 04d. The exact cone is a linear section of the
conditional Choi positive cone. At the critical tracial extension I/p,
the real span dimensions are (p−1)(p+1)^2 for Galois covariance, 2p−2 for
full linear Weil covariance, p+1 for additional Weyl+Galois covariance,
and 1 for Weyl+Weil covariance. The last class is depolarization; the
Weyl+Galois class already has real spectrum. Weyl translation covariance
is an extra assumption, not a synonym for the requested symplectic structure.

State bookkeeping matters: the finite BC phase marginal fixes diagonal
probabilities only. It is uniform at criticality. Away from criticality
its diagonal extension fails full Weil invariance, but a coherent
extension a I+(r−a)P can be positive and Weil invariant; ruling out the
diagonal extension would not rule out the latter. No finite-dimensional
unital representation of the full BC algebra retains nontrivial phases,
so these finite models cannot be presented as exact BC representations.

An explicit p=7 GKLS generator, formed by a small scalar-orbit perturbation
of depolarization, preserves I/p and all linear Weil/Galois symmetries but
has two different odd decay rates. Its complete positivity has an analytic
conditional-Choi bound; the script independently checks the actual
finite-time channels. Shear-conjugacy-class examples happened to have
equal odd real parts at p=3,5,7, so those examples were not used as the
counterexample. A two-prime reset construction also shows that fixed local
semigroups and CRT marginal compatibility leave a free coupling.
