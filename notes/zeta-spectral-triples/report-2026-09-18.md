# Report: the Connes-Consani-Moscovici construction, implemented rigorously and pushed past the paper (2026-09-17/18)

## What was built

`zst/` is a C library on FLINT/arb implementing arXiv:2511.22755 end to end with certified enclosures:
the truncated Weil form on the window `[lambda^-1, lambda]` (Loewner data with bounded series tails),
its even and odd blocks, the minimal even eigenpair (inverse iteration, then a Krawczyk operator on the
bordered eigen-system), a certificate of the paper's even-simple hypothesis (verified positive
definiteness of the deflated matrix and of the odd block), the spectrum of the perturbed scaling
operator (sign-scan candidates, interval-Newton certification of each root, completeness by count),
and certified comparison with arb's zeta zeros. Four test programs, two fuzz harnesses, a mutation
tester, and a ground-truth citation by TeX line for every formula. Details: `plan.md`,
`zst/README.md`, worklogs 2026-09-17 and 2026-09-18.

## Findings

1. **The paper reproduces exactly and is now certified.** At its own parameters (`x = lambda^2 = 13`,
   `N = 120`) the 50-row table comes back to every printed digit, as rigorous upper bounds, in 3 s.
   A typo in the paper's displayed constant `c(L)` was found; it is a multiple of the identity and
   does not affect the spectrum.

2. **How far it goes.** Certified results (`benchmark.md`, `bench/`):

   | x | N | first zero certified to | zeros certified below 1e-50 | below 1e-3 | wall |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | 13 | 120 | 2.4e-55 | 2 | 47 | 3 s |
   | 20 | 200 | 1.2e-92 | 26 | 95 | 15 s |
   | 30 | 300 | 2.6e-146 | 77 | 172 | 4 min |
   | 40 | 400 | 3.0e-200 | 140 | 251 | 2 min |
   | 50 | 500 | 2.9e-249 | 211 | 341 | 8 min |

   x = 60 and x = 80 (N = 600, 800) also completed with all roots certified (x = 80 in 55 min, 2.4 GB);
   their output files were lost to a cleanup mistake and x = 60 is being regenerated as this is
   written. x = 100 (N = 1000, 4000 bits) ran out of the 10.7 GB memory limit on the first attempt
   and was not finished after the memory rework.

3. **Three empirical laws, uniform across the grid.**
   - The first zero's accuracy is exponential in x and independent of N: 5.4 digits per unit of x,
     i.e. `e^{-4 pi x}`, the decay of the prolate eigenvalue defect the paper's Section 7 invokes.
     The primes up to 50 determine the first zero to 249 digits.
   - The error at height gamma grows like `10^{0.37 gamma}` at every x, so the usable range grows
     linearly: zeros with certified error below 1e-3 reach `gamma ~ 14.6 x - 40`.
   - N saturates at about 7.5 x (x = 20: N = 150 and 200 identical; x = 30: N = 300 and 450
     identical); reach is set by x alone. `eps_N` follows `10^{-5.1 x + 8}`, N-independent.

4. **What scale forces on the numerics** (each found by the benchmark, fixed test-first, recorded in
   the 2026-09-18 worklog): interval `LDL^T` cannot certify inertia at condition numbers `1e100`
   (squared amplification) while verified positive definiteness by approximate Cholesky plus
   residual can; the QR candidate step cannot run below the dynamic range of the rank-one term, so a
   sign scan with a fine geometric tail grid replaced it (tail roots come in pairs just past N); and
   any plain interval evaluation of the secular function's derivative is useless for the far-out
   roots, where a mean-value form is required. Peak memory was cut from 5.1 GB to 2.4 GB at N = 800
   by an O(N^2) Krawczyk step.

## What this means for the programme

The construction is a machine from window explicit-formula data to a self-adjoint operator with
provably real spectrum; its accuracy is governed by one exponentially small number (the prolate
defect) and one linear degradation in height. Both are now measurable rigorously at scales far beyond
the paper's, which is the raw material for testing the paper's missing step (`xi_lambda` against the
prolate guess `k_lambda`) and for the extensions in `plan.md` Section 6 (Dirichlet and Hecke
L-functions, transfer operators of graphs and curves, the Selberg zeta with the cusp comb).
