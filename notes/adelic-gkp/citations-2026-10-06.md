# Citations for the 2026-10-06 lane pages: the theorems quoted "from memory"

Author: `claude:opus-5.5`, 2026-10-06, citation lane N of an orchestrated session.

Status: **a record, not a round.** Nothing is registered in `db/claims.tsv` and no row is added to `db/provenance.tsv`, because the ten pages are not
distilled into shards and the gate would not use the rows. No existing page is modified. This page lists every theorem that the ten
`notes/adelic-gkp/` pages of 2026-10-06 quote "from memory": `curve-bridge.md`, `no-lift.md`, `cm-lift.md`, `cone-bridge.md`, `overlap-data.md`,
`zn-flux.md`, `gate-phases.md`, `zeta-ingredients.md`, `ff-dirichlet.md` and `data-ladder.md`. For each theorem it gives the source and, where TeX
exists, a byte-checked quote.

**Method.**

- I grepped the ten pages for "from memory", case-insensitive, and read each hit in its section. A theorem is listed if the page marks it as
  quoted from memory, either inline or in the page header (`zn-flux.md` l.7 and `zeta-ingredients.md` l.6–7 name their from-memory sources in the header).
- Every `id:file:line` in the location and quote columns was produced by a script, not typed. Line numbers cited inside remarks, and in §2, were checked by reading the lines. The script finds the quote in `refs/src/<id>/<file>` and checks three things: it
  occurs exactly once, it is a byte-exact substring of the cited lines (the newlines included), and it spans at most three lines. The one exception
  is `2006.13771:weil-compo.tex`, whose quoted line occurs twice in that file (l.109 and l.2077); l.109 is the occurrence cited, and it is the line
  that `db/provenance.tsv` row `prov:cc20-weilcrit` already cites.
- In the quote column, ` ↵ ` marks a line break in the source. Everything else in a quote is byte-exact, including doubled and trailing spaces.
- *Primary* means the quote is from the TeX of the paper the page names. *Secondary* means the paper is not on arXiv, and the quote is a
  restatement of it in an arXiv paper on disk. A secondary quote confirms the wording only as far as that restatement goes.
- Bibliographic data marked (Crossref) were checked against `api.crossref.org` on 2026-10-06. Data marked (bib: `id:file:line`) are byte-quoted
  from that paper's bibliography. Fields marked [unsure] are from my memory and should be checked.

## 2. Discrepancies, with the correct statements

**2.1 `cm-lift.md` §1.1 (l.31), row 15.** The page says that every elliptic curve over $\mathbb Q$ with CM by $\mathcal O_K$, $K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})$, is a quadratic twist of 49a1
"or of its isogenous 49a2". This is over-inclusive.

- $H_{-7}=x+3375$ and $H_{-28}=x-16581375$ (PARI `polclass`). PARI's `ellinit` gives 49a1, 49a3: $j=-3375$; 49a2, 49a4: $j=16581375=255^3$.
- So 49a2 has CM by the order $\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-7}]$ of conductor 2, not by $\mathcal O_K$.
- **Correct:** an elliptic curve over $\mathbb Q$ has CM by $\mathcal O_K$ iff $j=-3375$, iff it is a quadratic twist of 49a1. The twists of 49a2 are exactly the curves with CM by
  $\mathbb Z[\sqrt{-7}]$.

The page's later claims (the twist by $-7$ is 49a3; bad reduction at 7) are unaffected.

**2.2 `zeta-ingredients.md` §1 item 5 (l.59–61), row 35.** The page says that the orthogonal complement of the overlap profiles in $L^2(C_{\mathbb Q})$
carries the squeeze action with spectrum the critical zeros. Connes's Theorem 1 says something different.

- **Correct (Connes 1998, Theorem 1, `math/9811068:main.tex:873-885`):** the space is the cokernel $\mathcal H=L^2_\delta(C_k)/\mathrm{Im}(E)$ (`math/9811068:main.tex:805`), in a weighted space with Sobolev
  exponent $\delta>1$, not $L^2$.
- On each sector $\mathcal H_\chi$ the generator $D_\chi$ has discrete spectrum. That spectrum is the set of imaginary parts of the zeros of $L(\tilde\chi,\tfrac12+\rho)$ with real part
  $\tfrac12$.
- The multiplicity is capped at the largest integer $n<\frac{1+\delta}2$ (`math/9811068:main.tex:882-883`).
- The representation $W$ on $\mathcal H$ is not unitary: $\lVert W(g)\rVert=O(\log\lvert g\rvert)^{\delta/2}$, at `math/9811068:main.tex:831`.
- Connes calls $\delta$ "unnatural" (`math/9811068:main.tex:224`). The weighted space "artificially eliminates the non-critical zeros" (`math/9811068:main.tex:2235-2236`). In the unweighted $L^2$ setting (§VIII, $\delta=0$)
  there is no such point-spectrum statement. There all zeros enter the trace formula, and the non-critical ones appear as resonances (`math/9811068:main.tex:2239-2246`).

So "it sees no off-line zero" is right only for the $\delta>1$ construction, and by design. The phrase "in $L^2(C_{\mathbb Q})$" should read
"$L^2_\delta(C_{\mathbb Q})$, $\delta>1$". The phrase "orthogonal complement" should read "cokernel", with a non-unitary action.

**2.3 `no-lift.md` §4 (l.169–172), row 12.** The page says that every abelian variety over a finite field becomes isogenous, "possibly after extending the field",
to one with a CM lift. It also says that when neither the isogeny nor the extension is needed is the subject of Chai–Conrad–Oort.

- **Correct (`2101.05531:main.tex:345-348`):** every $A_0/\mathbb F_q$ is $\mathbb F_q$-isogenous to an abelian variety that admits a CM lifting. The classical Honda–Tate argument may need a finite
  extension of $\mathbb F_q$; Chai–Conrad–Oort, Thm 4.1.1, removes it.
- The isogeny is genuinely needed in general.
- For a CM lifting to a *normal* base, up to isogeny, the residual reflex condition is sufficient (CCO Thm 2.1.6, `2101.05531:main.tex:354`). It is also necessary for simple varieties (`:434`).
- Not every abelian variety over $\overline{\mathbb F}_p$ has a CM lifting itself (Oort; `2101.05531:main.tex:341-343`).

The page's conclusion, that the ordinary case is special because the lift is canonical, is unaffected.

**2.4 Not a discrepancy, but unverified: row 43.** The claim that the Frobenius of $y^3=t^3+t+1$ over $\mathbb F_7$ "is a normalised cubic Gauss sum over $\mathbb F_{343}$"
could not be checked against a source here. For diagonal curves the classical statement (Weil 1949) is in terms of Jacobi sums of the field of definition. The page
should either check it numerically or relabel it *heuristic*.
