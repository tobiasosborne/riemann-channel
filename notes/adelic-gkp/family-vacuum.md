# Families of steps that share a vacuum, and families that do not

Checks: `checks/check_family_vacuum.py`, output `checks/output_family_vacuum.txt` (**39 of 39 pass**, about 4 s).

The question comes from `data-ladder.md` §4b. That section says the datum that carries RH for a commuting family of steps on one lattice is the
common invariant vacuum. Lane C's CM family on $\mathcal O_K$ shares $J_K$, and $\zeta$'s prime dilations share the vacuum point $B$ of
$\Omega_{\rm FE}$ (`zeta-ingredients.md` §3.1). Is a shared vacuum automatic for commuting steps, and what does it need? This page tests it
on three families: two one-mode steps, the vertex-level Hecke pair of the $(13,17;5)$ LPS complex of shard 04u, and $\zeta$'s spectral model.

Conventions are those of `check_family_weil.py`. A step of norm $q$ satisfies $M^T\Omega M=q\Omega$. Its adjoint is $V=qM^{-1}$, and $S=M/\sqrt q$ is
symplectic. A **vacuum** for a set of steps is a real $J$ with $J^2=-1$ that commutes with every step and has $G_J=\Omega J$ symmetric positive
definite. The Weil form is $\tfrac12\Omega(M-V)$.

## 1. Two one-mode steps, and the commuting case

Take $M_q=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\q&\lambda\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ on $\mathbb Z^2$ with $\lambda^2<4q$, and $\Omega=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$.

**(1a)** (*proved here*; checked M1, symbolic) The adjoint is $V_q=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}\lambda&1\\-q&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, so
$J_q=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}-\lambda&-2\\2q&\lambda\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)/\sqrt{4q-\lambda^2}$. Equality $J_q=J_{q'}$ forces equal $(1,2)$
entries, hence $\sqrt{4q-\lambda^2}=\sqrt{4q'-\lambda'^2}$, and then $\lambda=\lambda'$ and $q=q'$. The equation $J_q=-J_{q'}$ has no solution. So
two different steps of this form never share a vacuum in these coordinates.

**(1b)** (*proved here*; checked M1)
$$M_qM_{q'}-M_{q'}M_q=\begin{pmatrix}q-q'&\lambda-\lambda'\\ q'\lambda-q\lambda'&q'-q\end{pmatrix},\qquad M_qV_{q'}=\begin{pmatrix}q'&0\\ q\lambda'-q'\lambda&q\end{pmatrix}.$$
The commutator vanishes iff $(q,\lambda)=(q',\lambda')$. Its diagonal is $q-q'$ whatever $\lambda,\lambda'$ are, so two such steps of different
norm never commute, even after normalisation. The second identity is stronger. $M_qV_{q'}$ is triangular with real eigenvalues $q'$ and $q$. So
$S_qS_{q'}^{-1}$ has eigenvalues $\sqrt{q/q'}$ and $\sqrt{q'/q}$, and the group generated by $S_q$ and $S_{q'}$ is unbounded. A common vacuum
would make that group a subgroup of a compact $U(1)$, so **no compatible $\Omega$ whatever admits a common vacuum** of two such steps with
$q\ne q'$. (For 12 of the 15 test pairs the plain product $S_qS_{q'}$ is already hyperbolic; M1.)

**(1c)** (*standard*, one line) A real $2\times2$ matrix with non-real eigenvalues determines its complex structure up to sign:
$J=\pm(M-\tfrac\lambda2)/\sqrt{q-\lambda^2/4}$. So two such steps share a vacuum iff they commute.

The CM steps do commute. Lane C's steps $\psi(\mathfrak p)$ are multiplications in the one field $K$, so they lie in the one algebra
$\mathbb R[J_K]\cong\mathbb C$. M1 re-checks this for $\psi(\mathfrak p_2),\psi(\bar{\mathfrak p}_2),\psi(\mathfrak p_{11}),\psi(\bar{\mathfrak p}_{11})$ on
$\mathcal O_{\mathbb Q(\sqrt{-7})}$: they commute, share $J_K$, and have Weil forms of both signs for the one $\Omega$. The steps $M_q$ are not
multiplications in one complex structure. Each step is the companion matrix of its own Hecke polynomial $x^2-\lambda x+q$, written in
coordinates (function, shifted function) that depend on $q$.

**Proposition 1** (*proved here*; checked M1 on a random three-step example). Let $M_1,\dots,M_k$ be pairwise commuting real $2n\times2n$ matrices
with $M_i^T\Omega M_i=q_i\Omega$ for one symplectic $\Omega$. The following are equivalent:

- (i) there is a common vacuum $J$;
- (ii) every $M_i$ is semisimple with all eigenvalues of modulus $\sqrt{q_i}$ (RH for each step);
- (iii) the group generated by the $S_i=M_i/\sqrt{q_i}$ is relatively compact.

When (i) holds, $J$ is a complex structure in which every $M_i$ is complex-linear with eigenvalues of modulus $\sqrt{q_i}$.

*Proof.* (i)⇒(ii): each $S_i$ preserves $\Omega$ and $G_J$, so it lies in the unitary group $\mathrm{Sp}\cap O(G_J)\cong U(n)$. (ii)⇒(iii):
commuting semisimple matrices are simultaneously diagonalisable over $\mathbb C$. In that basis the $S_i$ are unimodular diagonal matrices, so
the group they generate lies in a conjugate of a compact torus. (iii)⇒(i): average any positive form over the compact closure to get an
invariant positive $G$. Let $A=G^{-1}\Omega$, so that $\Omega(x,y)=G(x,Ay)$. $A$ is $G$-antisymmetric and commutes with every $S$ that preserves
both $G$ and $\Omega$, because $S^{-1}AS=G^{-1}S^T\Omega S=A$. Put $J=-A(-A^2)^{-1/2}$. This is a function of $A$, so it commutes with every
$S_i$. It satisfies $J^2=-1$, and $\Omega J=G(-A^2)^{1/2}>0$. ∎

Remarks.

- Commutativity is used only in (ii)⇒(iii). Without it, (i)⇔(iii) still holds, but (ii) is no longer sufficient: (1b) gives two elliptic
  steps that generate an unbounded group. Semisimplicity is needed: $\sqrt2\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}R&1\\0&R\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ has
  eigenvalues of modulus $\sqrt2$ and unbounded normalised powers (M1).
- **The proposed statement is not right as phrased.** "Commuting steps share a vacuum iff they are simultaneously of CM type $\Phi_+$ for one
  orientation" is false. Lane C's family shares $J_K$, yet conjugate primes give Weil forms of opposite sign for every orientation (M1, and
  `check_family_weil.py` F3). The correct condition is (ii): RH for each step, and nothing else. Given (i), the Weil form of $M_i$ on the $j$-th
  complex mode is $\sqrt{q_i}\sin\theta_{ij}$ times $G_J$ (`data-ladder.md` §4b). So "all Weil forms positive for one $\Omega$" is the further
  condition $\sin\theta_{ij}>0$ for all $i$ and $j$. This condition is about orientation, not about the vacuum. The second half of the proposed
  statement ("one complex structure in which all are complex-linear with $|\text{eigenvalue}|=\sqrt q$") is correct, and it is (i) restated.
- (*proved here*, one line) **One step's vacuum is the family's when it is unique.** Let $J$ be the only vacuum of $M_1$, and let $M_2$ commute
  with $M_1$. Then $S_2JS_2^{-1}$ is again a vacuum of $M_1$, because $S_2$ is symplectic. So it equals $J$, and $J$ commutes with $M_2$.
  (*sketched*, from Krein theory quoted from memory) A step with no real eigenvalues has a unique vacuum iff each of its eigenspaces is
  Krein-definite, for example when its eigenvalues are simple.

## 2. The Hecke family on the LPS complex

**Which family.** I used the vertex-level Hecke pair of the $(13,17;5)$ square complex of shard 04u. The construction is copied from
`scripts/lps_square_complex.py`: quaternions $S_{13}$, $S_{17}$, $\phi$ into $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb F_5)$, 120 vertices. $A_{13}$ is the adjacency of the
13-graph $Y$, the 14-regular Cayley graph of $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb F_5)$ on $\phi(S_{13})$. That is the LPS graph $X^{13,5}$, so its own
non-backtracking step has $q=13$. The task brief's "$T_5$, $q=5$" confuses the level 5 with the prime. $A_{17}$ is the 18-regular Cayley graph on
$\phi(S_{17})$, the vertex form of 04u's transverse transport ($T_1D=DA_{17}$).

The two operators commute exactly (M2). Square-completion $ab=\pm b'a'$ gives $\phi(a)\phi(b)=\phi(b')\phi(a')$. I did not work on
$H^1=K$, which has dimension 721. There 04u has only the one operator $T$, and the 13-graph's own step on $K$ would be the Hashimoto operator, not
of the form below.

**The joint spectrum** (*checked*, M2). Besides the trivial point $(14,18)$ and the bipartite sign $(-14,-18)$, there are ten joint eigenspaces
with integer eigenvalues $(\lambda_{13},\lambda_{17})$. Their multiplicities are $(\pm4,\pm5)$: 12, $(\pm4,\pm3)$: 4, $(\pm4,0)$: 18,
$(\mp2,\pm2)$: 15 and $(\mp2,\pm6)$: 10, with $\pm$ correlated. All are strictly Ramanujan. Call their 118-dimensional span $W$.

The steps are $M_q=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-I\\qI&A_q\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ on $\mathbb Z^{240}$. Both are similitudes of the one
$\Omega=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&I\\-I&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$. They do not commute: $[M_{13},M_{17}]=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}-4I&A_{13}-A_{17}\\17A_{13}-13A_{17}&4I\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, the block form of (1b).

**(a) The two vacua differ** (*proved here* by (1a) per joint eigenspace; *checked* M2).
$J_q=(M_q-V_q)(4q-A_q^2)^{-1/2}$ is a vacuum of $M_q$ on $W\otimes\mathbb R^2$ for $q=13$ and for $q=17$. However
$\max|J_{13}-J_{17}|=0.61$, and neither vacuum commutes with the other step.

Both Weil forms $\tfrac12\Omega(M_q-V_q)=\tfrac12\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}2q&A_q\\A_q&2\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ are **positive definite on $W$** for the
shared $\Omega$ (*proved here*: positive iff $A_q^2<4q$; checked). This is the mirror image of the CM family, which shares a vacuum but has Weil
forms of both signs. So positivity of every step's Weil form for one $\Omega$ is **neither necessary (CM) nor sufficient (graph)** for a shared
vacuum. This qualifies `data-ladder.md` §4b, which says that for a family no orientation makes every Weil form positive. That is true for families
of complex multiplications (CM, $\zeta$), not for families in general.

**(b) There is no common vacuum, for this $\Omega$ or any other** (*proved here*; *checked* M2).

- The commutant of $\{M_{13},M_{17}\}$ on $W\otimes\mathbb R^2$ has dimension $\sum_jm_j^2=1618$. It consists of the maps $I_2\otimes X_j$ on
  each joint eigenspace $\mathbb R^2\otimes E_j$. This was computed blockwise in the joint eigenbasis.
- The reason is that on each plane the two $2\times2$ steps have no common real eigenvector and do not commute. So they generate $M_2(\mathbb R)$,
  whose commutant is $\mathbb R$. For comparison, the commutant of $M_{13}$ alone has dimension $7124=2\sum m^2$: complex-linear maps on each
  eigenspace of $A_{13}$.
- Positivity fails for every element of the commutant: $\Omega(e\otimes v,(I\otimes X)(e\otimes v))=\omega(e,e)\,v^TXv=0$. On the two joint
  eigenspaces of odd multiplicity (15) the commutant does not even contain a complex structure.
- For any $\Omega$: the identity of (1b) holds blockwise, $M_{13}V_{17}=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}17I&0\\13A_{17}-17A_{13}&13I\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$. This
  matrix is integral with real eigenvalues 13 and 17, so $S_{13}S_{17}^{-1}$ is hyperbolic on every mode. In addition, $S_{13}S_{17}$ is
  hyperbolic on 6 of the 10 joint eigenspaces: those with $\lambda_{13}\lambda_{17}\le0$, where $\mathrm{tr}\le-(r+r^{-1})<-2$ with
  $r=\sqrt{13/17}$. The integral product $M_{13}M_{17}$ has 176 of its 240 eigenvalues off $|\mu|=\sqrt{221}$. So the normalised family generates
  an unbounded group on every joint eigenspace, and by Proposition 1 (i)⇔(iii) no compatible form admits a common vacuum.

**(c) Coordinates chosen per joint eigenspace do not help** (*proved here*; *checked* M2). A change of coordinates $P_j$ on the $j$-th plane, the
same for both steps, preserves the commutator. The commutator has diagonal $q-q'=-4\ne0$, and $\min_j\|[S_{13},S_{17}]\|=0.44$. So no choice
makes both steps rotations of one plane. The brief's guess that commutation might hold "unless $\lambda_q/\sqrt q$ ..." does not occur: the
$2\times2$ steps never commute when $q\ne q'$.

What does exist is a different family: the rotations $\sqrt q\,R(\pm\theta_q^{\,j})$ with $\cos\theta_q^{\,j}=\lambda_q^{\,j}/2\sqrt q$. These have
the same spectra, commute, and share $R(\pi/2)$ for all four sign choices per plane (checked). They are not simultaneously conjugate to
$(M_{13},M_{17})$, the choice of sign per $(q,j)$ is free, and whether they preserve any lattice was not examined (*open*).

**Conclusion for the graph** (*proved here*; *checked*). The Hecke family on the LPS complex shares a vacuum in none of the senses (a), (b), (c).
This holds although its Hecke operators commute, each step satisfies RH, and each step's Weil form is positive for the shared $\Omega$. This is a
*negative finding* for "commuting Hecke operators give a shared vacuum": commutation of the $T_q$ is not commutation of the steps.

## 3. $\zeta$

**Lane G's spectral model in real form** (*checked*, M3, first 50 zero pairs, $p=2,3,5$). On the plane of the pair
$\{\tfrac12\pm i\gamma\}$ the prime step is $M_p=\sqrt p\,R(\gamma\log p)$. The orientation of $\Omega_{\rm FE}$ is fixed so that $R(\pi/2)$ is the
positive vacuum; 04t's sign convention only flips $J\to-J$. The checks give:

- $M_2,M_3,M_5$ commute;
- $M_p^T\Omega_{\rm FE}M_p=p\,\Omega_{\rm FE}$, and every eigenvalue has modulus $\sqrt p$;
- $J=R(\pi/2)$ on every pair commutes with all three steps, with $\Omega_{\rm FE}J=I$;
- the Weil form of $M_p$ has eigenvalues $\sqrt p\sin(\gamma\log p)$, with 52, 48 and 46 negative out of 100 for $p=2,3,5$ (lane G's indefiniteness);
- $M_pM_{p'}$ is $\sqrt{pp'}$ times a rotation by $\gamma\log pp'$, and $M_pV_{p'}=\sqrt{pp'}\,R(\gamma\log(p/p'))$, so the normalised group is bounded.

**The contrast lies in the realisation, not in the spectral data** (*checked*, M3). Build from the same zeros the Bass-doubled steps
$B_p=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\p&2\sqrt p\cos(\gamma\log p)\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ per pair. They have the same spectra as $M_p$ but behave
exactly like the graph family: they do not commute, and $B_pB_{p'}$ is hyperbolic on 26, 32 and 31 of 50 zeros for $(2,3)$, $(2,5)$ and $(3,5)$
($B_pB_{p'}^{-1}$ on all 50, by (1b)). So the property "$\zeta$'s family shares a vacuum" belongs to the realisation of the step as
multiplication by $p^{\rho}$ in one complex coordinate. The Hecke eigenvalue data alone do not give it.

**Reading** (*heuristic*). The CM family and $\zeta$'s family are of one kind: every step is a complex multiplication in one structure, so by
Proposition 1 the shared vacuum costs nothing beyond RH for each step. The Hecke family on a graph is of the other kind. Each $T_q$ comes with
its own tree and its own non-backtracking step, and those steps generate an unbounded group however well their eigenvalues behave. So the finite
shadow of $\zeta$'s family is the CM and function-field family (lanes C and J; over $\mathbb F_q(t)$ the family is the powers of one Frobenius),
not the Hecke family of shard 04u. For $\zeta$ the shared vacuum is then not extra data beyond one step's vacuum (§1, uniqueness remark), but its
positivity is still RH for every step at once.
