Part I. The adelic GKP code

1. Phase space, Weyl operators, the lattice

On L2(A) (Schwartz–Bruhat core S(A)) put W(u,v)f(x)=ψ(vx)f(x+u) for u,v∈A. Then

W(u,v)W(u′,v′)=ψ(uv′−vu′)W(u′,v′)W(u,v),

so phase space is A2 with symplectic pairing ψ(ω), ω((u,v),(u′,v′))=uv′−vu′. Proved here (two lines).

Facts about Q⊂A (standard; Tate's thesis; not byte-cited):

  • Q is discrete: Q∩((−12,12)×Zˆ)={0}.
  • Q is cocompact: A=Q+([0,1)×Zˆ), so A/Q≅(R×Zˆ)/Z (the solenoid), of volume one.
  • Q is self-dual: Q⊥={x:ψ(xq)=1∀q∈Q}=Q.

So Q is a lattice in the locally compact sense (not a finite-rank Z-module), and L=Q×Q is a lattice in A2 whose symplectic complement is Q⊥×Q⊥=L: a Lagrangian lattice.

Proposition 1 (the code). ⟨Θ,f(⋅+q)⟩=⟨Θ,f⟩ and ⟨Θ,ψ(q′⋅)f⟩=⟨Θ,f⟩ for q,q′∈Q, and ⟨Θ,fˆ⟩=⟨Θ,f⟩. Up to scale, Θ is the only tempered distribution with the two invariances.

Proof. The invariances: ∑rf(r+q)=∑rf(r), and ψ(q′r)=1. Fourier: Poisson summation on A/Q with Q⊥=Q and volume one (standard). Uniqueness (sketched): ψ(q′⋅)-invariance for all q′ forces support in Q⊥=Q, a discrete set, so the distribution is ∑qcqδq (no derivatives at the finite places, and the real derivatives are excluded by the modulation invariance in the real variable); translation invariance makes cq constant. ∎

In GKP terms: Θ is the joint +1 eigen-"state" of the stabiliser group {W(λ):λ∈L} (up to the usual phase convention for W on L, trivial here because ψ(qq′)=1). The code dimension of a GKP code is [L⊥:L]; here it is 1. Θ is the adelic GKP qunaught. The real analogue is ∑n∈Zδn with ψ∞, i.e. the square lattice of spacing 2π when ℏ=1 and the phase is eiuv.

2. Cliffords: rationals, SL2(Q), ideles

Proposition 2 (proved here). DaW(u,v)Da−1=W(a−1u,av) for a∈A×. So Da is a Clifford (it normalises the Weyl group), the symplectic squeeze diag(a−1,a). For a∈Q×, Da maps L to L and DaΘ=Θ.

Proof. DaW(u,v)Da−1f(x)=ψ(avx)f(x+u/a). For the second claim, ⟨Θ,Daf⟩=|a|1/2∑qf(aq)=|a|A1/2⟨Θ,f⟩, and |a|A=1 for a∈Q× (product formula). ∎

More generally (standard; Weil 1964, not byte-cited): the metaplectic cover Mp2(A)→SL2(A) splits over SL2(Q), and Θ is fixed by the Weil representation of SL2(Q). The shear by t∈Q multiplies by ψ(tx2/2), which is 1 on Q; the Weyl element is the Fourier transform. So the stabiliser-preserving Clifford group of the code is H(Q)⋊SL2(Q). This invariance is the automorphy of theta.

Correction to the chat phrasing "logical operations". With a one-dimensional code there is no logical space; every stabiliser-preserving Clifford acts as a scalar, here trivially. Multiplication by rationals is a symmetry of the code, not a gate.

Ideles. For x∈A×∖Q×, DxΘ is the GKP qunaught of the squeezed lattice x−1Q×xQ, with net squeeze |x|A. Since Q× acts trivially, the squeeze orbit of the code is parametrised by CQ=A×/Q×. All of the arithmetic lives on this orbit (Part II).

3. What it corrects

Detection (standard GKP reasoning). For a displacement error W(e), e∈A2, each stabiliser W(λ) acquires the eigenvalue ψ(ω(λ,e)). The syndrome is therefore the character λ↦ψ(ω(λ,e)) of L, i.e. the class of e in A2/L=(A/Q)2, which is the Pontryagin dual of L. Applying W(−e′) for any e′ in the class leaves an element of L, which fixes Θ. So every displacement is detected and undone; with nothing encoded, "correction" means a perfect displacement sensor modulo the lattice, as for the real qunaught.

The syndrome space is adelic: by strong approximation each class has a representative with real part in [0,1) and finite part in Zˆ. A p-adic error p−ku (u∈Zp×) is equivalent modulo L to a displacement by p−ku−r for a rational r∈p−kZ with r≡p−ku mod Zp, i.e. to a real displacement by −r plus an error in Zˆ. The code converts p-adic errors into real ones with denominators.

Proposition 3 (rigidity; proved here). Let M⊂A2 be a subgroup commensurable with L=Q2 (i.e. M∩L has finite index in both). Then M=L.

Proof. L is divisible, and a divisible abelian group has no proper subgroup of finite index (the quotient would be finite and divisible, hence trivial). So M∩L=L, i.e. L⊂M with M/L finite. A is a Q-vector space, so nx∈Q2 implies x∈Q2: A2/L is torsion-free, and the finite group M/L is trivial. ∎

So a GKP code with d>1 cannot be built by passing to a sublattice or superlattice of Q2, unlike Z2⊂R2. The squeezed lattices DxL are Lagrangian too. Every global code in this family is a qunaught. Nontrivial codes appear only after truncation, §4, or locally, §5.

4. Level N: the code is a Bell pair across places

Fix N≥1. At the finite places take the GKP code with lattice NZˆ×NZˆ⊂Af2. Its symplectic complement is N−1Zˆ×N−1Zˆ (because Zˆ⊥=Zˆ), so its code space

CN={φ:suppφ⊂N−1Zˆ,φ(⋅+NZˆ)=φ},ea=1a+NZˆ,a∈N−1Zˆ/NZˆ≅Z/N2,

has dimension N2, and it is spanned by normalisable vectors. At the real place take the ideal GKP code with lattice NZ×NZ (pairing ψ∞): code dimension N2, basis the combs ca=∑n∈Zδa+Nn, a∈N−1Z/NZ.

Theorem 4 (proved here; checked C1, C2).

  1. For g∈S(R) and φ=∑aφaea∈CN: ⟨Θ,g⊗φ⟩=∑aφa⟨ca,g⟩. That is,

    Θ|S(R)⊗CN=∑a∈Z/N2ca⊗ea∗,

    the maximally entangled state between the real GKP qudit and the finite-adelic qudit, in the code bases.

  2. It is stabilised by Xb⊗Xb and Zb⊗Zb−1, b∈N−1Z (these are the Weyl operators W(b,0), W(0,b) of L, split across places).

  3. S(A)=⋃NS(R)⊗CN, increasing along divisibility. So Θ is the inductive limit of the Bell pairs.

Proof.

  1. Q∩NZˆ=NZ, so Q∩(a+NZˆ)=a+NZ for a∈N−1Z. Hence ⟨Θ,g⊗ea⟩=∑ng(a+Nn).
  2. Translation by b permutes the labels on both sides identically. Modulation by ψ(bx)=ψ∞(bx∞)ψf(bxf) multiplies ea by e2πiab (constant on a+NZˆ since bNZˆ⊂Zˆ) and the real side by e−2πibx, which equals e−2πiab on the support of ca.
  3. An element of S(Af) is locally constant with compact support, hence in some CN. ∎

C1 checks the stronger self-duality at level N (⟨Θ,f⟩=⟨Θ,fˆ⟩ with the finite Fourier transform eˆa=N−1ψf(a⋅)1N−1Zˆ) for N=1,2,3,6,12 to 10−25.

Reading. At every finite resolution the adelic qunaught is a Bell pair between the real oscillator and the finite places. Local errors at one place are detected against the other half, through the joint stabilisers. The real factor alone is an ordinary GKP code whose logical spacing is 1/N: resolving the finite places to level N shrinks the real displacements it can correct on its own to |ϵ|<1/2N. In the limit nothing is encoded and everything is sensed. At N=1 the finite factor is one-dimensional (1Zˆ) and the real factor is the qunaught ∑nδn: projecting the finite places onto their vacua turns the adelic code into the ordinary real GKP qunaught.

Heuristic remark: the squeezes Dp at a finite place do not preserve the level (DpCN⊂CpN up to relabelling). Whether the local terms of the explicit formula can be read as the leakage between levels N and pN, in the sense of the compression lemma (analytic §5, Lemma 6), is open.

5. Gaussians

Local vacua. The real Gaussian e−πx2 is characterised (among other ways) as the vector fixed by the maximal compact SO(2) (metaplectic U(1)) of the Weil representation: the oscillator ground state, Fourier self-dual. At p the maximal compact is SL2(Zp) and its fixed vector is 1Zp, also Fourier self-dual (standard; Weil). So

f0=e−πx∞2⊗⨂p1Zp

is the adelic vacuum, Tate's standard vector.

Proposition 5 (p-adic GKP codes; proved here). For integers a,b with a+b≥0, the lattice La,b=paZp×pbZp⊂Qp2 is isotropic, with complement p−bZp×p−aZp; its code space is the functions supported on p−bZp and paZp-periodic, of dimension pa+b, spanned by normalisable vectors, with logical Pauli group that of Z/pa+b. For a=b=0 the code is one-dimensional and its state is 1Zp.

Proof. ψp is trivial exactly on Zp, so (pkZp)⊥=p−kZp, and the support/periodicity description follows as in Proposition 1. The dimension is [p−bZp:paZp]=pa+b. ∎

So at the finite places "Gaussian", "vacuum" and "GKP qunaught" are the same state, and it is normalisable. The obstruction that makes ideal GKP states unphysical over R (no compact open subgroup, so no normalisable lattice state) is absent over Qp. Over R it is present: no nonzero f has f and fˆ both compactly supported (Connes, main.tex:2746-2749, quoted in analytic §8). That single fact is the archimedean blocker of analytic §8 in GKP language.

Gaussian states and theta functions (standard; checked C3–C5). The pure one-mode Gaussian states (up to displacement) are γτ(x)=eπiτx2, τ in the upper half-plane H=SL2(R)/SO(2) (the Siegel half-space for one mode). Overlaps of the code with the adelic Gaussian states are Jacobi's theta function:

⟨Θ,γτ⊗1Zˆ⟩=∑q∈Q∩Zˆeπiτq2=∑n∈Zeπiτn2=ϑ(τ).

Riemann's ϑ(t)=∑ne−πn2t is τ=it: the overlap of the ideal adelic GKP state with the vacuum squeezed at the real place. Its modular laws are Cliffords moving the Gaussian while Θ stays fixed:

  • S: ϑ(−1/τ)=τ/iϑ(τ), from Θˆ=Θ, 1ˆZˆ=1Zˆ and γˆτ∝γ−1/τ.
  • T fails, T2 holds: the shear by 1 multiplies the real factor by ψ∞(x2/2) (shifting τ by one unit) and the 2-adic vacuum by ψ2(x2/2)=(−1)x on Z (C4), while the odd-p vacua are invariant (x2/2∈Zp). So ϑ(τ+1)=∑(−1)neπiτn2≠ϑ(τ) and ϑ(τ+2)=ϑ(τ) (C3). The theta group Γθ=⟨S,T2⟩, rather than SL2(Z), is forced by the 2-adic vacuum (that Γθ is exactly the stabiliser, up to phase, of 1Z2 inside SL2(Z) is sketched, not proved here).

Finite-energy GKP. The realistic GKP state at the real place is e−βnˆ applied to the ideal comb, an element of Howe's oscillator semigroup (the contraction semigroup of complexified symplectic maps); its wavefunction is a Gaussian-enveloped comb of Gaussians, a Jacobi theta function in x (standard). The adelic finite-energy code is: level N at the finite places (exact, §4) and the oscillator semigroup at ∞. At p there is no continuous energy, since SL2(Zp) has no one-parameter subgroup; the p-adic analogue of energy is the level (the conductor), and the p-adic regulator is exact.

Connes's cutoff PΛPˆΛ (position and momentum both bounded by Λ) is the dilation-covariant finite-energy regulator. It is exact at the finite places (Λ-boxes are compact open lattices) and approximate at ∞, where the prolate (LPS) truncation has a plunge layer of ≍logΛ states (analytic §8).

7. The zeta function is an overlap

Tate (standard; check C5 for f0): for f∈S(A) and Res>1,

Z(f,s)=∫A×f(x)|x|sd×x=∫CQ(∑q∈Q×f(qx))|x|sd×x.

Read the two sides:

  • Left: a product. For f=f0 the integral factorises over places: ∫R×e−πx2|x|sd×x=π−s/2Γ(s/2) and ∫Qp×1Zp|x|sd×x=(1−p−s)−1. Each local factor is the Mellin profile of the local vacuum. The Euler product is the product structure of the vacuum. This is side A of the notebook (the gas; the primes do not talk), and it is a product state.
  • Right: the code. The inner sum is ⟨Θ,f(x⋅)⟩−f(0), the overlap of the squeezed test state with the code, zero mode removed. This is where the global lattice, which entangles the places (§4), enters.

The identity of the two is "unfolding": the integral over all of A× is the integral over the squeeze orbit of the code. With Ef(a)=|a|1/2∑q≠0f(qa), Z(f,s)=∫Ef(a)|a|s−1/2d×a (analytic §1).

8. The functional equation is the S-gate

(Naming, 2026-10-05: S here is the modular-group S, the Fourier transform. In GKP gate notation that is the Hadamard; the GKP phase gate S is the shear, modular T. See functional-equation-gate.md.)

The Fourier transform fixes Θ and f0 and conjugates Da to Da−1. Poisson summation then gives Z(f,s)=Z(fˆ,1−s) up to the two pole terms, which come from the two zero modes of the comb (q=0 and the constant; Connes's C⊕C(1), analytic §6). In decay-rate language: a mode ρ=β+iγ pairs with 1−ρ¯, rates (β,1−β) (what-rh-has-become.md §5). The symmetry is a Clifford symmetry of code and vacuum; RH is not a symmetry statement and does not follow from it.

9. The zeros are what the overlaps cannot contain

In the trivial-character sector Z(f,s)=ξ(s)Ff(s) with Ff entire for f∈S(A)0 (analytic §1). So the Mellin transform of every overlap profile Ef vanishes at every zero ρ: the zeros are squeeze frequencies absent from all overlap profiles. Connes's spectral realisation is the cokernel of E: in L2(CQ), the orthogonal complement of the overlap profiles carries the squeeze action with spectrum the critical zeros (Connes 1998, refs/src/math/9811068/main.tex; not re-cited here by line). An off-line zero would be a non-unitary mode |a|ρ−1/2, not in L2; in any cutoff it is an edge mode (analytic §4, Proposition 4). So the plain L2 picture sees only critical zeros; it is blind to exactly the thing in question (analytic L3).

10. Weil positivity: is the squeeze correlation functional a state?

Let A=Cc∞(R+×) (trivial-character sector) with convolution (composition of squeezes) and the involution h˜(x)=x−1h(1/x)‾, which is the adjoint for the squeeze representation normalised so that Res=12 is the unitary axis. Define the Hermitian functional

ω(h)=∑ρhˆ(ρ),ω(h∗h˜)=Z(h)=∑ρhˆ(ρ)hˆ(1−ρ¯)‾.
  • Weil's criterion (cited, cit:weil-criterion): RH ⟺ω(h∗h˜)≥0 for all h, i.e. ω is a (non-normalised) state on the squeeze algebra.
  • GNS (standard reformulation). If ω is positive, the GNS construction gives a Hilbert space Hω on which the squeezes act unitarily. Under RH, ω(h)=∑γhˆ(12+iγ) is a sum of unitary characters, so Hω≅ℓ2(zeros) and the squeeze generator has spectrum {γ}: the Hilbert–Pólya operator is the squeeze generator in the GNS space of ω, and "the metric" is the GNS inner product. The notebook's Gram-of-open-transfer-strips form (prop:ccm-tn-operator-gram) is this Gram matrix in window coordinates. Note that GNS here is tautological: it produces the space only once positivity is known. The content of RH is to produce Hω a priori.
  • What failure looks like. An off-line pair {ρ,1−ρ¯} contributes 2Rezw¯ with z=hˆ(ρ), w=hˆ(1−ρ¯) independent: a hyperbolic plane, signature (1,1) (C6). Then ω is a Krein-space "state" with one negative direction per off-line pair.
  • The explicit formula splits ω into computable pieces: Z=P−Loc (analytic, conventions).
    • P(h)=gˆ(0)+gˆ(1), g=h∗h˜: the two zero modes of the comb, rank two, signature (1,1).
    • Loc=∑vWv: at each place a single-mode squeeze trace. The distributional trace of Dλ on L2(Qv) is ∫|λ|1/2δ((λ−1)x)dx=|λ|v1/2/|1−λ|v: Lefschetz at the fixed point x=0. These are Connes's local terms up to normalisation (sketched; his Theorem 4, main.tex:1893-1906, semi-local trace formula). At p, λ=p±k gives the logp⋅p−k/2 terms of the prime side; at ∞ the integral gives the Gamma term.
    • So Weil positivity says: zero-mode term minus the sum over places of local squeeze traces is a positive form. Proposition 7 of analytic §6 sharpens it: one pole functional can be killed, and then RH ⟺−Loc≥0 on its kernel.
  • Connes's route in this language. Truncate to finite energy with QΛ=PΛPˆΛ. The trace of a squeeze on the truncated code-complement is (Connes's conjectured global trace formula) 2h(1)logΛ+∑vWv(h)+o(1): a phase-space volume term (the number of states in the box, measured in squeeze coordinates) plus the local fixed-point traces. The renormalised trace is of positive type at every Λ (analytic L5), and its limit is the harmonic-measure form, positive unconditionally; RH ⟺ that limit equals Weil's ω (analytic L4). The missing input is the asymptotic trace formula itself.

11. Calibration: the curve case, where it works

Let K be a function field of genus g over Fq, ΘK=∑x∈Kδx on AK, again a self-dual GKP qunaught. Now every place is non-archimedean, so every place has a stabiliser vacuum.

  • Overlaps are counts. For a divisor D=∑dvv, the product of local qunaughts 1D=⨂v1πv−dvOv has ⟨ΘK,1D⟩=#{x∈K:v(x)≥−dv∀v}=#L(D)=qh0(D) (standard). The overlap of two stabiliser states counts the common lattice points, as in finite-dimensional stabiliser theory.
  • Riemann–Roch is the self-duality of the code. ΘˆK=ΘK and 1ˆD∝1Kc−D (the dual lattice; Kc canonical) give qh0(D)=qdegD+1−gqh0(Kc−D) (standard: Tate/Weil).
  • The cutoff is exact, the cokernel of the overlap map is a 2g-dimensional logical space, the degree squeeze acts on it as the Frobenius companion matrix (an MPS of bond dimension 2g), and its similitude metric is the Rosati form ∑iφ(αi)φ(q/αi) (analytic §§13–14). Weil positivity is positivity of that form, and it holds because Θ (the theta divisor, the support of h0>0 in Picg−1, i.e. where overlaps of the code with product stabiliser states are nontrivial) is ample. For curves the GNS space of §10 exists a priori: it is H1, finite-dimensional, with a positive metric supplied by geometry, and the Hilbert–Pólya operator is F/q, unitary.

12. What is missing for Q, in this language

  1. No stabiliser vacuum at ∞. Overlaps of Θ with product states are Gaussian sums (ϑ), not counts (qh0). There is no exact Riemann–Roch, the finite-energy regulator has a plunge layer of ≍logΛ states at the window edge, exactly where off-line zeros would show as edge modes (analytic L8). Diagnosis, heuristic: the curve proof's positivity is a positivity of counts; at the real place there is nothing to count.
  2. No polarisation. Even granting a cutoff, nothing in the code supplies a structural reason for ω to be a state; the bond's own norms are blind (analytic L3, L9). The target, restated: realise ω(h∗h˜) as a squared norm (a fidelity, a probability, a Tr(φhφh†)) in a space where the squeezes act unitarily and the two zero modes have been removed, built before the zeros are known. This is the "Stinespring form with prime dilations as Kraus operators" question of the README, and analytic §14's "ample Φ".
  3. The level structure is suggestive, not yet useful. At each finite level the code is a Bell pair between the real oscillator and the finite places (§4). A positivity argument that runs level by level would need the archimedean half of the pair to be controlled exactly, which point 1 forbids. Open: whether the explicit formula's prime terms are the leakage between levels (§4 remark), and whether anything about the Bell structure survives the squeeze orbit beyond the Euler/theta unfolding of §7.

The language does not supply the positivity. It relocates the problem cleanly: RH ⟺ the squeeze correlation functional of the adelic GKP qunaught, with its two zero modes removed, is a state. For curves it is one because overlaps are counts and the theta divisor is ample.

14. A field extension: K=Q(2)

Added 2026-10-05 (TJO: "What about if I take a field extension of Q, eg sqrt2 and ask the same questions again"). Data: two real places, OK=Z[2], hK=1, dK=8, fundamental unit ε=1+2 with Nε=−1, ζK=ζ⋅L(⋅,χ8) with χ8(p)=(2/p). Character ψK=ψ∘TrK/Q.

14.1 The code is the same; the field is a torus (standard; phrasing mine).

  • K⊂AK is discrete, cocompact and self-dual for ψK (Tate), so ΘK=∑x∈Kδx is a qunaught; Proposition 3's rigidity argument applies verbatim (K divisible, AK a K-vector space). Same answer to "what does it correct": nothing is encoded, every displacement is sensed modulo K2.
  • In the basis {1,2}, x=a+b2, one has AK≅A2 and ΘK=Θ⊗Θ. Phase space is canonically V⊕V∗ with V=K as a Q-space; the trace form only identifies V∗≅V. As a two-mode adelic GKP state, ΘK is the same for every quadratic field.
  • The stabiliser-preserving Clifford group of the two-mode code is H(Q2)⋊Sp4(Q), which contains SL2(K) (Hilbert modular symmetries, via restriction of scalars and the trace form) as the part commuting with K.
  • Multiplication by α∈K× is a matrix in GL2(Q) on (a,b): 2↦(0210), ε↦(1211) (Q3). The idelic squeezes of K form the maximal torus TK(A)⊂GL2(A), and ζK is the Mellin transform of the overlap with Θ⊗Θ along the TK-orbit. Quadratic fields correspond to conjugacy classes of non-split maximal tori; the split torus Gm×Gm gives ζ(s)2.

14.2 Units: lattice-preserving squeezes between the real places (standard; checked Q3). In the real embeddings x↦(σ1x,σ2x), ε acts as diag(1+2,1−2): a two-mode squeeze, up to a sign, with net |Nε|=1. It is integral at every finite place, so it fixes both the code and the finite vacuum 1OˆK. On OK≅Z2 it is the hyperbolic toral automorphism (1211), a quantum cat map. Over Q the only such symmetries are ±1. By Dirichlet, the unit squeezes compactify the trace-zero archimedean squeeze direction to a circle of length RK=log(1+2) (up to a factor two from signs). Consequences: the regulator enters the residue, ress=1ζK=2r1hKRK/(wK|dK|)=L(1,χ8)=log(1+2)/2 (Q1); and the Fourier modes on that circle are Hecke's Grössencharaktere, a Z-indexed family of L-functions on the same orbit. The dilation RH is about, |x|AK, is the norm direction, which units do not touch.

14.3 Local vacua; the ramified prime is squeezed (standard; checked Q2, Q5, Q6).

  • Split p≡±1(8): Kp=Qp2, vacuum 1Zp⊗1Zp, local factor (1−p−s)−2.
  • Inert p≡±3(8): Kp=Qp2, vacuum 1Zp2, local factor (1−p−2s)−1.
  • Ramified p=2: K2=Q2(2), local factor (1−2−s)−1. Here ψK,2 has conductor 𝔡−1, 𝔡=(22)=(2)3, N𝔡=8. The vacuum 1O2 is the qunaught of the asymmetric lattice O2×𝔡2−1, and Fourier maps it to ∝1𝔡−1. A Fourier-symmetric 1𝔞 would need 𝔞2=𝔡−1, impossible with exponent 3 odd (Q6). (Asymmetric qunaughts 1M, lattice M×M∨, exist for every lattice M; what fails is symmetry under the S-gate.)

The Euler product by splitting type reproduces ζ(2)L(2,χ8) (Q2, relative error 4×10−7 at primes <2×105). The discriminant is the leftover squeeze of the vacuum, and it is exactly the factor |dK|s/2 in the completed functional equation: Fourier maps f0K to a squeezed copy of itself, the squeeze being by a finite idele generating 𝔡. Minkowski's theorem that every proper extension of Q ramifies reads: every extension carries a squeezed local vacuum somewhere. This is the number-field shadow of "no unramified (constant-field) tower"; analytic §14 argues the tower is not the blocker anyway.

14.4 The archimedean side (standard; checked Q4, Q5). Projecting the finite places onto their vacua gives, since K∩OˆK=OK, the real two-mode qunaught on the Minkowski lattice σ(OK)×σ(𝔡−1)⊂R2×R2, with 𝔡−1=122OK the trace dual (Q5). Overlaps with Gaussian states are Hecke–Hilbert theta functions on H×H, e.g. ∑x∈OKe−π(t1σ1(x)2+t2σ2(x)2). In (a,b) the trace form is diag(2,4), σ12+σ22=2a2+4b2 (Q4), and 1OˆK=1Zˆ(a)1Zˆ(b), so the K-vacuum is a product of two squeezed Q-vacua and all the entangling sits in the torus. This is a coincidence of Z[2]: for Z[1+52] the trace form (2113) is not diagonal over Z. Either way there are two places with Gaussian sums instead of counts: the archimedean blocker of §12 doubles.

14.5 For RH (standard). ζK=ζ⋅L(χ8), so RH for ζK is RH for ζ together with GRH for L(χ8): the extension contains the original problem. In squeeze-state terms the push-forward of the TK-orbit to the Q squeeze group along the norm splits into the trivial sector (ζ) and the χ8 sector, χ8 being the character of CQ/N(CK)≅Gal(K/Q) (class field theory). The Galois automorphism b↦−b is a parity Clifford that swaps the real places. Weil positivity for K has the structure of §10: two zero modes, minus local squeeze traces, now with two archimedean terms and the |dK| shift.

Reading (heuristic). The one structurally new resource is an infinite discrete group of lattice-preserving archimedean squeezes (units). That is the closest a number field comes to symmetries a polarisation would provide; but units act only in the trace-zero directions, and RH is about the norm direction. Open: whether the Grössencharakter family on the regulator circle (all of whose members satisfy GRH conjecturally) gives a positivity statement uniform in the angular frequency that is easier than the trivial-character one.
