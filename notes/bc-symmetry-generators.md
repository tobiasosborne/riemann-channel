# Bost–Connes stationary states and arithmetic-covariant generators

2026-09-13. The inverse problem specified by TJO is to determine a Lindbladian
with the BC state stationary, Galois symmetry, and compatible adelic symplectic
structure. The no-event generator and the jump map are both unknown. A fixed
scattering generator with a chosen reset map is only one possible ansatz.

This note completes a first, finite-level constraint calculation. It does not
construct the adelic bond, identify it with the scattering model, or prove RH.
Written arguments below are **unreviewed**: their claims carry `sketched` in
the lab book even where a complete derivation is supplied. Numerical claims
are separately labelled `numerical`. There was no independent reviewer in
this session. No novelty claim is made for the general GKLS machinery.

`outputs/`, report shards 02e and 04d, and the source loci in

## B0. Which state and which algebra?

At criticality the target equation is most naturally
\[
 \varphi_{\rm BC}\circ T_t=\varphi_{\rm BC},\qquad
 T_t\alpha_g=\alpha_gT_t
\]
on an as-yet unspecified bond algebra, with a CP unital semigroup in the
Heisenberg picture. A density matrix is appropriate only after a finite
model or a suitable normal representation has been specified. The normal
Gibbs density on the standard BC representation exists for beta > 1;
its critical limit is not a density there (shard 04c).

Fix an **odd prime** p. The finite phase algebra generated by e(k/p) is
C(F_p), represented diagonally on H_p = l^2(F_p). This is an actual finite
subalgebra of the BC phase algebra. The full matrix algebra M_p is an
additional model, not a finite copy of the full BC algebra. Restricting a
state to the phase algebra fixes its diagonal probabilities, not its
off-diagonal matrix entries. Finite level also does not mean finite
dimension for a congruence-subgroup scattering model.

**Proposition B0.1 (finite residue probabilities).** The unique BC KMS state
for 0 < beta <= 1, and the Galois average of the extremal states for beta > 1,
restrict at prime level to
\[
 w_\beta(0)=p^{-\beta},\qquad
 w_\beta(x)=\frac{1-p^{-\beta}}{p-1}\quad(x\ne0).
\]
In particular the critical restriction is uniform. Its *diagonal extension*
to M_p is I/p. At an arbitrary integer level N the critical phase marginal
is likewise uniform on Z/N and these marginals are compatible under reduction.

Proof. For a nonzero k mod p, the cited BC formula gives
phi_beta(e(k/p)) = (p^(1-beta)-1)/(p-1). Fourier inversion, using the sum of
the nontrivial p-th roots of unity = -1, gives the displayed probabilities.
For beta > 1 in the standard arithmetic boundary representation, the
probability of residue r is
\[
 \frac1{\zeta(\beta)}\sum_{n\ge1:\ n\equiv r\pmod p}n^{-\beta}.
\]
Its zero-residue mass is p^(-beta). Averaging the boundary action of F_p^*
spreads the remaining mass uniformly over the nonzero residues. A single
extremal state's restriction generally is not Galois invariant. At beta = 1
the BC formula vanishes on every nontrivial finite-order phase, at every
level N, which proves uniformity and compatibility. Source: Connes–Marcolli,
math/0404128, `houcheschapter1final7.tex:1820–1837`.

**Proposition B0.2 (finite representations lose the phase algebra).** No
finite-dimensional unital *-representation of the full BC algebra retains
the nontrivial finite phase observables.

Proof. In finite dimension the BC relation mu_n^* mu_n = I makes mu_n
unitary. The other relation, at r=0, is
\[
 I=\mu_n\mu_n^*=\frac1n\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}e(k/n).
\]
The right side is the spectral projection onto the eigenvalue 1 of the
unitary e(1/n), so e(1/n)=I. This holds for every n. Thus the critical phase
state, which has phi(e(1/n))=0 for n>1, cannot be obtained in such a
representation. This excludes an exact finite representation, not finite
restrictions, CP compressions, or asymptotic approximations. Source relations:
math/0404128, `houcheschapter1final7.tex:1764–1775`.

## B1. Three distinct symmetry requirements

Use the centred Weyl operators already defined in shard 02c:
\[
 W_{x,y}|r\rangle=e_p\big(y(r+x/2)\big)|r+x\rangle.
\]
They obey W_v^* = W_(-v), Tr W_v = p delta_(v,0), and
W_v W_w = e_p(omega(v,w)/2) W_(v+w), with
omega((x,y),(x',y')) = y x' - x y'. The sign of omega is fixed here.

* **Galois covariance** means commuting with Ad U_a, where
  U_a|x> = |ax>, a in F_p^*. On phase functions this induces multiplication
  by a^(-1) in the argument; relabelling a by its inverse gives the usual
  BC convention. The induced action on Weyl labels is
  (x,y) -> (ax,a^(-1)y), a diagonal symplectic transformation.
* **Linear symplectic covariance** means commuting with the conjugation
  action of the Weil implementation of SL_2(F_p). On Weyl labels it is
  exactly W_v -> W_(gv). Fourier transform and a quadratic chirp generate
  this action; scalar phases of their implementation disappear under Ad.
* **Weyl-translation covariance** additionally means commuting with Ad W_z
  for all z. This is stronger than carrying a Weyl algebra or a Weil
  representation. We study it as an optional constraint, not as something
  TJO's specification automatically entails.

Parity P|x> = |-x> is the implementation of the central symplectic element
-I and also U_(-1). Thus the local models already preserve the even and odd
operator sectors under X -> PXP. Their dimensions are (p^2+1)/2 and
(p^2-1)/2. Identifying this local grading with the Phantasm's grading is
still additional work.

**Proposition B1.1 (state invariance and extensions).** A unique stationary
density of a covariant semigroup is invariant under its symmetry group.
The diagonal residue extension diag(w_beta) is invariant under the Galois
torus; it is invariant under the full Weil action iff beta=1. Nevertheless
the same residue probabilities have a unique *Weil-invariant matrix
extension*, when that extension is positive:
\[
 \sigma_\beta=aI+(r-a)P,\quad
 r=p^{-\beta},\quad a=\frac{1-r}{p-1}.
\]
It is positive iff r <= 2/(p+1), or
beta >= log((p+1)/2)/log p. Its parity-block eigenvalues are r and 2a-r.
At criticality it reduces to I/p. Full Weyl invariance forces I/p.

Proof. Covariance transports stationary states, proving the uniqueness
assertion. The Fourier transform sends any diagonal density to a matrix
whose diagonal is uniform, so Fourier invariance of that diagonal density
forces w_beta uniform. For the extension assertion, SL_2 acts transitively
on nonzero Weyl labels, so its invariant operator space is spanned by I and
sum_(v != 0) W_v = pP-I. The latter identity follows by summing the Weyl
matrix entries over y first. Matching diagonals fixes a and r-a. The
parity multiplicities are (p+1)/2 and (p-1)/2, giving the positivity criterion
and trace one. Commuting with every Weyl operator forces a scalar matrix,
since the Weyl operators span M_p. These statements prevent a false no-go:
failure of the **diagonal** extension to be symplectic invariant does not
exclude every extension. None of the coherent extensions has been identified
with the full BC bond state. For beta > 1, a symmetry-broken extremal state
can belong to a stationary family; uniqueness is the premise of the obstruction.

## B2. The exact finite-dimensional inverse problem

**Theorem B2.1 (stationary covariant GKLS cone).** For a specified density
sigma and unitary action U on C^d, all compatible Schrödinger generators
form the cone
\[
 \mathfrak C_U(\sigma)=\{L:\ L(X^*)=L(X)^*,\quad
 \operatorname{Tr}L(X)=0,\quad L(\sigma)=0,\quad
 [L,\operatorname{Ad}U_g]=0,\quad Q C_L Q\ge0\}.
\]
Here C_L = sum_(ij) |i><j| tensor L(|i><j|), Q = I-|Omega><Omega|,
and |Omega> = d^(-1/2) sum_i |i,i>. These are linear equations and one
positive-semidefinite constraint. They are necessary and sufficient for a
finite-dimensional CPTP semigroup with the stated covariance and state.

Proof. Necessity of conditional Choi positivity follows by differentiating
the positive Choi matrix of exp(tL) on the orthogonal complement of |Omega>.
For sufficiency set C_P = Q C_L Q, giving a CP map P with traceless Kraus
operators. The Hermitian difference C_L-C_P has zero Q-Q block, hence is
the Choi matrix of KX+XK^* for some K. Trace annihilation then forces
K+K^*+P^*(I)=0. Thus
L(X)=-i[H,X]+P(X)-{P^*(I),X}/2, which is GKLS and generates a CPTP
semigroup in finite dimension. The standard bounded GKLS form is also
recorded in Siemon–Holevo–Werner, 1707.02266, `TORUN.tex:158–164`.
The remaining linear identities pass to the exponential.

Equivalently, in any orthonormal traceless operator basis F_i,
\[
 L(X)=-i[H,X]+\sum_{ij}A_{ij}
 \left(F_iXF_j^*-\tfrac12\{F_j^*F_i,X\}\right),\qquad A\ge0.
\]
Here H is Hermitian. Taking Tr H=0 makes this parametrisation unique: Q C_L Q fixes A, and
the remaining commutator fixes H modulo scalars. Covariance therefore
requires H in the commutant of U and A in the commutant of the induced
representation on traceless operators. If that representation is
direct-sum_lambda V_lambda tensor C^(m_lambda), then
A = direct-sum_lambda I_(dim V_lambda) tensor A_lambda, A_lambda >= 0.
Stationarity is a further linear equation coupling these blocks and H.

Every invariant faithful sigma has an interior feasible point:
L_sigma(X)=sigma Tr X-X. Its conditional Choi matrix is
Q(I tensor sigma)Q, strictly positive on the range of Q. This elementary
existence observation does not give scattering modes. It shows that state
and symmetry alone cannot select the wanted dynamics.

## B3. How much freedom survives at the critical residue state?

**Theorem B3.1 (dimensions).** At sigma=I/p, for odd prime p, the real
dimensions of the linear spans of the finite GKLS cones are

| imposed commuting actions | dimension, including the overall time scale |
|---|---:|
| Galois torus U_a | (p-1)(p+1)^2 |
| full linear Weil/SL_2 action | 2p-2 |
| Weyl translations and the Galois torus | p+1 |
| Weyl translations and the full Weil action | 1 |

These are dimensions of cones' spans, not claims that every coefficient in
an orbit basis can be chosen nonnegative independently. Only the last two
have the simple nonnegative orbit-rate parametrisation below.

Proof for the first two rows. Trace annihilation and L(I)=0 remove the
identity row and column in the Weyl basis, leaving ordered pairs of
nonzero v,w in F_p^2. Covariance makes coefficients constant on simultaneous
group orbits. Hermiticity requires l_(-v,-w)=conj(l_(v,w)); because -I belongs
to both groups it makes every orbit coefficient real. Thus the real
dimension equals the number of ordered-pair orbits. The torus acts freely
on every nonzero vector and therefore on every such pair, giving
(p^2-1)^2/(p-1). Under SL_2, dependent pairs have the p-1 labels w=a v,
a != 0. Independent pairs have the p-1 labels det(v,w) != 0. Each label
is one orbit. This gives 2p-2. The depolarizing generator has conditional
Choi matrix Q/p, so sufficiently small perturbations in **every** permitted
Hermitian direction remain GKLS; positivity does not shrink these dimensions.

**Proposition B3.2 (additional Weyl covariance).** All Weyl-covariant GKLS
generators on M_p have the form
\[
 L(X)=\sum_{v\ne0}c_v(W_vXW_v^*-X),\qquad c_v\ge0.
\]
Additional Galois covariance makes c_v constant on the p+1 torus orbits:
the two punctured coordinate axes and xy=b for b != 0. In particular
c_v=c_(-v), so their Weyl eigenvalues are real and nonpositive. Additional
full symplectic covariance makes all rates equal, giving exactly
\[
 L(X)=\gamma\big(\operatorname{Tr}(X)I/p-X\big),\qquad\gamma\ge0.
\]

Proof. In the canonical traceless Weyl basis, Ad W_z has pairwise distinct
characters. B2.1 makes A diagonal and H scalar (hence zero in the traceless
convention), producing the rate formula. The orbit descriptions prove the
counts and the equality c_v=c_(-v). The eigenvalue at W_w is
sum_v c_v(e_p(omega(v,w))-1); opposite terms pair into real cosines. SL_2
is transitive on v != 0. Finally sum_v Ad W_v(X)=p Tr(X)I, so a common rate
c gives gamma=p^2 c. This proves the last two rows of the table.

Full affine symmetry in this model removes all oscillatory zero-like modes.
It is a conditional obstruction to imposing those extra *commuting*
symmetries, not an obstruction to an adelic symplectic structure as such.

## B4. Full symplectic symmetry does not force the odd decay rate

**Proposition B4.1 (explicit counterexample at p=7).** Let D(X)=Tr(X)I/p,
let a be a primitive root mod p, and define the complex-linear map R_a by
R_a(I)=0 and R_a(W_v)=W_(av) for v != 0. Put
\[
 L=D-\mathrm{id}+\epsilon R_a,\qquad \epsilon=1/(4p^2).
\]
This is a CPTP generator with unique stationary state I/p, commuting with
the entire Weil action and with parity. At p=7 its odd-sector eigenvalues are
\[
 -1-\epsilon,\quad -1+\epsilon/2+i\sqrt3\epsilon/2,\quad
 -1+\epsilon/2-i\sqrt3\epsilon/2,
\]
each with multiplicity 8. Thus all the prescribed finite state, linear
symplectic and Galois symmetries coexist with **unequal odd decay rates**.

Proof. Scalar multiplication commutes with SL_2 and with v -> -v, so R_a
is equivariant and Hermiticity preserving. It annihilates both trace and I.
It need not itself be positive or symplectic. Its superoperator Frobenius
norm is sqrt(p^2-1); reshuffling into a Choi matrix preserves that norm.
Hence the operator norm of Q C_(R_a) Q is < p. The conditional Choi matrix
of D-id is Q/p, so the displayed epsilon leaves it strictly positive on
range Q. Apply B2.1. On traceless operators R_a is a permutation, so all
eigenvalues of L have real part <= -1+epsilon < 0, proving uniqueness.
For p=7, multiplication by a has eight length-six cycles on the 48 nonzero
labels, and a^3=-1. The odd sector selects the three sixth roots whose
cube is -1, giving the spectrum above. No zeta zeros were used.

The script also constructs class-averaged Weil-unitary jump generators and
adds H=hP, an invariant Hamiltonian. These supply further freely variable
frequencies. In the tested shear classes at p=3,5,7 the odd real parts happen
to coincide; those class examples alone do **not** refute an equal-rate
statement. The scalar-orbit perturbation at p=7 is the counterexample.

## B5. Compatibility between two prime levels still leaves coupling freedom

**Proposition B5.1 (couplings with fixed marginals).** Let D_p and D_q be the
completely depolarizing channels and fix positive local rates gamma_p,gamma_q.
For 0 <= c <= min(gamma_p,gamma_q), set
\[
 L_c=(\gamma_p-c)(D_p-\mathrm{id})\otimes\mathrm{id}
 +(\gamma_q-c)\mathrm{id}\otimes(D_q-\mathrm{id})
 +c(D_p\otimes D_q-\mathrm{id}).
\]
These CPTP generators all have the unique stationary state I/(pq), commute
with the full local Weyl and Weil actions, and have the **same** restrictions
on the p-only and q-only observable algebras. Their decay rates on those
sectors are gamma_p and gamma_q, while on the joint traceless sector the rate
is gamma_p+gamma_q-c. Thus c changes the coupling without changing either
one-prime dynamics. Under CRT, these are also compatible prime-residue models.

Proof. Each channel-minus-identity term generates a CP Poisson semigroup;
the displayed coefficients are nonnegative. Depolarization commutes with
every unitary conjugation. Decompose each operator space into the identity
line and its traceless complement. On the four tensor sectors the
eigenvalues are 0, -gamma_p, -gamma_q, -gamma_p-gamma_q+c, with the first
simple. This also proves the restrictions and all claims of compatibility.
For c>0 the joint eigenvalue differs from the sum of the one-prime values.
The statement concerns two prime levels only; an infinite consistent,
conservative adelic system has not been constructed.

## B6. Evidence, implications, and the next mathematical target

The deterministic script checks p=3,5,7. It compares residue probabilities
with Hurwitz-zeta sums, tests state extensions directly, counts symmetry
orbits by BFS and independently by Burnside, and checks conditional Choi
positivity and Choi positivity of exp(tL) at two nonzero times. It checks
trace preservation, uniqueness, covariance under Hilbert-space Fourier,
chirp and dilation operators, the Egorov identity, odd-sector invariance,
the exact p=7 counterexample spectrum, and two-prime restrictions.
Numerical checks do not replace B0–B5's arguments or independent review.

The concrete deliverable is the cone B2.1 with the orbit reduction B3.1.
The stationary phase data and the symmetries leave a substantial cone;
imposing affine/Weyl covariance instead removes the desired oscillations.
Consequently the remaining arithmetic input must specify how the full BC
algebra (including prime isometries) acts on the bond, which transformations
commute with the dynamics, and how arithmetic intertwiners select the
Kossakowski blocks and couplings. The two-prime example shows that merely
asking for marginal compatibility does not select those couplings.

The next target is therefore an explicit representation or CP comparison
map linking the finite phase/Weil data to a scattering bond, respecting the
actual character action and the arithmetic boundary state. Only with that
map can one impose the scattering odd-sector condition on the cone rather
than fit an operator to known zeros. The Gamma_0(N) scattering/character
calculation remains open; this note does not claim to have done it. Prime
powers, the real place, metaplectic compatibility, and the critical
operator-algebra limit likewise remain open. The standard BC time flow
has not been identified with the unknown cMPS transfer flow.

Finally, the matrix test -(B sigma+sigma B^*) >= 0 from the conversation
is necessary and sufficient only for a **fixed** finite no-event B with
the scalar reset ansatz and positive exit flux. B2.1 allows B, Hamiltonian,
and all jump terms to vary. Failure of that reset test is not a no-go for
this larger inverse problem.
