Weil Positivity, Contracted

The Weil form of a zeta function is an overlap of two open pieces of a tensor network. This page poses Weil positivity in that language, one example at a time: a permutation, a shift, a regular graph, a quantum channel, a curve over a finite field, and finally the Riemann zeta function, where the network is known and the metric that would contract it is the unknown.

Dotted terms open a definition. Every panel is computed live; eigenvalues appear only in panels marked comparison, everything else is built from ring norms. Colour is used consistently: rings, cycles, primes, counts eigenvalues, zeros, anything negative the trivial set: pole, leading eigenvalue, the ±1 bond legs, strips, Gram matrices the metric the conjugate layer; the archimedean place.

0. The counting side is a sequence of ring norms

A is a fixed local tensor \(A\) with one physical index \(s\) and two bond indices, i.e. a family of matrices \(A_s\) on a bond space \(V\) of dimension \(n\). Closing \(L\) copies into a ring gives the state \(\psi_L = \sum_w \operatorname{Tr}(A_{w_L}\cdots A_{w_1})\,|w\rangle\), and its norm is a trace of a power of the :

\[ \|\psi_L\|^2 \;=\; \sum_{w}\big|\operatorname{Tr}(A_{w_L}\cdots A_{w_1})\big|^2 \;=\; \operatorname{Tr} E^L, \qquad E = \sum_s A_s\otimes\overline{A_s}\ \text{ on } V\otimes\overline V . \]

The left side is a sum over closed words, one nonnegative term per ring configuration: this is side A of the parent page, the counting side. The right side is a power sum of the eigenvalues of \(E\): side B. The identity \(\exp\sum_L \operatorname{Tr}E^L\,u^L/L = 1/\det(1-uE)\) is the one identity of the parent page, and the \(t_L = \operatorname{Tr}E^L\) are its counting data. Everything on this page is a statement about the sequence \(t_L\) and the operator \(E\) behind it.

1. The Weil form is an overlap of two open strips

On the parent page the Weil form was the Toeplitz form \(\sum_{j,k}c_j\overline{c_k}\,t_{k-j}\) built from counts, and its positivity for every window was the Riemann hypothesis in each example. Here is what the same form is in the network. Cut the ring open: an of length \(j\) is the operator \(E^j\) on the doubled bond. Two strips of lengths \(j\) and \(k\) can be glued end to end, one of them reversed and conjugated, and the glued diagram is the Hilbert–Schmidt overlap \(\operatorname{Tr}\big((E^j)^*E^k\big)\). If \(E\) is unitary, \((E^j)^* = E^{-j}\) and the glued diagram is the ring of length \(k-j\):

\[ E^*E = 1 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \operatorname{Tr}\big((E^j)^*E^k\big) = \operatorname{Tr}E^{k-j} = t_{k-j}, \qquad\text{so}\qquad T_{jk} = t_{k-j} = \big\langle E^j, E^k\big\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}} . \]

A matrix of inner products is a , and a Gram matrix is positive semidefinite. That is the whole of Weil positivity in the unitary case, and it is why the permutation of the parent page had a positive form for free.

Movie 2 · gluing two strips. \(E^j\) against \(E^k\); the reversed conjugate layer; the glued ring; the pairwise annihilation \(E^*E = 1\) that leaves a ring of length \(k-j\); the Toeplitz matrix as a grid of glued diagrams.

Where the metric sits

In every interesting example \(E\) is not unitary in the metric the bond came with. The gluing then needs a \(G>0\) on the bond, and the statement becomes an identity about a twisted adjoint \(X^\sharp = G^{-1}X^*G\):

prop:ccm-tn-operator-gram · proved If \(E^*GE = G>0\), then for every window \(T_{jk} = \operatorname{Tr}\big((E^j)^\sharp E^k\big)\) and \(c^*Tc = \|p_c(E)\|^2_{\mathrm{HS},G}\) with \(p_c(z) = \sum_j c_jz^j\). The Toeplitz form is the Gram matrix of the strips in the \(G\)-twisted Hilbert–Schmidt product, hence positive semidefinite. Conversely, Gram equality for all windows forces \(E\) to be unitary in some metric.

Writing \(\tilde E = G^{1/2}EG^{-1/2}\), the condition \(E^*GE = G\) is \(\tilde E^*\tilde E = 1\): the metric is a seam of \(G^{\pm1/2}\) tensors inserted on every bond leg, after which the unitary picture applies verbatim. When does such a \(G\) exist?

prop:hp-inner-product-discrete · proved A positive \(G\) with \(E^*GE = G\) (on the retained space, with critical radius \(1\)) exists iff \(E\) is diagonalisable with every eigenvalue of modulus one. It is a Hilbert–Pólya inner product: in it \(E\) is unitary and \(-i\log E\) is self-adjoint.

So the two halves of the problem separate. Positivity of the Toeplitz form is computable from counts. Existence of the metric is the statement that the spectrum lies on the circle and is semisimple. The finite Weil theorem of the parent page links them, and adds the one thing positivity cannot see: a Jordan block on the circle has positive counts but no metric.

The disc and the circle

The parent page proved, in finite form, that positivity of the Toeplitz form is a statement about the retained spectrum. In the notebook's general version the two inputs are kept apart:

thm:weil-positivity-finite · proved For any operator \(X\), \(S\) and \(r\), the rescaled sequence \(\nu_\ell = r^{-\ell}\big(\operatorname{Tr}X^\ell - \sum_{\mu\in S}\mu^\ell\big)\) is positive definite iff \(|\mu|\le r\) for every retained \(\mu\). A mode inside the disc passes.
thm:weil-duality-pairing · proved If the retained multiset is invariant under the \(J(\mu) = r^2/\bar\mu\), then \(W(c) = \sum_\mu p_c(\mu/r)\,\overline{p_c(J\mu/r)}\) (each mode paired with its partner) and positivity is equivalent to every retained mode lying on \(|\mu| = r\).

Positivity alone is the spectral-gap half of RH, "\(\operatorname{Re}\rho\le\frac12\)". The functional equation is the reflection symmetry, and only together do they put the spectrum on the circle. In network terms the reflection has a concrete origin, which step 4 exhibits: a pairing of the letters.

2. Permutations: point letters, a unitary transfer matrix

The parent page began with a permutation \(\sigma\) of \(n\) points and its matrix \(P\). As a network (the notebook's shard 03b, \(\pi = (1\,2)(3\,4\,5)\)): the bond is \(\mathbb C^n\), and the natural letters are the points, one matrix per point,

\[ A_x = |\sigma x\rangle\langle x|, \qquad \sum_x A_x = P, \qquad E = \sum_x |\sigma x\rangle\langle x|\otimes|\sigma x\rangle\langle x| . \]

A word \(w = (x_1,\dots,x_L)\) has \(\operatorname{Tr}(A_{x_L}\cdots A_{x_1}) = 1\) exactly when \(x_{k+1} = \sigma x_k\) around the ring, i.e. when it is the orbit of a point of period dividing \(L\); so the ring of length \(L\) is the sum of one orthonormal term per fixed point of \(\sigma^L\), and \(\|\psi_L\|^2 = \#\operatorname{Fix}(\sigma^L) = \operatorname{Tr}P^L\). On the doubled bond, \(E\) acts as \(P\) on the diagonal vectors \(|x\rangle\otimes|x\rangle\) and kills every off-diagonal one: its spectrum is that of \(P\) together with \(n^2-n\) zeros. The zeros are the trivial set here, \(r = 1\), and \(P\) is unitary in the native metric, so \(G = 1\) and step 1 applies with no work: the Toeplitz matrix of fixed-point counts is the Gram matrix of the strips \(P^0,\dots,P^{K-1}\).

The notebook's shard also grades a cycle odd and closes the ring with the parity operator inserted, \(t_k = \operatorname{Tr}(\Pi P^k)\). The odd cycle's eigenvalues then appear in the numerator of the graded zeta function: they are zeros, not poles, and the supertrace sequence is not positive definite. That is the sign convention of the curve on the parent page, \(T_k = q^{k/2}+q^{-k/2}-N_kq^{-k/2}\), seen at the level of letters: a positive form for zeros counts them with the sign reversed.

3. Shifts: the pole, and why positivity is only half

Let the letters be the symbols \(a\) of an alphabet and the allowed transitions a \(0/1\) matrix \(M\): the network has one letter per allowed transition, \(A_{a\to b} = |b\rangle\langle a|\), and \(E\) acts as \(M\) on the diagonal of the doubled bond. The ring norm \(t_L = \operatorname{Tr}M^L\) counts closed walks. Now there is a pole: the counts grow like \(\lambda_{\max}^L\), the Perron eigenvalue, which is the fixed point of the channel and carries no information about the rest of the spectrum. Subtracting it and rescaling by \(r = \sqrt{\lambda_{\max}}\) gives the retained sequence \(\nu_\ell = \lambda_{\max}^{-\ell/2}(t_\ell - \lambda_{\max}^\ell)\), and "RH for the shift" is the statement that every remaining nonzero eigenvalue has modulus \(\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}}\): the fluctuation of the counts is the square root of the leading term.

The golden-mean shift (\(M = \begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}\), eigenvalues \(\varphi, -1/\varphi\)) shows the gap between positivity and RH. Its retained mode \(-1/\varphi\) has modulus \(0.618 < \sqrt\varphi = 1.272\): the Toeplitz form is positive (the mode is inside the disc) and RH fails. The 4-symbol cyclic rule \(M = 1 + C_4\) has eigenvalues \(2, 1\pm i, 0\): the retained modes have modulus \(\sqrt2 = \sqrt{\lambda_{\max}}\), the form is positive, RH holds, and the retained set is invariant under \(\mu\mapsto 2/\bar\mu\). What the golden mean lacks is exactly the reflection symmetry. In the network, nothing in a general transition matrix pairs the letter \(a\to b\) with a letter \(b\to a\); the next example supplies that pairing.

4. Regular graphs: the reversal of an edge is the functional equation

A \((q+1)\)-regular graph is a shift whose alphabet is the set of directed edges and whose allowed transitions are \(e\to e'\) with \(\operatorname{head}(e) = \operatorname{tail}(e')\) and \(e'\ne\bar e\): the walk may not immediately reverse. The transition matrix is the \(B\), the ring norm \(t_L = \operatorname{Tr}B^L\) counts closed non-backtracking walks, and the zeta function \(1/\det(1-uB)\) is the . The alphabet now has an involution, edge reversal \(e\mapsto\bar e\), and that single extra structure produces the functional equation:

\[ \det(1-uB) \;=\; (1-u^2)^{|E|-|V|}\,\det\big(1-uA+qu^2\big), \]

the Ihara–Bass formula. The spectrum of \(B\) is \(\pm1\) with multiplicity \(|E|-|V|\) together with, for each eigenvalue \(a\) of the adjacency matrix \(A\), the two roots of \(\mu^2 - a\mu + q = 0\). Those two roots multiply to \(q\): the retained set is invariant under \(\mu\mapsto q/\mu\), which with real coefficients is the reflection \(J\) for \(r = \sqrt q\). The trivial set is \(\{q,1\}\) from \(a = q+1\) (the pole), \(\{-q,-1\}\) from \(a = -(q+1)\) if the graph is bipartite, and the \(\pm1\). Now both theorems of step 1 apply, and Weil positivity of the non-backtracking ring norms is equivalent to every retained mode lying on \(|\mu| = \sqrt q\), which is \(|a|\le2\sqrt q\) for every nontrivial \(a\): the . Huang's criterion \(h_k = \nu_0 - \nu_k\ge0\) is the same statement in the form of the \(2\times2\) minors (the notebook's observation X1).

When it exists, it is the inner product in which the non-backtracking walk, restricted to the modes off the trivial set and rescaled by \(\sqrt q\), is a unitary. The Ramanujan property of the graph is the existence of that metric.

5. Kraus families: which pairing buys which half

Replace the permutation matrices behind a graph by arbitrary matrices. A family \(B_1,\dots,B_D\) on a bond \(V\) of dimension \(n\), with a fixed-point-free reversal \(i\mapsto\bar i\) of the letters, defines the maps \(\mathcal E_i = \operatorname{Ad}(B_i)\) on \(\operatorname{End}(V)\) and the : a letter register \(\mathbb C^D\) is added to the doubled bond, and the non-backtracking transfer operator on \(W = \mathbb C^D\otimes\operatorname{End}(V)\) is

\[ T\,(v\otimes|i\rangle) = \sum_{j\ne\bar i}\mathcal E_i v\otimes|j\rangle, \qquad \operatorname{Tr}T^\ell = \sum_{\substack{w\in[D]^\ell\\ w_{k+1}\ne\bar w_k}}\big|\operatorname{Tr}(B_{w_\ell}\cdots B_{w_1})\big|^2 \;\ge\;0 . \]

The second identity says that the ring norms of the lifted network are genuine MPS ring norms, summed over non-backtracking words only, and that they are nonnegative for every family whatever: it uses only \(\operatorname{Ad}(A)\operatorname{Ad}(B) = \operatorname{Ad}(AB)\) and \(\operatorname{Tr}\operatorname{Ad}(A) = |\operatorname{Tr}A|^2\). The spectrum of \(T\) is also always closed under conjugation. Beyond that, the two natural ways to pair the letters buy the two halves of RH separately (the notebook's Kraus dichotomy, shard 08b):

thm:kraus-inverse-pairing-duality · proved Inverse pairing \(B_{\bar i} = B_i^{-1}\): \(\det(1-uT) = (1-u^2)^{N(D-2)/2}\det(1-u\Sigma+(D-1)u^2)\) with \(\Sigma = \sum_i\mathcal E_i\) and \(N = n^2\). The retained set is invariant under \(\mu\mapsto q/\mu\), \(q = D-1\): the functional equation.
thm:kraus-weil-criterion · proved Adjoint pairing \(B_{\bar i} = B_i^\dagger\): \(\Sigma\) is Hilbert–Schmidt self-adjoint, so its spectrum is real, but there is no duality, and Weil positivity is exactly the one-sided bound \(|\mu|\le r\). Both pairings hold iff every \(B_i\) is unitary, and then Weil positivity of the retained rescaled ring norms is Hastings' bound \(|\lambda|\le 2\sqrt{D-1}/D\) on the nontrivial eigenvalues of the channel \(\Phi = \Sigma/D\).

A quantum channel that is a Ramanujan quantum expander is therefore a Kraus family whose non-backtracking ring norms form a positive definite sequence, in the same sense, with the same trivial set and the same radius \(\sqrt{D-1}\), as a Ramanujan graph. The classical graph is the case where the \(B_i\) are the permutation matrices of the generators; the Weil–LPS channels of the notebook are exact examples with \(V = \ell^2(\mathbb F_p)\).

6. Artin–Schreier curves: a transfer matrix that is a scaled unitary

For the curve \(y^q - y = g(x)\) over \(\mathbb F_q\) with \(g(x) = \sum_{j=0}^{J}a_jx^{1+q^j}\) quadratic in the Frobenius-shift sense, the notebook's note artin-schreier-mps exhibits an honest MPS. An element of \(\mathbb F_{q^m}\) in a self-dual normal basis is a ring of \(m\) sites with alphabet \(\mathbb F_q\) on which Frobenius is the shift; \(\operatorname{Tr}g(x)\) becomes a translation-invariant quadratic form of range \(J\); and the exponential sum \(S_m(g) = \sum_{x\in\mathbb F_{q^m}}\psi(\operatorname{Tr}g(x))\) is the ring contraction of a transfer matrix of size \(q^J\),

\[ E_{(s_1,\dots,s_J)\to(s_2,\dots,s_J,x)} = \psi\Big(a_0x^2 + \sum_{j=1}^{J}a_j\,s_{J+1-j}\,x\Big), \qquad S_m = \epsilon_m\operatorname{Tr}E^m, \qquad N_m = q^m + \sum_{a\in\mathbb F_q^\times}S_m(ag) , \]

with a sign \(\epsilon_m = \pm1\) fixed by the sign law of shard 06b (thm:as-sign-law): \(\epsilon_m = -(-1)^m\) when \(P_g(-1)\ne0\), as for \(g = x^{1+q}\), and periodic with a longer period otherwise. The transfer matrix is, up to that sign, the Frobenius matrix of the exponential sum, uniformly in \(m\).

The physical index is \(x\in\mathbb F_q\), the bond is \(\mathbb F_q^J\). Here the metric of step 1 is the native one: in \((EE^\dagger)_{s's''}\) the oldest bond spin \(s_1\) appears only in the linear term \(a_Js_1(x'-x'')\), so the sum over \(s_1\) is \(q\,\delta_{x'x''}\) whenever \(a_J\ne0\), and

\[ EE^\dagger = q\,1 . \]

\(E/\sqrt q\) is unitary, every eigenvalue has modulus \(\sqrt q\), and the Toeplitz form of \(t_k = \operatorname{Tr}(E/\sqrt q)^k\) is the Gram matrix of the open strips in the standard Hilbert–Schmidt product with \(G = 1\). The Riemann hypothesis for this family is manifest: it is Parseval for additive characters, the same mechanism as the permutation of step 2. The functional equation is \(E\mapsto qE^{-\dagger}\), rationality is finiteness of the bond. The note also records where this stops: for \(g\) of degree three or more in any digit the trace form is not local in the shift basis, there is no fixed local tensor, and the Weil bound is Deligne's theorem, not a property of a transfer structure.

7. The Riemann zeta function: the strips are known, the metric is not

Read the parent page's explicit formula in the language above. Time is now the log coordinate \(x\in\mathbb R\) and the strip of length \(x\) is dilation by \(e^x\), the operator \(E^x\) of a continuous transfer semigroup. Its "ring norm" is a distribution, not a number: the explicit formula says that

\[ \text{“}\operatorname{Tr}E^x\text{”} \;=\; 2\cosh(x/2)\;-\;\sum_{n}\frac{\Lambda(n)}{\sqrt n}\big(\delta_{\log n}+\delta_{-\log n}\big)\;-\;(\text{archimedean kernel}), \]

pole minus primes minus archimedean place, and that its Fourier transform is \(\sum_\rho \delta_{\gamma_\rho}\), the spectral side. A smeared strip \(E^g = \int g(x)E^x\,dx\) has finite overlaps, and the Gram matrix of a family of smeared strips is exactly the parent page's Gram matrix of the Weil functional:

\[ G_{ij} = \big\langle E^{g_i},E^{g_j}\big\rangle = W(g_i*\tilde g_j) . \]

Every entry is a finite computation from the primes, the pole and the archimedean term. Under RH this is the Gram matrix of the functions \(\hat g_i\) restricted to the zeros in \(\ell^2(\text{zeros})\): the metric that step 1 asked for exists, and in it the dilation semigroup, restricted to the zero modes, is unitary (Hilbert–Pólya). Without RH, it is the notebook's question. The network is completely known: the letters are the prime dilations, the counts are the explicit formula. What is unknown is whether there is any metric \(G\) on the retained bond in which the strips have these overlaps, i.e. whether the sequence of overlaps is a Gram sequence at all. That is Weil's criterion, and it is what the notebook's Riemann tomography programme treats as an unknown quantum state: the Gram matrices computable from arithmetic are its measurable moments.

The second family the notebook studies is the Connes–Consani–Moscovici window: Fourier modes on a dilation window \([\lambda^{-1},\lambda]\), \(x = \lambda^2\), whose Gram matrix is a Loewner matrix computed in certified ball arithmetic by the zst code. There the counts-to-spectrum step of the finite examples reappears: in step 2 the strips \(P^0,\dots,P^{K-1}\) become linearly dependent once \(K\) exceeds the number of distinct eigenvalues, and the kernel polynomial's roots are the spectrum. For zeta the spectrum is infinite, so the Gram matrix has no kernel, but its minimal eigenvector is a near-dependence among the strips, and the CCM correction turns that direction into an operator whose spectrum reproduces the low zeros to dozens of digits from the primes below \(x\).
