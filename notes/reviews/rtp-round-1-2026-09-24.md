## Verdicts

### C1. Bordering lemma and the Loewner interval: MINOR

*Independent proof.* (1)–(2): the congruence `[[I,0],[-c^*G^{-1},1]] B [[I,-G^{-1}c],[0,1]] = diag(G, s)`, `s = d - c^*G^{-1}c`,
gives inertia and `det B = det G * s`. (3): for fixed `d`, `s <= d` with equality iff `G^{-1/2}c = 0`, so `c = 0` is the
unique maximiser of `det B`. (4): for a real centred Gaussian, `I(X;Y) = (1/2) log(det G * d / det B)`, so
`Delta I = log(d/s) = 2 I(X;Y)` (for a complex circular Gaussian it would be `I`, not `2I`; the forms here are real).
Loewner section: with `c(b) = u + b w`, `d(b) = a_j +- b/j` affine in `b = b_{N+1}`, `s(b) = d(b) - c(b)^*G^{-1}c(b)` is a
quadratic with leading coefficient `-w^*G^{-1}w < 0` (since `w_{e,0} = sqrt2/j != 0`, `w_{o,i} = -2i/(i^2 - j^2) != 0`),
so each block's admissible set `{s >= 0}` is an interval, its endpoints are exactly where the bordered block is
singular, and the vertex of the parabola (the block's max-det point) is its midpoint. The unstructured column: the
even column vanishes iff `sqrt2 b_j/j = 0` and `b_i (1/(i-j) + 1/(i+j)) = 2 i b_i/(i^2 - j^2) = 0` for `i <= N`, i.e.
iff all `b_1..b_{N+1}` vanish (sympy confirms at `N = 4`; the even column alone already forces it).

*Numerics.* Five random trials (`det B = det G s`, PSD iff `s >= 0`, max det at `c = 0` over 917 admissible random `c`,
`Delta I = 2 I` to 1e-9); on the true zeta window (`x = 13`, `N = 10 -> 11`, 120 digits) the Schur complements are
exactly quadratic (fourth-point residual 1e-114) and all four interval endpoints give singular bordered blocks.

*Defect.* C1 says "the admissible set is an interval, its centre is the structured MaxEnt prediction". For the
window form in the `V_n` basis (both blocks), the admissible set of `b_{N+1}` is the **intersection** of the even and
odd intervals, and the max-det point maximises `s_e s_o` on it. That maximiser is **not** the centre:

| `(x, N)` | 13, 10 | 13, 30 | 13, 56 | 13, 100 | 25, 20 | 25, 100 | 25, 134 | 50, 50 | 50, 300 | 50, 352 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| joint max-det offset from the centre (half-widths) | −0.30 | +0.16 | −0.016 | −0.0008 | +0.30 | +0.13 | −0.006 | −0.29 | −0.043 | −0.012 |

Lane A1's Lemma A1.1'(2) and the draft shard's `lem:bordering-interval`(ii) say this correctly ("of each block";
"the joint max-det point maximises `s_e s_o`"); the claim-draft wording does not. Also "given `a_{N+1}`" and "the

### C4. Kronecker-sum lemma: VALID

*Proof.* Every Gram entry depends only on `D = |t_alpha - t_beta|` (translation invariance of `Psi` on the log line).
In the `nomix` matrix an entry is (i) the common diagonal `d` if `alpha = beta`, (ii) `g(|a_p - b_p| log p)` if `alpha`,
`beta` differ only in coordinate `p`, which is the entry `G_(p)[a_p, b_p]` of the one-prime form on `{0..A}`, (iii) zero
otherwise. The operator `d I + sum_p I (x) .. (x) (G_(p) - d I) (x) .. (x) I` has exactly these entries. Its spectrum is
`{d + sum_p (mu_p - d)}`, so the minimal eigenvalue is `d + sum_p (lambda_min(G_(p)) - d)` with eigenvector
`(x)_p v_(p)`, unique when each `lambda_min(G_(p))` is simple (A2 certifies simplicity).

Remarks (not defects). The hypothesis "admissible `delta`" is **not needed** for the lemma: it holds for any even
kernel, which the scratch script checks with a random kernel on `{2,3}` `A = 2, 3` and `{2,3,5}` `A = 1, 2` (entrywise
agreement 3e-15, eigenvalue sum rule 1e-12, product eigenvector `1 - overlap <= 1e-15`). Admissibility is needed only
for the separate statement that mixed entries carry no prime term. "The" eigenvector needs simplicity.

### C6. What a single-place restriction sees: INVALID as stated

1. **The displayed identity presupposes RH.** For real `g`, `(g * g~)^(s) = g^(s) g^(1 - s)` and the explicit formula
   gives `W(g * g~) = sum_rho g^(rho) conj(g^(1 - conj rho))` (the notebook's own `cit:weil-criterion`, `db/claims.tsv`
   row 103). This equals `sum_rho |g^(rho)|^2` term by term only when `1 - conj rho = rho`, i.e. on the critical line.
   An off-line pair `rho, 1 - conj rho` contributes `2 Re[g^(rho) conj(g^(1 - conj rho))]`, which can be negative. As
   displayed, `W(g * g~) = sum |phi^(rho)|^2 |P(p^{-rho})|^2` is a sum of squares and would make positivity of every
   one-place restriction automatic, which is an RH-type statement. The next clause ("an off-line pair is visible to it
   unless `P` vanishes") contradicts the display. Game rule 1 is violated by the formula, not by any computation.
2. **"Mixed `{p, q}` entries have no prime term"** holds only for test functions whose autocorrelations are supported
   within `2 delta < 2 delta_max(S, A)` of the lattice ratios (lane A2 §1.3). For wider bumps a mixed entry picks up
   every prime power near the ratio (A2's non-admissible checks at `r = 6, 8`).
3. **"The content of Landau's formula"; "their zero-side Landau main term vanishes".** Landau's formula is a
   sharp-cutoff asymptotic, `sum_{0<gamma<=T} x^rho = -(T/2pi) Lambda(x) + O_x(log T)` (image of p. 553 checked). The
   smoothed zero sums in C6 are *equal*, exactly, to the prime side by the explicit formula; for an admissible mixed
   entry they equal pole minus archimedean, a known O(`delta`) number. Calling that "the error term of Landau's formula
   at composite `x`" (shard `obs:single-place-restriction`) conflates an exact smoothed identity with a sharp-cutoff

### C8. The maximum-determinant next lag is the disc centre, i.e. Burg's extension: VALID

*Independent proof (one step).* Write `W_{K+1}(x)` for the order-`K+1` window with new lag `x`. By the Schur
complement, `det W_{K+1}(x) = det W_K * g(x)`, `g(x) = nu_0 - u(x)^* W_K^{-1} u(x)`, and `g(x) = g(c) - (W_K^{-1})_{00}
|x - c|^2` with `(W_K^{-1})_{00} > 0`. So `det W_{K+1}` is a strictly concave function of `x` on the disc, maximal
exactly at the centre `c`, and positive completions are exactly the disc. By the Levinson (Szegő) recursion
`det W_{K+1}/det W_K = (det W_K/det W_{K-1})(1 - |gamma_{K+1}|^2)` with `gamma_{K+1}` the next Verblunsky
(reflection) coefficient; maximising the left side is `gamma_{K+1} = 0`. Hence max-det = centre = zero reflection
coefficient, which is Burg's disc (`ch2b.ocr.txt:303-304`: radius `P_{N-1}`, centre `-sum R(N-n) b_n`) and his centre
choice (`:337-340`).

*Multi-step (lane B1's derivation, checked).* For the band pattern `|i - j| <= K` of an `n x n` Toeplitz matrix, the
feasible completions form a convex set on which `log det` is strictly concave, and `d log det / dW_ij = (W^{-1})_ji`;
a completion whose inverse vanishes on every free entry is therefore the unique maximiser (this needs neither GJSW nor
chordality). The centre-by-centre extension (all later reflection coefficients zero) is the AR(`K`) covariance: its
Gaussian process has `x_t` independent of `x_{t-K-1}, x_{t-K-2}, ...` given the `K` previous values, so the density
factorises over windows of `K+1` consecutive variables and the precision matrix is banded. Hence it is the max-det
completion, and it is Toeplitz. Burg's own MaxEnt statement is about spectra (`int log P`); the finite-matrix
identification is the notebook's, and correct. Vandenberghe–Andersen's statement (`paper.txt:5026-5067`, `5360-5368`)

### Erratum to this review (the reviewer's own error, carried into the corrected texts)

The C12(ii) paragraph above says: "The pole contributes to the Schmidt defect only because its mixed entries are
split off from its axis entries". Audit A's corrected paragraph says: "the pole kernel `e^{+-D/2}` factorises over
places, so the only non-factorising cross-place kernel is the archimedean `rho(D)`". **Both are wrong.**

The pole kernel `2 cosh(D/2) = e^{D/2} + e^{-D/2}` is a *sum* of two kernels, each of which factorises. The pole
matrix is therefore `c^2(X Y^T + Y X^T)`: rank two, with product vectors in its range, but not a tensor product. On
its own (mixed archimedean part removed) it produces a Schmidt defect of the same order as the full form (table above).

Corrected sentence, for the shard, for metric-as-state §3, and in place of the two passages above: "The pole
kernel `2 cosh(D/2) = e^{D/2} + e^{-D/2}` is a sum of two kernels that each factorise over places. The pole part of
the Gram matrix therefore has rank two, but it is not a tensor product and on its own correlates the places.
Both parts of the mixed entries carry inter-place correlation; to first order along `v_min` the pole part is the
larger (2.4 to 21 times, opposite sign), and only the archimedean kernel `rho(D)` is of full rank."

This makes `obs:rtp-round-1-reading`(ii) MINOR below. The same sentence in
