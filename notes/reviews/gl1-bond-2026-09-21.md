## Verdicts

### B1. The class-group bond

**VERDICT B1: VALID.** Every clause is right and I reproduced all of it independently, at genus
one *and* at genus two.

(a) I enumerated all effective divisors of degree `<= 5` on both curves as multisets of closed
points (closed points = Frobenius orbits of exact size `k` in `C(F_{q^k})`, `k <= 6`), computed the
class of each by the orbit trace in `E(F_{q^d})` (rationality of the trace checked, not assumed, for
every closed point of degree `<= 5`), and found `a_n = sum_{[D] in Pic^n}(q^{h^0(D)}-1)/(q-1)`
exactly for `n = 0..5` on both curves: D2 `a = [1,1,3,7,15,31,63]`, D3 `a = [1,4,16,52,160,484,1456]`,
matching the Euler product `prod_P (1-T^{deg P})^{-1}` over `b_d = [1,2,4,5,8,8]` resp.
`[4,6,8,12,48,124]` closed points and the Taylor coefficients of `P/((1-T)(1-qT))`. Every class of
degree `n >= 1` carries exactly `(q^n-1)/(q-1)` effective divisors and, in degree 0, only the trivial
class carries one — i.e. the count `(q^{h^0}-1)/(q-1)` of the proof is exact class by class, not
merely in the aggregate.

(b) holds for every `n >= 1` on both curves, with the single deviation at `n = 0 = 2g-2`. I also
tested (b) where the round does not: on a genus-two curve, `C : y^2 = (x^4+x+1)(x^2+1)` over `F_3`,
`N_k = [5,17,26,89,215,674]`, `P(T) = 1+T+4T^2+3T^3+9T^4` (all four `|alpha_i| = 1.732050807569`),
`a = [1,5,21,72,234,720,2178]`. The generic formula with `h = P(1) = 18` gives `[-6,0,18]` in degrees
`0,1,2` and the true value from degree 3 on: **the deviation set is exactly `n <= 2g-2 = 2`**, as (b)
says, and the deviations are three, not one.

(c) `P = 1-(q+1-h)T+qT^2`, `Z = 1+hT/((1-T)(1-qT))`, `h = P(1) = N_1`: exact in sympy for both
curves, `P_{D2} = 1-2T+2T^2`, `P_{D3} = 1+3T^2`, matching `def:elliptic-cavity`.

(d) is true. Two remarks on the *proof*, neither of which makes anything printed false.

*Remark 1 (the proof is far heavier than the statement).* `h = P(1)` follows from (a)+(b) alone, in
two lines and with no Lefschetz formula, no Jacobian and no uncited source: by (b) the tail of `Z`
is `-h/(q-1) . T^{2g-1}/(1-T)` plus a pole at `T = 1/q`, so `lim_{T->1}(1-T)Z(T) = -h/(q-1)` and
`P(1) = (1-q) . (-h/(q-1)) = h`. I verified the equivalent statement — that the tail
`a_n = P(1)(q^{n+1-g}-1)/(q-1)` holds for every `n > 2g-2` — for `g = 0,1,2,3` on synthetic Weil
polynomials (`P(1) = 1, 1, 4, 8, 40`) and, independently, on the genus-two curve above, where the
Euler product over the enumerated closed points gives `a_n` and `P(1) = 18` with no cohomology
anywhere. The printed proof instead invokes "Weil; the Lefschetz formula on `H^1` of the Jacobian",
which is not byte-cited and is not needed.

*Remark 2 (the rational-point hypothesis in (d) is superfluous).* The proof writes
"`J(F_q) = Pic^0(C)(F_q)` when a rational point exists". Over a **finite** field this is
unconditional: `Br(F_q) = 0` (Wedderburn / Lang), so `Pic(C/F_q) -> Pic(C_{\bar F_q})^{Frob}` is
onto, and F. K. Schmidt's theorem gives a rational divisor class of degree one whether or not a
rational point exists. (A rational point is needed elsewhere in B1 — for "`Pic^n` is a torsor for
`Pic^0`, translate by `nP_0`" — and is a standing hypothesis of the proposition, so nothing is
broken; but (d) does not need it and should not advertise it as the reason.) What the Lefschetz step
*does* need, and does not say, is `H^i(J) = wedge^i H^1(J)` (the alternating sum collapses to
`det(1-Fr|H^1)` only for an abelian variety) and `H^1(J) ~ H^1(C)`.

*Reading (dictionary, not theorem).* "the bond `l^2(Pic^0) (x) l^2(Z)` with the degree shift as
transfer has ring norms `a_n`" exhibits **no operator**: the degree shift on `l^2(Z)` is not trace
class and no transfer matrix whose length-`n` ring norm is `a_n` is written down. What is a theorem
is the two-term recursion `a_{n+1} = (q+1)a_n - q a_{n-1}` for `n > 2g-2`, whose characteristic roots
are `q` and `1`; calling those "the two eigenvalues of the generic part" is fair, calling `a_n` a
ring norm of a named bond is not yet. The `P(T)`-as-`H^1`-factor sentence correctly absorbs F4
(`thm:scattering-superdeterminant`'s odd spectrum is `alpha_i, q, q^2` in `w = T^2`).

### B2. The theta functional; Poisson summation is Riemann–Roch

**VERDICT B2: MINOR.** (a), (b) and the Poisson/Riemann–Roch core of (c) are correct, and the
fibre-volume bookkeeping — the point I was asked to attack — survives. The defect is the
parenthesis about the fixed locus of the theta involution, which is false for every `g >= 2`.

*What holds.* (a): I counted `#L(nP_0)` for `n = 0..4` on both curves by enumerating the entire
`F_q`-span of `{x^i y^j : j <= 1, 2i+3j <= n}` and separating the resulting functions by their values
at all 64 (D2) resp. 783 (D3) affine points of `C(F_{q^6})`: `2,2,4,8,16` and `3,3,9,27,81`, i.e.
exactly `q^{h^0}` including the zero function, which is what `Theta(xf) = #L(D)` counts.

(b) is right, including the `1/(q-1)`. The two displayed integrals are the *same* integral unfolded
over `K^x`, and the two fibre normalisations are forced and consistent:
`ker(A^x -> Div) = prod_v O_v^x` has volume 1, so each fibre of `{div >= 0} -> {D >= 0}` has volume 1
and the `A^x` integral is `sum_{D>=0} q^{-s deg D}`; while
`ker(A^x/K^x -> Pic) = (prod_v O_v^x)K^x/K^x ~ prod_v O_v^x / (prod_v O_v^x cap K^x)`
and `prod_v O_v^x cap K^x = H^0(C,O_C)^x = F_q^x` has order `q-1` — which I checked in the only form
a finite computation can: the unique effective divisor of degree 0 is `0` and `h^0(0) = 1`, so
`L(0) = F_q` and the global units are `q-1` in number. Hence fibre volume `1/(q-1)`, and the two
routes agree coefficient by coefficient for `n = 0..5` on both curves. The consistency is not
automatic — it *is* the statement `#{E >= 0 : E ~ D} = (q^{h^0(D)}-1)/(q-1)` of B1(a), which I
verified class by class.

(c), Poisson = Riemann–Roch: correct, and the constant is right. With the self-dual measure
(`vol(A/K) = 1`) one has `vol(O_A) = q^{1-g}` and `hat(1_{O_A}) = q^{1-g} 1_{O_A(K_C)}`, where
`O_A(K_C) = {t : v(t_v) + n_v >= 0}` is the annihilator of `O_A` under `psi`; Poisson at an idele `x`
with `div x = D` then reads `q^{h^0(D)} = q^{deg D} . q^{1-g} . q^{h^0(K_C-D)}`, which is
Riemann–Roch. I verified the identity in the form `q^{h^0(D)} = q^{deg D+1-g} q^{h^0(K_C-D)}` for
every class of degree `-4..4` on both curves, and verified the *shape and constant* of the Fourier
step (`hat(1_U) = |U| . 1_{U^perp}`, `|U||U^perp| = |G|`) in finite self-dual models `Z/12`, `Z/16`
for every subgroup. The functional equation `Z(1/(qT)) = q^{1-g}T^{2-2g}Z(T)` is exact in sympy for
`g = 0,1,2,3` (the prefactor is nontrivial at `g = 0` and `g >= 2`, so `g = 1`'s `1` is not doing the
work).

**Defect 1 (the headline).** (c) ends: "On `Pic^{g-1}` the map `[D] -> [K_C-D]` preserves the theta
divisor `W_{g-1} = {[D] : h^0(D) > 0}` (its fixed locus is the `K_C`-translate of the 2-torsion, **in
general larger** than `W_{g-1}`; numerics F5)." The words "in general larger" are **false for every
`g >= 2`**, and the genus-one examples are the only place they hold. On my genus-two curve, a class
of degree `g-1 = 1` has `h^0 <= 1` (Riemann–Roch: `h^0(D) = h^0(K-D)` with `deg(K-D) = 1`), so every
class of `W_1` is `[P]` for exactly one rational point and `|W_1(F_{3^k})| = N_k = 5, 17, 26, 89,
215, 674` for `k = 1..6`; the fixed locus `{[D] : 2[D] = [K_C]}` is a torsor under `Pic^0[2]`, of
order at most `2^{2g} = 16` over *any* field. So the fixed locus is strictly **smaller** from `k = 3`
on, and geometrically `dim W_{g-1} = g-1 >= 1` against a finite fixed locus. Neither set contains the
other in general (a theta characteristic need not have sections).

*Corrected statement.* "…preserves the theta divisor `W_{g-1}`. Its fixed locus is the set of
theta characteristics `{[D] in Pic^{g-1} : 2[D] = [K_C]}`, a torsor under `Pic^0[2]` (possibly empty
over `F_q`) of order at most `2^{2g}`; at genus one this is larger than `W_0 = {[0]}` (**2** classes
for D3, **1** for D2 — I recomputed both), while for `g >= 2` it is finite against a
`(g-1)`-dimensional `W_{g-1}` and is in general strictly smaller. The two sets are incomparable in
general." Note that the numerics lane's F5 carries the same "in general strictly larger", so the
error was inherited, not introduced; it must not be inherited a third time into the shard.

**Defect 2 (scope).** (b) states two integral identities with no convergence range. Both converge
only for `Re s > 1` (`|T| < 1/q`); the equality with `Z(q^{-s})` elsewhere is by continuation.
Add "for `Re s > 1`, and elsewhere by analytic continuation".

**Defect 3 (the hidden input).** "`hat f = q^{1-g} 1_{O_A(K_C)}`-type translate by a canonical
divisor" is not a statement, and it silently chooses the additive character. The conductor divisor of
`psi` is a canonical divisor precisely when `psi` is built from a nonzero global differential through
the residues — that choice, not Poisson, is where Serre duality enters. As printed, "for this `f`
Poisson summation **is** Riemann–Roch" should read "Poisson summation for `f = 1_{O_A}`, with the
self-dual measure and the additive character `psi_omega` attached to a global differential `omega`
(so that the conductor of `psi_omega` is the canonical divisor `K_C = div omega`), is Riemann–Roch";
and `hat f = q^{1-g} 1_{O_A(K_C)}` should be displayed as an equation with `O_A(K_C)` defined.

*Reading.* "the automorphism acts on the bond state as `D -> K_C-D`" is a fair gloss (Poisson pairs
`Theta_f(x)` with `Theta_{hat f}(x^{-1})`, and `x -> x^{-1}`, `f -> hat f` is `D -> -D` composed with
the conductor shift by `K_C`), but the Weyl element does **not** fix `f = 1_{O_A}`: it sends it to
`q^{1-g}1_{O_A(K_C)}`. The bond state is not an eigenvector of the automorphism; only the pair
(state, its image) is exchanged. Say so.

### B3. The cusps of the tree quotient are the class-group bond

**VERDICT B3: MINOR.** (a) and (b) are true as stated and I verified (b) at genus two, where it has
content. The defect is (c): its one-word proof "(c) Arithmetic." is wrong about half of the claim,
which is conditional on two hypotheses of the notebook, and the accompanying *Reading* asserts a
diagonalisation that is false.

*What holds.* (a) The Steinitz argument is correct for `Gamma = GL_2(R)`: `L -> L cap R^2` is a
bijection from `Gamma\P^1(K)` onto the rank-one projective classes, every class occurs (because
`M (+) M^{-1} ~ R (+) R`), and the answer is `Pic(R)`, not a quotient of it. There is no `R^x` to
divide by: `R^x` lies in the stabiliser of every line. `SL_2(R)` gives the **same** count, because
`det : Stab_{GL_2(R)}(L) -> R^x` is onto (act by `a` on `M` and `b` on `R^2/M ~ M^{-1}`, `det = ab`).
`Pic(R) = Pic(C)/<[P_0]> ~ Pic^0(K)` **does** need `deg P_0 = 1`, and the proposition states it; with
`deg P_0 = d` one gets `h d`, which is exactly Lorscheid's byte-cited `h_X d_x`
(`1012.3513:hecke.tex:992`). I recomputed `h = 1` (D2) and `h = 4` with `Pic^0 = Z/4` (D3, element
orders `[1,2,4,4]`).

(b) is correct, and the argument for the genus-one value is correct: for `n >= 1` the weight
`(q^{h^0}-1)/(q-1)` is constant on `Pic^n` and `sum_{[D] in Pic^n} chi([D]) = 0` by orthogonality on
the `Pic^0`-torsor, so only `n = 0` survives and `L = 1`. I confirmed `L(T,chi) = 1` to `T^5` for all
three nontrivial characters of D3 and `L(T,chi_0) = Z(T)`. The degree-`2g-2` claim is the only part
of B3 with content beyond genus one, and I tested it on the genus-two curve with a genuine
everywhere-unramified quadratic character (`K(sqrt f_1) = K(sqrt f_2)` since `f_1f_2 = y^2`; `chi`
computed place by place, the two recipes cross-checked where both apply, `chi = +1` at both infinite
places): **`L(T,chi) = 1 + 3T + 3T^2`**, exactly degree `2g-2 = 2`, with the coefficients of
`T^3..T^6` vanishing, and the same from the divisor sum `sum_{D >= 0, deg D = n} chi([D])` for
`n <= 4`. Confirmation that this is the right object: `P_C(T)L(T,chi) = 1+4T+10T^2+18T^3+30T^4+
36T^5+27T^6` has degree `6 = 2g'` with `g' = 2g-1 = 3` (Riemann–Hurwitz for an unramified double
cover), satisfies the genus-three functional equation `c_{2g'-i} = q^{g'-i}c_i`, and all six of its
inverse roots have modulus `sqrt 3`.

### B5. What is and is not gained

**VERDICT B5: MINOR.** The conclusions are right and I confirmed both negative claims, but the
sentence that carries the `K_HW` half of the argument is false as written: it says *Frobenius* where

|---|---|---|
| `{H : Z^* H Z = r^2 H}` (`thm:arithmetic-metric`) | **4** | **1** (a single ray) |
| `{H : (Z^2)^* H (Z^2) = r^4 H}` (Frobenius normal) | **8** | **2** |
