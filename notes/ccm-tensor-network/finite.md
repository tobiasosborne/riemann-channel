# Finite CCM as a tensor network: operator Gram, moment GNS, and what is reconstructed

Status: author draft, subsequently attacked in `notes/reviews/ccm-tensor-finite-r1.md`.
Adjudicated scopes and current statuses live in `db/claims.tsv`; unregistered extensions remain SKETCH. Previously reviewed results are
cited with their database status, but no status is promoted by its author.
This file owns only the finite lane requested in `notes/ccm-tensor-network/BRIEF.md`.

The proposal under audit is `HANDOFF.md:181-202`.  Its useful core is real, but it conflates four
different spaces and two different metrics.  After separating them, the exact finite statement is:

> On the critical circle, the Weil Toeplitz matrix is the Gram matrix of the powers of the
> **retained transfer operator** in the native Hilbert--Schmidt metric only when that retained
> operator is natively unitary (or under an explicit all-window converse).  A merely
> unitarisable operator gives the same contraction in a similarity-induced operator metric.
> Independently of either realisation, the moment functional has a cyclic GNS space.  At the exact
> critical window its null polynomial is the square-free polynomial of the distinct retained
> spectral support.  It equals the original operator's minimal polynomial only when the retained
> operator is semisimple.  The GNS operator reconstructs the trace semisimplification; weights can
> recover algebraic multiplicities, but trace moments lose Jordan data, physical MPS letters, and
> the native bond metric.

This is a tensor-network statement, rather than just a spectral tautology, because the Gram
factor is an explicit two-layer contraction of vectorised transfer powers, and it has an exact
clock-register purification and an exact bounded-bond unary-clock automaton.

## 0. Local notation and source anchors

Local notation is deliberately plain so that definitions can be centralised later.

- `B` is the physical MPS auxiliary space, `dim B = D`.
- `K := B tensor conjugate(B) ~= End(B)` is the doubled transfer space, `dim K = d = D^2`.
- `E := sum_s A_s tensor conjugate(A_s)` acts on `K`.
- `L := End(K)` is the operator Hilbert space containing the powers of `E`, `dim L = d^2 = D^4`.
- `r > 0` is the critical radius and `U := E_ret/r` is a retained restriction when such a
  restriction has actually been specified.
- `t_n := Tr(U^n)` for `n >= 0`, `t_{-n} := conjugate(t_n)`, and
  `W_N[j,k] := t_{k-j}`, `0 <= j,k <= N`.
- `p_c(z) := sum_{j=0}^N c_j z^j`.
- `H_mom` is the cyclic moment/GNS space.  It is not `B`, `K`, or `L`.

The report definitions are `def:graded-lattice-tensor`, `def:rescaled-trace-sequence`, and
`def:weil-form`.  The following reviewed results are inputs, not new claims here:

- `thm:weil-positivity-finite` (PROVED): positivity iff retained modes lie in the disc;
- `thm:weil-duality-pairing` (PROVED): reflection duality upgrades the disc to the circle;
- `prop:hp-inner-product-discrete` (PROVED): circle plus semisimplicity iff unitarisable;
- `prop:weil-blind-jordan` (PROVED): trace Weil forms do not see Jordan blocks.

For the finite Toeplitz structure, `refs/src/2511.23257/Araki-final-oct25.tex:760-786` states the
Vandermonde factorisation, lines 792-800 state the rank-`n` kernel-polynomial corollary, and lines
898-909 give its GNS proof and warn that a higher-dimensional kernel has arbitrary extra roots.

## 1. The four spaces and the natural ring contraction

### F1 (SKETCH): the space ladder and ring contraction

**ASSUME.**  Let `(A_s)_{s in S}` be matrices on `B`, and for a word
`w = s_1 ... s_n` put `A_w = A_{s_1} ... A_{s_n}`.

**PROVE.**

1. `E = sum_s A_s tensor conjugate(A_s)` acts on `K`, not on the original auxiliary space `B`.
2. The ungraded ring norm is

       Tr_K(E^n) = sum_{w in S^n} |Tr_B(A_w)|^2.

3. If `B = B_+ direct-sum B_-` has parity `P`, then with `Gamma = P tensor conjugate(P)`,

       Tr_K(Gamma E^n) = sum_w |Tr_B(P A_w)|^2.

4. A Gram vector `|E^j>>` belongs to `L ~= K tensor conjugate(K)`, one full doubling above the
   usual doubled MPS transfer.  Its raw vector dimension is `D^4`.

**Addressable proof.**

<1>1. Kronecker multiplicativity gives
`E^n = sum_w A_w tensor conjugate(A_w)`.

<1>2. For finite matrices, `Tr(X tensor conjugate(X)) = Tr(X) conjugate(Tr(X))`.  Taking the
trace of <1>1 proves item 2.

<1>3. Insert `Gamma` in the trace in <1>1 and use
`Tr((P A_w) tensor conjugate(P A_w)) = |Tr(P A_w)|^2`.  This proves item 3 and agrees with
`def:graded-lattice-tensor`.

<1>4. Vectorisation is the Hilbert-space identification
`End(K) ~= K tensor conjugate(K)`.  Since `dim K=D^2`, `dim End(K)=D^4`.

**Consequence.**  Calling `E` “the bond operator” is harmless shorthand only if the doubled bond is
understood.  CCM's cyclic moment space is a quotient generated by the powers of an operator on
`K`; it does not reconstruct the physical auxiliary space `B`.

### Retained subtraction is not automatically a physical contraction

The raw identities above use all of `E`.  The report's Weil sequence subtracts a trivial
sub-multiset. To turn the retained trace into powers of a restriction, one must specify an
invariant retained space `K_ret` and put `E_ret = E|K_ret`. Taking a union of whole
generalised eigenspaces is a sufficient canonical spectral choice, not a necessary condition:
partial multiplicity selections need an extra invariant-subspace choice (for E=I_2 any line
works). Even then, `K_ret` need not be orthogonal in the native metric. A formal
subtraction of trace modes is not by itself a tensor-network partial trace, nor is a signed graded
divisor a positive Gram system.  This qualification is missing from `HANDOFF.md:184-188`.

## 2. When the Weil matrix is an operator-power Gram matrix

Use the native Hilbert--Schmidt inner product on `L`, linear in the second variable,

    <X,Y>_HS := Tr_K(X^dagger Y).

### F2 (SKETCH): native unitarity gives the exact two-layer contraction

**ASSUME.** `U` is unitary on a finite Hilbert space `K_ret` of dimension `m`.

**PROVE.** For all `j,k >= 0`,

    W_N[j,k] = t_{k-j} = <U^j,U^k>_HS,
    c^dagger W_N c = || sum_j c_j U^j ||_HS^2.

**Proof.** Since `(U^j)^dagger=U^{-j}`,
`<U^j,U^k>_HS=Tr(U^{k-j})`.  The definition of negative moments makes this equal to
`t_{k-j}` for both signs.  Expanding the squared norm gives the second formula.  Diagrammatically,
the bra layer contains `j` copies of `U^dagger`, the ket layer contains `k` copies of `U`, and
unitarity cancels `min(j,k)` pairs, leaving one ring with signed relative winding `k-j`.

This is the natural tensor contraction sought in the handoff.  It is stronger than merely saying
that `W_N` has some abstract spectral Gram factor.

### F3 (SKETCH): similarity unitarity uses a different operator metric

**ASSUME.** `G > 0` on `K_ret` and `U^dagger G U = G`.

**DEFINE.** On `End(K_ret)`,

    <X,Y>_G := Tr(G^{-1} X^dagger G Y).

**PROVE.** This is positive definite and

    W_N[j,k] = <U^j,U^k>_G.

**Addressable proof.**

<1>1. Put `S=G^{1/2}`.  Cyclicity of trace gives

    <X,Y>_G = Tr((S X S^{-1})^dagger (S Y S^{-1})),

so the form is the pullback of the native Hilbert--Schmidt form and is positive definite.

<1>2. The `G`-adjoint is `X^sharp=G^{-1}X^dagger G`; the hypothesis says `U^sharp=U^{-1}`.
Therefore `<U^j,U^k>_G=Tr(U^{k-j})`.  For `n>0`,
`Tr(U^{-n})=Tr((U^n)^sharp)=conjugate(Tr(U^n))`, so this is exactly the stipulated negative-moment
extension, not an additional inverse-moment assumption.

**Metric warning.**  `prop:hp-inner-product-discrete` supplies some `G` exactly when the retained
operator is semisimple on the circle.  It does not say `G=I`, and it does not say that `G` factors
as a metric inherited from the original MPS auxiliary space.  Inserting `G` and `G^{-1}` as seams
in the two-layer network is a different contraction from the native Hilbert--Schmidt contraction.
Equivalently, `S U S^{-1}` is natively unitary, but `U` need not be.

The Pauli/F5 companion example computed by the root lane makes the distinction concrete: its
normalised companion is `G`-unitary with `G=[[1,-1],[-1,5]]`, while its native Hilbert--Schmidt
norm squared is `6`, not its dimension `2`.

### F4 (SKETCH): the exact converse needs all windows

**ASSUME.** A finite square matrix `U` satisfies

    Tr((U^j)^dagger U^k) = t_{k-j}

for every `j,k >= 0`, where `t_n=Tr(U^n)` and `t_{-n}=conjugate(t_n)`.

**PROVE.** `U` is unitary in the native metric.

**Addressable proof.**

<1>1. The displayed equality makes the full two-sided trace sequence positive definite.  By
`thm:weil-positivity-finite` with `r=1`, all eigenvalues lie in the closed unit disc.

<1>2. The infinite Toeplitz matrix has finite rank because it is a Gram matrix of vectors in the
finite-dimensional space `End(K_ret)`.

<1>3. In the Poisson-kernel proof of `thm:weil-positivity-finite`, any eigenvalue strictly inside
the disc contributes a measure with strictly positive density on the circle (the eigenvalue zero
contributes Haar measure).  Its monomials are linearly independent in every degree, giving
infinite Toeplitz rank.  Hence every eigenvalue is on the unit circle.

<1>4. Taking `j=k=n` gives `||U^n||_HS^2=t_0=dim K_ret` for every `n`, so the powers are bounded.
A Jordan block at a unit-modulus eigenvalue would grow polynomially; hence all peripheral blocks
are semisimple.

<1>5. Unitary Schur triangularisation gives an upper triangular `T` with the eigenvalues of `U`
on its diagonal.  The case `n=1` says

    dim K_ret = ||U||_HS^2
              = sum_a |lambda_a|^2 + sum_{a<b}|T_ab|^2.

The first sum already equals `dim K_ret`, so all strict upper entries vanish.  Thus `U` is normal
with unit-modulus spectrum, hence unitary.

**Sharp finite-window counterexample.**  The converse is false for a single fixed window.  Let

    X = [[1,1],[0,0]].

Then `X^2=X`, `Tr X=1`, `||X||_HS^2=2`, and on the window `{0,1}` the operator Gram matrix of
`(I,X)` is `[[2,1],[1,2]]`, exactly the trace Toeplitz matrix.  Nevertheless `X` is not unitary.
Thus `HANDOFF.md:186-187` may say “if unitary”, or “iff for every window”, but not an unqualified
finite-window “iff”.

**Signed-lag warning.**  For complex traces the contraction is `Tr(U^{k-j})`, not
`Tr(U^{|j-k|})`.  For the scalar unitary `U=i`, the two orientations give `i` and `-i`; replacing
the signed lag by an absolute value destroys Hermiticity.  Absolute lags are safe only after a
separate reality/evenness hypothesis.

## 3. Clock-register purification and its exact kernel

### F5 (SKETCH): the Weil matrix is a reduced density matrix, up to the compulsory transpose

**ASSUME.** The native-unitary setting of F2, and define `v_j=vec(U^j) in L`.

**DEFINE.** The unnormalised history state is

    |Omega_N> := sum_{j=0}^N |j>_C tensor |v_j>_L.

**PROVE.**

1. `W_N[j,k]=<v_j,v_k>` is the Gram matrix of the operator powers.
2. The clock marginal of the displayed state is

       rho_C[j,k] = <v_k,v_j> = W_N[k,j],

   so `rho_C=W_N^T=conjugate(W_N)`, not `W_N` in general.
3. Replacing `|v_j>` by its coordinatewise conjugate makes the clock marginal equal to `W_N`.
   In the real-even case `W_N=W_N^T`, so the distinction disappears.
4. `Tr W_N=(N+1)m`; dividing `Omega_N` by `sqrt((N+1)m)` gives a normalised state.
5. For a coefficient column `c`,

       c in ker W_N  iff  sum_j c_j U^j = 0 in End(K_ret).

   The kernel of the literal clock marginal `W_N^T` is `conjugate(ker W_N)`.

**Proof.** Items 1--4 are the definition of partial trace with the convention that the inner
product is linear in the second variable.  For item 5,
`c^dagger W_N c=||sum_j c_j U^j||_HS^2`; a positive Gram matrix kills `c` exactly when this norm is
zero.

This is the precise “zero bond-operator superposition” interpretation.  It is a relation among
operators on the doubled transfer space; it is not a vanishing physical MPS word superposition.

### F6 (SKETCH): an exact unary-clock MPS automaton with bond independent of window length

Suppose `U` is semisimple with `q` distinct eigenvalues `z_a` and spectral idempotents `P_a`, so
`U^j=sum_a z_a^j P_a`.  Encode `j` by the unary word `1^j 0^{N-j}` on `N` clock qubits.  On the
virtual space `C^q direct-sum C^q`, put

    Z = diag(z_1,...,z_q),
    M_1 = [[Z,0],[0,0]],        M_0 = [[0,I],[0,I]].

Start with the row `(1,...,1; 0,...,0)`.  At the right boundary map either copy of basis vector
`e_a` to the physical endpoint vector `|P_a>>`.

**PROVE.**

<1>1. `M_0 M_1=0`, so every clock word containing `01` has zero amplitude.

<1>2. For `j<N`, the word `1^j0^{N-j}` reaches the second copy with coefficient vector
`(z_1^j,...,z_q^j)`; for `j=N` it remains in the first copy with that vector.

<1>3. The endpoint map produces `sum_a z_a^j|P_a>>=|U^j>>`.  Therefore the network is exactly

    sum_{j=0}^N |1^j0^{N-j}> tensor |U^j>>.

Its MPS bond is at most `2q <= 2 dim K = 2D^2`, independent of `N`; the endpoint physical space is
`End(K)`, of dimension `D^4`.  If the clock is kept as one `(N+1)`-level site, the bipartite Schmidt
rank is exactly `rank span{I,U,...,U^N}=min(N+1,q)` in this semisimple setting.  The automaton is a
short exact tensor-network description, not evidence that the original physical MPS has this
clock as a local degree of freedom.

## 4. Moment GNS, kernel polynomial, and exact reconstruction

### F7 (SKETCH): the cyclic moment space is a unitary dilation in the disc case

By `thm:weil-positivity-finite`, if the normalised retained eigenvalues `z_a=lambda_a/r` lie in the
closed disc with algebraic multiplicities `m_a`, the moments have the positive representing
measure

    mu = sum_{|z_a|=1} m_a delta_{z_a}
       + sum_{|z_a|<1} m_a P_{z_a}(theta) dtheta/(2 pi),

where `P_z(theta)=(1-|z|^2)/|e^{i theta}-z|^2` (and `P_0=1`).  Then

    H_mom = closure of C[z,z^{-1}] in L^2(mu),
    U_mom f(omega)=omega f(omega),       Omega_mom=1,
    t_n=<Omega_mom,U_mom^n Omega_mom>.

`U_mom` is unitary for every positive moment sequence, even when the original retained modes lie
strictly inside the disc.  In that case it is a unitary dilation of the moments, usually
infinite-dimensional; its circle spectrum must not be identified with the eigenvalues of `E/r`.
Reflection duality is what removes the Poisson parts and makes the measure finite atomic.

### F8 (SKETCH): critical atomic reconstruction and the three window regimes

**ASSUME.** All retained normalised modes lie on the unit circle.  Let
`{z_1,...,z_q}` be the **distinct** support and let `m_a>0` be their algebraic multiplicities.

**PROVE.**

1. The Weil matrix factorises as

       W_N = V_N diag(m_1,...,m_q) V_N^dagger,
       V_N[j,a] = conjugate(z_a)^j

   (equivalently use the conjugate Vandermonde convention), and

       c^dagger W_N c = sum_a m_a |p_c(z_a)|^2.

2. `rank W_N=min(N+1,q)` and

       ker W_N = {c : p_c(z_a)=0 for every a}.

3. Put `q_sf(z)=product_a(z-z_a)`.  At the critical window `N=q`, `ker W_q` is one-dimensional
   and its polynomial is a scalar multiple of `q_sf`.
4. For `N<q`, `W_N` is positive definite.  Its minimal eigenvector minimises the positive
   least-squares expression in item 1 but annihilates no exact operator in general.
5. For `N>q`,

       ker W_N = {q_sf(z) s(z) : deg s <= N-q}.

   An arbitrary nullvector has all true support points plus arbitrary extra roots.  The gcd of the
   null polynomials, or the intersection of their zero sets, recovers the support.

**Addressable proof.**

<1>1. Since `t_n=sum_a m_a z_a^n` and `conjugate(z_a)=z_a^{-1}`, substitute into
`W_N[j,k]=t_{k-j}` to obtain the factorisation and quadratic form.

<1>2. Evaluation at distinct nonzero nodes is a Vandermonde map, of rank `min(N+1,q)`.  The
positive diagonal weights do not change its rank or kernel.

<1>3. At `N=q`, a nonzero polynomial of degree at most `q` vanishing at the `q` distinct nodes is
a scalar multiple of their monic product.

<1>4. If `N<q`, the Vandermonde map has full row rank, so the matrix is positive definite; a
strictly positive minimum is not an annihilation relation.

<1>5. Polynomial division by `q_sf` proves the over-resolved formula.  This is also exactly the
warning in `refs/src/2511.23257/Araki-final-oct25.tex:908-909`.

**Minimal-eigenvector correction.**  In the under-resolved regime, subtracting the smallest
eigenvalue, `W_N-epsilon_min I`, creates a new singular positive Toeplitz matrix and hence a new
Caratheodory measure. If its radical is one-dimensional, its kernel polynomial reconstructs
that fitted support, not the original transfer spectrum. A degenerate minimum requires the
common-zero/gcd prescription and permits arbitrary extra roots in an individual nullvector.
For example three unit cube roots with N=1 give W=3I and shifted matrix zero, so a chosen
nullvector can have its polynomial root at 2. Exact transfer reconstruction begins only when
the original `epsilon_min=0`, with the window qualifications of F8.

### F9 (SKETCH): what the cyclic quotient reconstructs

In the atomic circle case define

    A_mom := C[z]/(q_sf),
    <[p],[q]>_mom := sum_a m_a conjugate(p(z_a)) q(z_a).

Multiplication by `[z]` is unitary, `[1]` is cyclic, and `dim A_mom=q`.  This is the finite moment
GNS representation.

The quotient has one line for each **distinct** spectral point.  Multiplicity `m_a` is stored as a
weight in the metric/cyclic state, not as `m_a` copies of the eigenspace.  Once the nodes are known,
the exact moments solve the full-column-rank Vandermonde system

    t_n = sum_a m_a z_a^n,       n=0,...,q,

so the weights, and hence algebraic multiplicities when they are known a priori to be positive
integers, are uniquely recovered.  Thus:

- the GNS operator itself reconstructs the distinct support once;
- support plus recovered weights reconstructs the characteristic polynomial and the similarity
  class of the **semisimple** operator `direct-sum_a z_a I_{m_a}`;
- neither object reconstructs Jordan block sizes or eigenvectors in the native bond metric.

### F10 (SKETCH): the null polynomial is square-free, not generally the minimal polynomial

For an arbitrary finite matrix with distinct eigenvalues `z_a`, the actual minimal polynomial is

    m_U(z) = product_a (z-z_a)^{s_a},

where `s_a` is the largest Jordan-block size at `z_a`.  The trace moments are always
`t_n=sum_a m_a z_a^n`; nilpotent Jordan parts have zero trace.  Therefore the critical Toeplitz
kernel returns `q_sf`, and

    q_sf(U)=0  iff  U is semisimple.

**Sharp counterexample.**  For `U=[[1,1],[0,1]]`, `t_n=2` for every integer moment defined by
conjugate extension.  Every `W_N` is `2` times the all-ones matrix.  Its degree-one null polynomial
is `z-1`, but `U-I != 0`; the minimal polynomial is `(z-1)^2`.  The GNS space is one-dimensional
although the transfer space is two-dimensional.  This is `prop:weil-blind-jordan` seen directly in
the kernel construction.

Hence the handoff sentence “the kernel vector is the minimal polynomial of `E`” survives only as:

> At the critical window, the kernel vector is the square-free polynomial of the distinct retained
> support.  It is the minimal polynomial of the retained operator exactly under semisimplicity.

It reconstructs `E` itself on the cyclic GNS module only when the retained semisimple operator has
simple spectrum (equivalently, is cyclic).  With repeated eigenvalues the weights remember the
multiplicities, but the cyclic quotient still has only one line per point.

## 5. Grading is a signed moment problem, not automatically a Gram matrix

### F11 (SKETCH): exact graded criterion on the circle

Let `E=E_0 direct-sum E_1` preserve the doubled parity, and suppose both retained sector spectra
are on the critical circle.  At a distinct point `z_a`, write
`d_a=m_0(z_a)-m_1(z_a)`.  The supertrace Toeplitz form is

    c^dagger W_str c = sum_a d_a |p_c(z_a)|^2.

Therefore the full moment sequence is positive definite iff `d_a>=0` at every distinct point;
the necessity is already visible in any window with `N>=q-1`, where `q` is the size of the union
of the two sector supports.  On a smaller fixed window, nonnegative net weights remain sufficient
but a negative weight can be hidden by truncation.  If the arithmetic sign convention makes zeros
positive, replace the supertrace by its negative or explicitly move the chosen even divisor to the
other side; then the required net weights change sign.  What cannot be done is to infer positivity
merely because the odd block is unitary.

**Proof.** Apply the Vandermonde calculation of F8 separately to the two sectors and subtract.
Polynomial interpolation at the distinct support isolates each net weight, proving necessity as
well as sufficiency.

**Sharp tensor counterexample.**  Take `B=C^{1|1}`, parity `P=diag(1,-1)`, and the singleton even
letter `A=P`.  Its doubled transfer is `E=P tensor conjugate(P)`, equivalently `Ad(P)` on
`End(B)`: the two-dimensional even (population) sector has eigenvalue `+1`, and the two-dimensional
odd (coherence) sector has eigenvalue `-1`.  Both blocks are natively unitary and every
positive-length `P`-closed ring norm is nonnegative:
`Str(E^n)=2-2(-1)^n`, equal to `4` for odd `n` and `0` for even `n`.  But `t_0=0` and

    W_1 = [[0,4],[4,0]]

has eigenvalues `+4,-4`.  Termwise nonnegative ring norms do not imply Toeplitz positivity.

The net divisor also loses sector data: `(m_0,m_1)=(2,1)` and `(1,0)` have the same weight `d=1`.
CCM applied to supertraces can reconstruct only the net graded divisor unless the two sector traces
are supplied separately.  This refutes the handoff's proposed implication in lines 193-196.

## 6. Relation to the physical MPS block map and parent nullspaces

For a fixed physical length `ell`, the standard MPS block map is

    Gamma_ell : End(B) -> (C^{|S|})^{tensor ell},
    Gamma_ell(X) = sum_{w in S^ell} Tr(X A_w) |w>.

The support of the block reduced state is generated by `im Gamma_ell`; a standard local parent
term projects onto `(im Gamma_ell)^perp` (up to the usual boundary and injectivity qualifications).
This must be kept separate from the length-polynomial map

    Phi_N : C^{N+1} -> End(K_ret),       Phi_N(c)=sum_j c_j U^j,

whose Gram matrix is `W_N` and whose kernel is the CCM nullspace.

The two maps have different domains, codomains, and meanings:

| object | varies | target | nullspace means |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Gamma_ell` | boundary matrix at one fixed physical length | physical word Hilbert space | redundant boundary data, not the physical parent space |
| `Phi_N` | coefficients across different lengths | operators on the doubled transfer | a polynomial identity among transfer powers |

The physical parent space is `ker Gamma_ell^dagger=(im Gamma_ell)^perp`, not `ker Gamma_ell`.
For labelled tensors `(A_0,A_1)=(1,0)` on `B=C`, the latter kernel is zero while the one-site
parent space is `span{|1>}`.
Thus a projector onto `ker W_N` is a global clock constraint for the conjugated-feature
transfer-history state. For the un-conjugated history displayed in F5 it is instead the
projector onto `conjugate(ker W_N)`. It is
not, without an additional construction, a local frustration-free Hamiltonian for the original
physical MPS.  The root lane is treating the Hamiltonian interpretation separately; the safe
finite conclusion here is only the exact Gram/nullspace identity F5.  In particular,
`p(U)^dagger p(U)=0` at an exact annihilator says `p(U)=0`; it does not manufacture a nontrivial
physical parent Hamiltonian.

## 7. Ring norms do not recover physical letters

### F12 (SKETCH): non-identifiability occurs before the CCM quotient

Even the full transfer matrix `E` does not canonically determine a Kraus/letter list: Kraus
families admit physical-index unitary mixing and nonminimal enlargements.  Trace powers contain
strictly less information than `E`: they determine algebraic eigenvalues (given sufficient exact
moments and dimension), but not eigenvectors or Jordan structure.

The smallest counterexample fixes a labelled two-letter physical alphabet and a one-dimensional
auxiliary space.  The tensors

    (A_0,A_1)=(1,0),                 (A'_0,A'_1)=(1/sqrt(2),1/sqrt(2))

both have scalar doubled transfer `E=1`, so every ring norm and every CCM moment is `1`.  Their
labelled physical product states are respectively `|0>^{tensor n}` and
`((|0>+|1>)/sqrt(2))^{tensor n}`.  Hence ring norms do not recover basis-resolved physical letters
or physical amplitudes.  If physical-basis rotations are quotiented out, nonminimal Kraus
enlargement still changes the alphabet while preserving `E`; and trace moments still fail to
recover `E` itself.

The Jordan example in F10 gives a second obstruction at fixed matrix size: matrices with the same
trace moments can have different nilpotent parts.  Consequently “counts -> bond generator” is an
inverse spectral result only after one explicitly declares the quotient: distinct retained
support, optionally its positive weights, and semisimple similarity class.  It is not recovery of
the physical MPS tensor.

### Definition proposals for `02i_definitions_ccm_tensor.tex`

**`def:ccm-tn-space-ladder` (exact proposed text).**

> For a lattice MPS tensor `(A_s)` on an auxiliary space `B`, the doubled transfer space is
> `K=B tensor conjugate(B) ~= End(B)` and the doubled transfer is
> `E=sum_s A_s tensor conjugate(A_s) in End(K)`.  The transfer-operator Hilbert space is
> `L=End(K)` with native Hilbert--Schmidt form `Tr(X^dagger Y)`.  Given a positive-definite trace
> moment sequence, its cyclic moment/GNS space is the completion of Laurent polynomials modulo the
> radical of the moment form.  These four spaces—`B`, `K`, `L`, and the moment/GNS space—are distinct.

**`def:ccm-tn-clock-gram` (exact proposed text).**

> For moments `t_n` with `t_{-n}=conjugate(t_n)`, the order-`N` Weil matrix is
> `W_N=(t_{k-j})_{0<=j,k<=N}` and the length-polynomial map of an operator `U` is
> `Phi_N(c)=sum_{j=0}^N c_j U^j`.  When `W_N[j,k]=Tr((U^j)^dagger U^k)`, `W_N=Phi_N^dagger Phi_N`
> in the native operator metric and `ker W_N=ker Phi_N`.  For
> `Omega_N=sum_j |j> tensor |U^j>>`, the clock marginal is `W_N^T`; conjugating the feature vectors
> gives clock marginal `W_N`.

**`def:ccm-tn-trace-semisimplification` (exact proposed text).**

> For a finite retained spectrum with distinct normalised points `z_a` and algebraic
> multiplicities `m_a`, its trace semisimplification is the weighted commutative cyclic model
> `C[z]/(product_a(z-z_a))` with inner product
> `sum_a m_a conjugate(p(z_a))q(z_a)` and multiplication by `z`.  It records each distinct point
> once and records algebraic multiplicity in the cyclic metric; it records no Jordan block sizes.

**`prop:ccm-tn-operator-gram`**

> Let `U` act on a finite Hilbert space.  If `U` is unitary in the native metric, its trace Weil
> matrix satisfies `W_N[j,k]=Tr((U^j)^dagger U^k)` for every `N`; if
> `U^dagger G U=G` with `G>0`, the same identity holds in the operator metric
> `Tr(G^{-1}X^dagger GY)`.  Conversely, native Gram equality for every pair of nonnegative powers
> forces native unitarity, but equality on one fixed finite window does not.

Dependencies: `thm:weil-positivity-finite; prop:hp-inner-product-discrete`.

**`prop:ccm-tn-clock-state`**

> In the operator-Gram setting, vectorised transfer powers form a history state whose clock
> marginal is the transpose of the Weil matrix, and the Weil kernel consists exactly of coefficient
> vectors whose polynomial gives a zero operator superposition.  If the transfer has `q` distinct
> semisimple modes, the unary-clock history state has an exact MPS automaton of bond at most `2q`,
> independent of the clock length.

Dependencies: `prop:ccm-tn-operator-gram`.

**`thm:ccm-tn-atomic-reconstruction`**

> For a positive finite trace divisor on the critical circle with `q` distinct support points, the
> order-`N` Weil matrix has rank `min(N+1,q)` and kernel the polynomials of degree at most `N`
> vanishing on the support.  At `N=q` its kernel polynomial is the square-free support polynomial;
> at `N>q` arbitrary nullvectors have additional arbitrary roots, and at `N<q` the minimal
> eigenvector is a fit rather than an annihilator.  Vandermonde inversion recovers the positive
> weights separately.

Dependencies: `thm:weil-duality-pairing; cit:ccm-cf-vandermonde` (or the existing source row for
`refs/src/2511.23257/Araki-final-oct25.tex:760-800`).

**`prop:ccm-tn-trace-semisimplification`**

> The critical Weil kernel annihilates the trace semisimplification.  Its square-free support
> polynomial annihilates the original retained operator iff that operator is semisimple; trace
> moments and the cyclic GNS quotient do not recover Jordan blocks.  With recovered positive
> integer weights they determine only the semisimple similarity class, not the native metric or
> physical MPS letters.

Dependencies: `thm:ccm-tn-atomic-reconstruction; prop:weil-blind-jordan`.

**`prop:ccm-tn-graded-net-criterion`**

> For circle-supported even and odd sector traces, the full supertrace moment sequence is positive
> definite iff the net multiplicity `m_0(z)-m_1(z)` is nonnegative at every distinct support point;
> a window of degree at least one less than the union-support size already detects the criterion
> (with the inequality reversed after a global sign reversal).  Sector unitarity and termwise
> nonnegative supertrace ring counts do not imply this Toeplitz positivity, and the net moments do
> not determine the two sector multiplicities separately.

Dependencies: `thm:ccm-tn-atomic-reconstruction; def:graded-transfer-channel`.

**`obs:ccm-tn-no-letter-reconstruction`**

> Ring norms and their CCM moment quotient do not determine basis-resolved physical MPS letters,
> a Kraus presentation, the native bond metric, or Jordan data.  They determine at most retained
> spectral support and, under positivity with exact moments, its net/algebraic weights.

Dependencies: `prop:ccm-tn-trace-semisimplification; prop:ccm-tn-graded-net-criterion`.
