                                                      Abstract
              We prove that the Riemann
                                      √       zeta function, all Dirichlet L-functions, and all finite-order
          Hecke L-functions over Q( −3) are zero-free in Re s > 47/48. Our proof combines cubic theta
          reflection, a quadratic large sieve, and Hecke zero-density estimates with Mellin continuation.


1        Introduction
The Quasi-Riemann Hypothesis asserts that there exists a constant c > 0 such that every nontrivial
zero ρ of the Riemann zeta function satisfies Re ρ ≤ 1 − c. Here a nontrivial zero means a zero
in 0 < Re s < 1. We give an argument with √ c = 1/48 that also treats Dirichlet L-functions and
finite-order Hecke L-functions over F = Q( −3).
Theorem 1. The Riemann zeta function has no zeros in Re s > 47/48. More generally, every
finite-order Hecke L-function over F , and every Dirichlet L-function, is zero-free in this half-plane,
whether its character is primitive or imprimitive. For a principal character, the L-function has its
usual simple pole at s = 1; this pole is allowed in the assertion.
    The boundary Re s = 47/48 is not included in the zero-free assertion. Throughout, L-functions
are uncompleted, with their usual meromorphic continuations; a zero is a point where the function
is holomorphic and vanishes.
    The supplied manuscript [1] states the stronger zero-free half-plane Re s > 7/8. We adapt
its probe and Mellin-continuation framework, in particular the constructions in its Sections 4, 6,
and 7, to give a shorter argument for the weaker half-plane in Theorem 1. The inputs are the
unconditional cubic theta formulas in [2, Section 5 and Appendix A], the quadratic Hecke large sieve
in [3, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2], and the general Hecke zero-density estimate in [4, Lemma 3.3].
The conditional prime-sum and dispersion estimates in [2] are not used. Appendices A and B give
the arithmetic and reflection calculations for the probe used here.
    Poisson summation introduces finite-order twists over F , so we work with one zero supremum
for this family. The norm pullback transfers the result to Dirichlet functions in Subsection 5.2.
    Write LF (s, η) for the Hecke L-function of a character η over F , and let PF be the set of
primitive finite-order Hecke characters over F . Define the family zero supremum
