## 0. Centeleghe–Stix and the dictionary

**PROVED (quoted theorem, precise mathematical statement).** Let p be prime and let AV_p^com be the full subcategory of abelian varieties over F_p whose simple isogeny factors avoid the conjugacy class of √p. There exists a contravariant functor T inducing an anti-equivalence with pairs (T,F) such that T is finite free over Z, F⊗Q is semisimple with non-real Weil p-number eigenvalues, and there exists V∈End_Z(T) with FV=p. Its rank is 2 dim A and Frobenius acts as F. Morphisms of pairs commute with F. In particular, characteristic-polynomial root conditions alone are insufficient: semisimplicity and integral V are required. V is unique and also satisfies VF=p.

Source: T. G. Centeleghe and J. Stix, *Categories of abelian varieties over finite fields I: Abelian varieties over F_p*, Algebra & Number Theory **9** (2015), 225–265, Theorem 1 ([author preprint](https://www.math.uni-frankfurt.de/~stix/research/preprints/CENTELEGHE-STIX-AVFp-part1-20150110.pdf)). The exact real-root clause is: “F ⊗ Q is semisimple, and its eigenvalues are non-real Weil p-numbers.” The excluded conjugacy class has minimal polynomial x²−p over Q; x±√p are not separate integral irreducible factors.

**SHARPENED (choices).** The anti-equivalence depends on a compatible ind-system. A fixed choice gives an isomorphism class, not an intrinsically canonical variety independent of that choice. The covariant dual variant can be normalized to extend Deligne on ordinary objects; this normalization is used below whenever applying Howe. Centeleghe–Stix §§7.3–7.4 describe the invertible-module ambiguity. Nonordinary polarization data do not follow from a bare alternating matrix under an unspecified equivalence.

**PROVED (graph hypotheses).** For A=A_s symmetric integral and |λ|<2√p, each real eigenspace of A gives a semisimple block with distinct nonreal roots of x²−λx+p. Therefore F=[[A,−pI],[I,0]] is semisimple. Here V=[[0,pI],[−I,A]] is integral, FV=VF=pI, F+V=diag(A,A), and FᵀΩF=pΩ for Ω=[[0,I],[−I,0]]. This meets the theorem without ordinariness or squarefreeness. The factors x²+p (the supersingular elliptic blocks of the examples) are allowed; x²−p is excluded. No assertion of an ordinary canonical lift is involved.

**PROVED (trivial eigenvalues).** On a connected d-regular graph, +d occurs exactly when the signing is balanced (product +1 around every cycle), and −d occurs exactly when it is antibalanced (cycle product (−1)^length). This follows from equality in the adjacency norm bound: along every edge the extremal eigenvector has constant absolute value and compatible signs. On a bipartite graph these two conditions coincide. A nontrivial flux removes +d, but can retain −d on a nonbipartite graph. Strict Ramanujan excludes both, and also excludes the band endpoints.

**PROVED (which zeta).** P(u)=det(I−uF)=det(I−uA+pu²) is the L-polynomial of H¹ of the associated abelian variety. The curve candidate is P(u)/((1−u)(1−pu)); it is not automatically a curve zeta. The full super-Ihara zeta in `graph-super.md` is a ratio of signed and unsigned numerators, not P alone. The formal curve counts are p^k+1−Tr(F^k); periodic-syndrome counts are det(I−F^k), the point counts of the abelian variety. These agree only in dimension one in general.

**PROVED (convention audit).** The present F is integrally symplectically conjugate to the old notebook step F_old=[[0,−I],[pI,A]] by S=[[0,I],[−I,0]]: F=S F_old S^−1. Thus the unit-twist tests transport without changing Ω. In the present convention Ω(F−V)=[[2I,−A],[−A,2pI]], positive definite exactly in the strict band.


## 1. J1: existence versus a unit in the stated order

**REFUTED as stated, already by K₄.** Existence follows from §0, but the principal repair recorded in `howe-positivity.md` is **not in Z[F,V]**. The distinction is between the order generated by Frobenius and Verschiebung, the full integral central order, and the entire endomorphism ring. A choice of categorical duality and positivity is also needed in nonordinary cases. The per-orbit certificates and the three remaining nonordinary cases are below.

### 1.1 The criteria, with their actual hypotheses

**PROVED (Howe, ordinary case).** Fix Deligne's embedding ε and its CM type Φ_ε={φ:v_p(φ(F))>0}. An integral alternating nondegenerate form B on T defines a polarization precisely when B(Fx,y)=B(x,Vy) and B(x,ιy) is symmetric positive definite for a (equivalently any) purely imaginary ι∈Q[F] with Im φ(ι)>0 for all φ∈Φ_ε. It is principal precisely when B is unimodular. “Totally imaginary” describes ι; positive definiteness describes the real bilinear form, not a totally imaginary matrix. This includes ordinary modules with multiplicities. Source: E. W. Howe, *Principally polarized ordinary abelian varieties over finite fields*, Transactions of the American Mathematical Society **347** (1995), 2361–2401; the precise bilinear formulation is transcribed in Goresky–Tai, `refs/src/1701.07742/main.tex:3474–3497,3556–3590`.

**PROVED (Bergström–Karemaker–Marseglia, scoped statement).** Fix a squarefree non-real Weil p-polynomial h, CM algebra L and CM type Φ. Suppose the isogeny class has an A₀ with an endomorphism-preserving canonical CM lifting of type Φ and endomorphism order S that is Gorenstein and conjugation-stable. There is a totally real unit α∈S× such that an isogeny μ:B→B∨ is a polarization iff γ=α⁻¹G(μ) obeys bar(γ)=−γ and Im φ(γ)>0 for each φ∈Φ. Its degree is the ideal-module index [G(B):G(μ)G(B∨)]. Source: J. Bergström, V. Karemaker and S. Marseglia, *Polarizations of abelian varieties over finite fields via canonical liftings*, International Mathematics Research Notices **2023**(4), 3194–3248, Theorem A / 4.10 ([primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/2101.05531v3#S4.Thmtheorem10)). The unit α is a comparison datum; it is not an assertion that any unimodular graph Ω can be repaired by a unit in Z[F,V]. None of our nonordinary characteristic polynomials is squarefree, so this theorem cannot be applied directly to them.

S. Marseglia, *Computing abelian varieties over finite fields isogenous to a power*, Research in Number Theory **5** (2019), article 35, extends module computations to powers; its polarization criterion in §5 is explicitly for the **ordinary** case ([paper](https://d-nb.info/1206168595/34)). Replacing the nonordinary problem by Howe's p-adic nonunit rule is invalid: both roots of a slope-½ pair are nonunits.

### 1.2 The small order and its obstructions

**PROVED.** Put R₀=Z[F,V], C=F+V=diag(A,A). Every element of R₀ has a unique expression a(C)+F b(C), where a,b∈Z[t] reduced modulo the monic minimal polynomial m_A. Existence follows from F²−CF+p=0. Uniqueness follows over Q by looking at the two different Frobenius roots over each real λ. The Ω-adjoint sends F to V and fixes C; hence its fixed subring is exactly Z[C]. Consequently, if uΩ is alternating for a central u∈R₀, then u=f(C) with f integral. Being unimodular is equivalent to f(A)∈GL_n(Z). This proves the proposed smaller real-subring condition is forced, not an extra optional pattern.

For ordinary paired modes ±λ, arithmetic signs must be opposite: μ and −μ have equal p-adic valuations and opposite imaginary parts. Thus Ω and −Ω fail whenever such a pair occurs. This proves their failure for K₄ and every ordinary orbit of the cube and K₅; in mixed cases it gives a necessary condition on ordinary subvarieties after the normalization of §0.

**PROVED (five exact obstruction certificates).**

1. **K₄ pattern ±1,±√5.** A central unit has f(±1)=±1, opposite, and f(√5)=a+b√5 of norm −1. The first condition implies a≡0 mod4: write the even part of f as Σ c_j t^(2j), use Σc_j=0 and 5≡1 mod4. But a²−5b²=−1 is then impossible mod16. This applies also to cube mask 5 and Petersen mask 6, which contain these ordinary modes.
2. **Rational modes ±1,±2.** A unit takes values ±1 at all four. Opposite values at ±1 force f(2)=f(−1) and f(−2)=f(1), by congruence mod3. Yet f(2)≡f(−2) mod4 forces equality, a contradiction. Only the ±1 sign condition uses ordinariness. This applies to Petersen masks 1,10 and K₃,₃ mask 6.
3. **Gluing 0 to ±√5.** Write f(√5)=a+b√5, with a≡f(0)=±1 mod5. A unit would have norm ±1; opposite signs on the ordinary √5 modes require norm −1, whereas the norm is 1 mod5. This excludes Petersen mask 22 and K₅ mask 13.
4. **Modes ±√3.** The maximal real order is Z[√3], and norm −1 requires a²−3b²=−1, impossible mod3. This excludes cube masks 3 and 23, even if the real central order is enlarged.
5. **K₅ ordinary orbits.** For θ²−θ−8=0, the maximal order is Z[(1+√33)/2]. Its units are ±ε^k, ε=23+4√33=19+8θ. Norm −1 is impossible mod3; half-integral candidates for norm +1 are impossible mod8, and the least integral Pell solution is (23,4), so ε generates the positive units. Normalize f(1)=1, f(−1)=−1. Evaluating the quadratic relation at 1 and −1 yields f(θ)|_(θ=1)≡1 mod8 and f(θ)|_(θ=−1)≡−1 mod6. But ε has residues 3 mod8 and −1 mod6; the first condition forces the positive sign and even k, contradicting the second. The mirrored orbit follows by t↦−t.

`checks/jac_howe.py` verifies all the integer congruences and the finite Pell minimality certificate; these are proofs of nonexistence, not bounded failed searches.

### 3.1 K₄: a trigonal Jacobian isogeny class

### 3.5 Why resultant one cannot be the obstruction for a connected signed graph

**PROVED (general).** Let A be the symmetric signed adjacency matrix of a connected graph. Its real Weil polynomial is h(t)=det(tI−A). There cannot be a factorization h=h₁h₂ with both h_i nonconstant in Z[t] and Res(h₁,h₂)=±1.

Indeed, the resultant condition supplies integral Bezout polynomials, hence a polynomial e∈Z[t] equal to 1 mod h₁ and 0 mod h₂. Then E=e(A) is an integral symmetric idempotent, neither0 norI. As a real orthogonal projector, 0≤E_ii≤1, so each diagonal entry is0 or1. From E_ii=Σ_j E_ij² it follows that E has no off-diagonal entries. Thus E is the coordinate projector onto a nonempty proper vertex subset. Since EA=AE, no graph edge crosses that subset, contradicting connectedness. The same proof uses the squarefree minimal polynomial if desired. Therefore the elementary resultant-one criterion is structurally unavailable here; stronger gluing obstructions might still apply.

Reference for its arithmetic role: E. W. Howe and K. E. Lauter, *Improved upper bounds for the number of points on curves over finite fields*, Annales de l'Institut Fourier **53** (2003), 1677–1737 ([primary journal](https://www.numdam.org/item/?id=AIF_2003__53_6_1677_0)); their *New methods for bounding the number of points on curves over finite fields*, in *Geometry and Arithmetic*, EMS (2012), develops gluing exponents and reduced resultants ([author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.6308)). The underlying split-polarization obstruction is usually credited to Serre. A citation to “resultant1” must specify a complete partition, not merely two factors with resultant1: the other factors can mediate gluing.

**PROVED (cycle-sign obstructions).** Let S_ℓ be the sum of sign products over unoriented simple ℓ-cycles. For a simple (p+1)-regular graph,

Tr A⁴=n(p+1)(2p+1)+8S₄,

N₄=p⁴+1+n(p−1)−8S₄,

a₄=(p⁴−p²)/4−2S₄.

For cubic graphs this is a₄=3−2S₄. The cube's six square faces have respectively two, four, or six negative cycle products. Two negatives give S₄=2 and a₄=−1 (masks1,9). Four negatives give S₄=−2 and a₄=7 (masks3,5, the count-positive orbits). Six negatives give A²=3I in the remaining orbit, so N₆=−23; all these assertions are checked directly in `jac_verify.py`.

Petersen has no triangles or squares. Thus Tr F⁵=10S₅ and a₅=6−2S₅. Its12 pentagons have four, six, eight, six negative signs for masks1,6,10,22. The first has S₅=4 and a₅=−2. These are intrinsic cycle products, invariant under switching. A negative-edge count for an arbitrary representative is not invariant, and the number of negative fundamental cycles changes with the chosen basis; the full H¹(Y,F₂) class, modulo graph automorphisms, is the appropriate datum.

**SHARPENED (what does not distinguish curves).** The matching-polynomial average depends only on the underlying graph, not its chosen flux, so it cannot distinguish the cube's passing and failing fluxes. Ramanujan bounds and an invariant Gaussian vacuum likewise do not imply curve counts. Principal polarizability is insufficient: cube masks1,9 have a verified principal unit twist for an appropriate ordinary embedding and nevertheless have a₄<0. Conversely K₄ and several K₅ fluxes have no repair in the stipulated small ring and still have Jacobian isogeny classes. Supersingularity is not the dividing line, as the K₅ witnesses show.

The integer-kernel bases used here are saturated: they are columns of a unimodular Smith transformation, not denominator-cleared rational nullspaces.

**SHARPENED (reading).** The robust line is: **a strict Ramanujan flux over a prime field supplies an abelian-variety module; some of its Weil polynomials are curve L-polynomials and some provably are not.** A graph is not literally a curve, and the full super-Ihara ratio is not its curve zeta. “A polarized abelian variety that is never a Jacobian” is false for the isogeny-class question and also prematurely asserts a transported principal polarization in nonordinary cases. Counts fix the isogeny class. The integral module, the chosen equivalence, duality, principal polarization, and Schottky/Torelli realization are successive additional questions.
