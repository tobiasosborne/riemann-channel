## 1. T1, the dual lattice, phase convention and vertex towers

**Claims restated.** For the stated ordering of Weyl operators the symmetric phase is positive, with cocycle `psi(omega/2)`. A rank-two lattice of determinant valuation k has symplectic dual `(det g)^(-1)L`; the minimal isotropic representative of its homothety class has k=0 or 1. Its code dimension is `p^k`. Scaling that representative by `p^m` produces a nested tower of dimensions `p^(k+2m)`, not a unitary identification of all levels.

**PROVED (Lemmas 1.1–1.2; C1).** Direct multiplication gives

`W(u,v)W(u',v') = psi(uv') W(u+u',v+v')`.

Multiplying by `psi(s uv)` changes this exponent to `uv' - s(uv'+u'v)`. At s=1/2 this is `(uv'-u'v)/2`, with the **positive** sign for the stipulated omega. Its restriction is trivial on an isotropic Z_p lattice because 2 is a unit. At s=-1/2, take the self-dual lattice generated by `(1,1/p)` and `(0,1)`: squaring the first generator produces the nontrivial extra scalar `psi(2/p)`. Thus the brief's sign is wrong. Technically the negative convention still defines a *cocycle* (a coboundary change); the phrase “not a cocycle on L” in Lemma 1.2 should read “not the trivial cocycle on L.” The correction itself is correct. `independent_mub_trace.py` checks both the square and a nonzero symplectic pairing, so it tests the cocycle sign, not only commutativity.

**PROVED (Propositions 1.3–1.5).** Since `g^T J g=(det g)J`, the condition `omega(x,gZ_p^2) subset Z_p` is equivalent to `(det g)g^(-1)x in Z_p^2`. Hence the asserted dual formula. If k>=0, the genuine index is `p^(2k)`; if k<0 the reverse index is `p^(-2k)` (there is no negative group index). Homothety shifts k by `2v(c)`, and a unit scalar fixes a Z_p lattice as a subset. Thus `L0=p^(-floor(k/2))L` is unique as a lattice, not just as a class. An index-p inclusion changes k by one. For k<0 the nontrivial central commutator excludes a common eigenvector. This verifies the colour claim including its hidden choice of representative.

In standard coordinates, the stabilisers of `p^a Z_p + p^b Z_p` impose period `p^a Z_p` and support `p^(-b)Z_p`, giving exactly `p^(a+b)` basis cosets. For a general symplectic basis this description is transported by a metaplectic implementer; it is not literally the same support condition in the original x coordinate. Smith decomposition is a statement about lattices (right multiplication by `GL_2(Z_p)` is harmless). A Weil implementer is only determined up to phase; no global splitting of the local metaplectic cover over all of `SL_2(Q_p)` is needed for the dimension argument.

**PROVED with scope clarification (C2).** `pL subset L` gives `C_L subset C_(pL)` because the stabiliser representation is genuinely linear on L; dimensions grow by `p^2`, so the inclusion is strict. Conjugation by `D_c` induces `diag(c^(-1),c)`, not scalar multiplication by c. A homothety with `c^2 != 1` cannot preserve all Weyl commutators: varying the symplectic pairing over Q_p forces its multiplier to be exactly one, not merely a unit. The harmless homotheties c=±1 are exceptions to “no Clifford implements a homothety.” Arbitrary isotropic lattices have Clifford-equivalent codes iff their **determinant valuations are equal**, not merely congruent mod 2. “Iff same colour” is correct for the chosen minimal representatives, or for two towers compared level by level. At p=3, levels 0 and 1 of the same even vertex have dimensions 1 and 9; this is the counterexample to an unqualified colour-only reading. The candidate row needs this scope explicitly.

Independent evidence: `independent_lattices.py` verifies duality and canonical parity on 36 rational lattices at p=3,5,7 and prints the towers `(1,9,81,729)` and `(3,27,243,2187)` at p=3. The general proof above supplies the claim beyond the samples.

## 2. T2, neighbours and mutually unbiased bases

**Claim restated.** The p+1 self-dual superlattices of the minimal qudit lattice are its tree neighbours, and their one-dimensional stabiliser codes extend by logical Weyl displacement to the standard p+1 mutually unbiased bases. The dual inclusions put a qunaught vector in each adjacent qudit code.

**PROVED (Propositions 2.1–2.2, Theorem 2.3).** With `M=pZ_p + Z_p`, its dual is `Z_p + p^(-1)Z_p`. The quotient has p^2 elements and the pairing is F_p-valued after identifying `p^(-1)Z_p/Z_p` with F_p. Each line is maximal isotropic and its inverse image is self-dual. Multiplication by p converts the index-p superlattice condition into the tree's index-p sublattice condition, so no neighbours are omitted or duplicated. The reverse inclusion of stabilisers gives the dual code inclusion (the proof's wording “M subset L means more stabilisers” refers to L).

On the qudit, `Wtilde(u,j/p)=zeta^(uj/2) Z^j X^u`. The fixed vectors are the computational delta for the vertical line and the quadratic chirps `p^(-1/2) zeta^(-c x^2/2)` for slopes c. The phase calculation in the lane is correct. Displacements in the complementary quotient yield all p distinct stabiliser characters, hence an orthonormal basis. For two slopes the squared Gauss sum is p: expanding its norm square and setting x=y+h kills every h!=0 term by additive-character orthogonality. Division by p^2 gives 1/p. This proves unbiasedness without assuming it from the standard MUB nomenclature.

The notation `1_L` for a general phase-space lattice must mean its chosen normalized stabiliser vector, not an indicator function of a subset of Q_p; tilted lattices give chirps. Under the orthonormal coordinate identification, `delta_0` corresponds to `sqrt(p) 1_(p Z_p)`, so the lane's parenthetical equality without that factor is only an equality of lines. Neither issue changes the claimed MUBs.

**NUMERICAL, independently reproduced.** `independent_mub_trace.py` constructs bases from rank-one character projectors `(1/p) sum_r zeta^(-jr) S^r`, not the author's eigensolver. It prints every basis, every within-basis Gram matrix, and every cross-basis squared Gram matrix for p=3,5,7; all tests pass. The original MUB output prints only Boolean Gram checks and cross-basis extrema despite its stronger docstring; the independent output supplies the full matrices the brief requests.

## 3. T3, geodesics, Newton polygons and square classes

**Claims restated.** Ends are Q_p lines; an apartment is exactly the lattices split by two transverse lines. A symplectic diagonal dilation translates by twice the valuation of its parameter. For a hyperbolic projective matrix its translation length is `v(det)-2v(tr)`, and the zero extension of this expression classifies elliptics. The lane further describes when this translation is implemented by a Clifford squeeze.

**PROVED (Propositions 3.1–3.3).** The ray lattices `(ell intersect Z_p^2)+p^n Z_p^2` have cyclic quotient of order p^n, and coherent primitive generators converge to a unique Q_p line. The inverse construction is valid. The lattices `p^a Z_p e1 + p^b Z_p e2`, modulo scalar multiplication, form the unique path between the two limiting lines. Applying `diag(a^(-1),a)` changes the coordinate b-a by `2v(a)`; its metric translation length is `2|v(a)|` (the signed displacement is `2v(a)`). A unit fixes the apartment pointwise, so the “fixes no vertex” clause needs v(a)!=0, which the proof correctly supplies.

**PROVED with an elliptic-proof repair (Theorem 3.4).** Put t=tr gamma, d=det gamma. If `2v(t)<v(d)`, after dividing eigenvalues by `p^v(t)` the polynomial reduces to `X(X-t0)` with two simple roots over F_p. Hensel gives two Q_p roots, of valuations v(t) and v(d)-v(t). This justifies the length-one Newton segments rather than merely asserting their roots are rational. Rescaling one eigenvector makes the eigenbasis symplectic. The apartment calculation then proves the length formula. Conversely the one-segment polygon gives equal valuations, and scaling makes the determinant valuation 0 or 1. The integral finite module `Z_p[gamma]Z_p^2` is a lattice; in the first case gamma and its inverse preserve it, giving a fixed vertex.

For odd determinant valuation, the lane's inference “gamma^2 x=x and odd d(x,gamma x), hence distance one” is false. At p=3 or 5, `gamma=((0,1),(p,0))` has `gamma^2=pI`, yet `L=diag(1,p^2)Z_p^2` has `d(L,gamma L)=3`. The correct argument is that gamma exchanges the endpoints of the odd-length segment `[x,gamma x]`, so it inverts its **central edge**. The classification survives. The displayed max formula is the geometric/stable translation length (allowing an edge midpoint); it is not the minimum displacement over vertices for an inversion, which is 1. In the stated torsion-free, inversion-free Gamma these notions coincide. Discreteness is also essential to C5: intersection with a compact vertex stabiliser is finite, so torsion-freeness excludes nonidentity elliptics. The identity is of course an elliptic exception.

**REFUTED as worded in the candidate `prop:geodesic-squeeze`.** Even length alone does not make gamma a Q_p symplectic squeeze. The PGL class lifts to SL_2(Q_p) iff its determinant square class is trivial, equivalently iff its eigenvalue ratio lambda is a square. For `p=3, gamma=diag(1,18)`, the length is 2 but the unit part 2 is nonsquare mod 3. Thus `sqrt(lambda)` is unavailable over Q_3. The body notices this unit obstruction, while its candidate row suppresses it.

**PROVED (the correctly scoped C4).** Write lambda=`p^ell u`. For even ell=2m the translation factor alone is projectively `diag(p^(-m),p^m)`; gamma is this squeeze times the axis-fixing projective unit `diag(1,u)`. For odd ell=2m+1 it is that squeeze times `diag(1,p)` times the unit. These are identities of phase-space/projective maps; writing `D_(p^m)` beside `diag(1,p)` is shorthand, not a product of two operators on the same Hilbert space. The latter factor is a similitude, so a general odd step is not a Clifford.

**NUMERICAL, independently reproduced.** `independent_lattices.py` constructs radius-three balls by BFS and Hermite reduction of augmented integral modules modulo p^24, rather than the author's determinant/minimum-entry distance implementation. It tests splitting by whether an eigenprojector preserves the lattice, rather than by computing the two eigenline intersections. Ball sizes are 53 and 187. For the lane's four hyperbolic examples, exactly seven vertices minimize displacement and exactly the same seven pass the projector test. Additional random matrices, axes missing the base vertex, the inversion counterexample and the nonsquare-unit counterexample all pass their asserted tests. Eigenvalues use digit-by-digit Hensel lifting, independently of the author's Newton iteration.

## 4. T4a, the genuine Euler product

**Claim restated.** Oriented tailless nonbacktracking closed walks are conjugacy classes of hyperbolic deck transformations with an axis basepoint; a power of a primitive element contributes the primitive length, not the length of the power. The Ihara Euler exponent is the original translation length.

**PROVED (Theorem 4.1).** Discreteness plus torsion-freeness makes vertex stabilisers trivial, and absence of inversions makes the action a graph covering action. A lifted closed walk joins x to gamma x uniquely. Its closing nonbacktracking condition makes all translates concatenate to an axis; thus the path length equals the translation length. Conversely an axis segment projects to just such a walk. Changing the lift conjugates gamma and translates x simultaneously. The axis stabiliser acts freely by translations: an orientation reversal would fix a vertex or invert an edge, and an element acting trivially on the line is in a vertex stabiliser. Consequently this stabiliser, and hence the centraliser, is infinite cyclic. Modding the axis by the generator leaves exactly ell_0 vertices. This gives

`N_k = sum_[gamma0 primitive],m ell_0 [m ell_0=k]`,

and integrating `sum N_k u^(k-1)` proves the stated Euler product. Primitive classes are oriented; inverse classes are both counted. The normalization table's `Gamma_gamma \\ <gamma>` is not the relevant quotient (and for a proper power the displayed group is not a subgroup of `<gamma>`). It must be `Gamma_gamma \\ Axis(gamma)` with counting measure on vertices, or the corresponding translation centraliser quotient with its primitive period.

This proof is independent of the cited Ihara theorem. Independent sparse Hashimoto counts and Euler-factor extraction below verify the multiplicities. Calling these all “Clifford squeezes” would reintroduce the odd-length/unit obstruction; the correct statement is about hyperbolic PGL classes and their eigenvalue ratios.

## 5. T4b, exact code traces and what they do not identify

### 5.1 The finite compression theorem is correct

**Claim restated (Theorem 4.2).** With `C_n` the code of `p^n Z_p^2` and `P_n` its orthogonal projector,

`Tr(P_n D_a P_n) = |a|_p^(1/2) p^min(v_p(1-a),2n)`.

**PROVED, including the expansion and identity cases.** Use the orthonormal indicators `e_C=p^(n/2)1_(C/p^n+p^n Z_p)`, with C modulo `p^(2n)`. For `v(a)>=0`, the diagonal entry is `|a|^(1/2)` exactly when `(a-1)C=0 mod p^(2n)`, and is otherwise zero. The kernel of multiplication by a-1 has `p^min(v(1-a),2n)` elements. For `v(a)<0`, necessarily `v(1-a)=v(a)<0` and `v(1-a^(-1))=0`. The adjoint relation gives

`Tr(P_n D_a P_n) = conjugate Tr(P_n D_(a^-1) P_n) = |a|^(-1/2)`.

The claimed expression agrees because `p^v(a)=|a|^(-1)`. This is the missing case distinction in the lane's compressed last algebraic step; one cannot in general move a valuation through the minimum, but here the inverse has valuation zero at 1. At a=1, the operator is P_n and its trace is exactly its rank `p^(2n)`.

For a!=1, the kernel `|a|^(1/2) delta(y-ax)` has a well-defined diagonal fixed-point pullback: the linear map `x -> (1-a)x` is invertible, and Haar scaling gives its integral `|a|^(1/2)/|1-a|`. This is a flat/distributional trace, not a Hilbert-space trace of D_a. The finite compressions stabilize to it once `2n>=v(1-a)`. At a=1 that transversality fails, and there is no finite flat trace.

**Independent matrix certificate.** `independent_mub_trace.py` constructs the whole matrix by exact integration over finite residue cells. For a=`p^v u`, sample `x=(C+p^(2n)t)/p^n`, with t modulo `p^max(-v,0)`. Each sample records the output coset or escape from support. Thus each entry divided by `|a|^(1/2)` is an exact rational count. The script checks 78 cases for p=3,5, n=0,1,2, including units, negative valuations, a=1 and `v(1-a)=5>2n`; it also computes the lane's three n=3 examples. It obtains, at p=5, traces `125`, `1/5`, and `15625` for a=126, 1/25 and 1 respectively at n=3. All are correct.

### 5.2 A trace identity, but not an invariant code dynamics

**REFUTED if “trace on the code” means restriction.** For nonunit a the code is not invariant under D_a. At n=0, `D_p 1_Zp=p^(-1/2)1_(p^-1 Zp)`, which is outside C_0. The valid finite endomorphism is the compression `C_n(a)=P_n D_a P_n|C_n`. Already

`C_0(p) C_0(p^-1) = p^-1 I != I = C_0(1)`.

The independent full matrices also check this failure for n=1,2. Therefore the formula is an actual, proved identity of finite **compression traces**; it is not an action of the dilation group on one finite code, nor an intertwining of Hashimoto dynamics with that code. The trace equality alone proves no determinant formula `zeta_Y=det(1-u C_n(...))^-1` and no common representation of Gamma. In particular at n=0 it already reduces each orbit to a scalar weight. The notation `Tr_Cn D_a` in Theorem 4.3 and the annotations must explicitly mean `Tr C_n(a)`.

### 5.3 The square in C3 and the phase-space qualification

**PROVED numerical identity (Theorem 4.3, first display).** Choose the eigenvalue ratio lambda with positive valuation ell. Then `|1-lambda|=1`, so `Tr C_n(lambda)=p^(-ell/2)` for every n. The inverse ratio gives the same trace. If `g_gamma=gamma/sqrt(det gamma)` is considered over a splitting extension with the absolute value extending `|p|=p^-1`, then

`det(1-g_gamma)=2-tr(gamma)/sqrt(det gamma)`

has absolute value `|tr gamma|/|det gamma|^(1/2)=p^(ell/2)`. Hence the reciprocal determinant and `|det gamma|^(1/2)/|tr gamma|` are numerically the same weight. Choice of sign of the square root does not change this absolute value in the hyperbolic case.

**PROVED square, with coordinate/phase qualification.** In a symplectic eigenbasis,

`gamma^2/det gamma = diag(lambda^-1,lambda)`.

D_lambda is the specifically chosen Weyl intertwiner for this diagonal map. For a general gamma one must conjugate by the chosen metaplectic implementer of the eigenbasis change. “The Weil image” without a lift/phase convention is too strong: the local Weil action is metaplectic/projective on SL_2, while the lane has fixed the torus operator D_a explicitly. None of these qualifications changes the doubling of tree length from ell to **2ell**.

Moreover a **fixed finite cutoff is not conjugacy invariant**. This is stronger than a notation issue. Let `Q_t f(x)=psi(t x^2/2)f(x)` with t=1/p, and conjugate the diagonal hyperbolic matrix by the phase-space shear implemented by Q_t. Its eigenvalue ratio is still p. With P_0 held fixed, however,

`Tr(P_0 Q_t D_p Q_t^-1 P_0) = p^-1/2 integral_Zp psi((1-p^2)x^2/(2p)) dx`.

At p=5 the quadratic Gauss integral is `-1/sqrt(5)`, so this trace is **-1/5**, whereas `Tr(P_0 D_5 P_0)=1/sqrt(5)`. `independent_boundary.py` computes the finite Gauss sum directly. Thus the all-n weight formula applies to the standardized ratio dilation, or to a cutoff transported with its eigenbasis; it is not the all-n compression trace of every conjugate of `gamma^2/det gamma` in one fixed base code. A large-cutoff flat trace can be conjugacy invariant without its small finite compressions being so.

The weight is attached to the original primitive class gamma as a scalar observable of its eigenvalue ratio (equivalently the tangent multiplier at an attracting fixed boundary line). Its expression uses the dilation implementing the normalized square. The correct Euler factor remains `(1-u^ell)^-1`, indexed by the original primitive gamma. Replacing that exponent by the translation length of its square gives `(1-u^(2ell))^-1` and hence `zeta_Y(u^2)`, a different series; squares are not primitive elements of Gamma. There is no contradiction in keeping ell while decorating its orbit by `Tr C_n(lambda)`, but there is no “Euler product of these D_lambda operators with their own tree lengths” either. Also `gamma -> gamma^2/det gamma` is not a group homomorphism, as the exact matrix counterexample in `independent_boundary.py` prints.

**REFUTED universal phase-space reading (C3).** The claim that this is the Lefschetz trace of `g_gamma` on functions on **Q_p^2** needs det gamma to be a Q_p square. Even translation length is insufficient; `diag(1,18)` at p=3 is again a counterexample. For odd ell, such as `gamma=diag(1,p)`, g_gamma is not a Q_p-linear map at all. Passing to `K=Q_p(sqrt(p))` does not repair the asserted normalization: a Haar Jacobian on K^2 is `|Norm_(K/Qp) det(1-g_gamma)|_p`, the square of the extended p-adic absolute value here. The actual K-phase-space fixed-point trace is `p^-ell`, not `p^(-ell/2)`. Thus the displayed reciprocal determinant is a valid numerical equality over the extension, but a Q_p phase-space Lefschetz interpretation exists only when the normalization is defined over Q_p. If lambda is a square, D_(sqrt(lambda)) implements the original symplectic lift and has trace `p^(-ell/4)`; otherwise that operator is unavailable over Q_p. The lane's phrase “even ell” is not a substitute for this hypothesis.

### 5.4 The full explicit formula fails as printed for bipartite quotients

Let N=|V(Y)|, q=p, epsilon=1 when Y is bipartite and 0 otherwise, and remove both trivial adjacency eigenvalues ±(q+1) when present. Ihara–Bass gives the following **corrected identity**, for k>=1:

`sum_nontrivial w^k = sum_[gamma0],m:m ell0=k ell0 Tr C_n(lambda(gamma0)^m)`

`                     - N(q-1)q^(-k/2)[k even]`

`                     - (1+epsilon(-1)^k)(q^(k/2)+q^(-k/2)).`

Proof: logarithmically differentiate `(1-u^2)^(N(q-1)/2) product_lambda(1-lambda u+q u^2)` to get N_k; divide by `q^(k/2)` and remove the normalized root pairs belonging to the trivial adjacency eigenvalues. Substitute Theorems 4.1 and 4.2 into `q^(-k/2)N_k`. This derivation is valid with no Ramanujan assumption.

**REFUTED as stated in Theorem 4.3 / its candidate theorem.** That display omits the epsilon term despite claiming to include bipartite graphs and writing “non-trivial lines.” For `X^{5,13}` and k=1, the nontrivial spectrum is symmetric, so its sum is zero; N_1=0 and the tree term vanishes, but the displayed right side is `-(sqrt(5)+1/sqrt(5))`, about -2.683281573. At k=8 the missing alternating pair creates an error 625.0016. These are not rounding errors. The author correctly includes the term in §4(c), its script and C6, which do not repair the contradictory theorem display automatically. The orbit term *alone* remains correct for bipartite graphs. One could alternatively retain the alternating pair on the spectral left and say “non-uniform” instead of “non-trivial”; this is not the spectral convention used in §4(c).

### 5.5 Connes normalization, sign and the identity singularity

**PROVED normalization, with limits on the interpretation.** In graph step-time k, the orbit coefficient is `ell0 p^(-m ell0/2)`. In physical time `t=k log p`, the primitive period is `ell0 log p`, so the coefficient in the logarithmic-derivative distribution is `(ell0 log p)p^(-m ell0/2)`. Connes's prime-p term has primitive period log p, hence `(log p)p^(-m/2)`. These have the same functional rule “primitive period times normalized fixed-point weight,” not a bijection between graph prime cycles and the single rational prime p. The graph formula uses positive k and counts inverse oriented cycles as separate classes; the zeta formula has both signed times. These choices must be stated before comparing multiplicities. At p, graph q=p; the lane's reused letter q for the LPS reduction prime is unrelated to the degree parameter.

The signs in `graph-ihara.md` §5 check out: the graph's spectral trace is cycles minus the trivial pair and tree term, whereas the zeta zero functional has pole contributions minus local terms. This is a sign comparison of the explicit formulas. It does not prove a new identification of their underlying spaces or a code-complement trace formula for zeta.

**REFUTED identity-cutoff claim and proposed §10 annotation.** `Tr C_n(1)=p^(2n)` is not `2h(1)log Lambda` with Lambda=p^n. At p=5,n=1,h(1)=1 the values are 25 and approximately 3.218875825. A point value of a singular trace kernel is not the divergence of its integral against a test function.

There is a useful precise repair. Normalize multiplicative Haar measure by `vol(Z_p^*)=1`. On units, each annulus `v(1-a)=r>=1` has measure `p^-r`, while the trace is `p^min(r,2n)`. Including r=0 and the final small ball gives exactly

`integral_(Z_p^*) Tr C_n(a) d^*a = 2n+1`.

Equivalently Burnside's lemma counts the `2n+1` valuation orbits of the unit group on `Z/p^(2n)` (including zero). The independent script verifies this by enumerating finite permutation fixed points, without using the trace formula. If `vol(Z_p^*)=log p`, the integral is `2log Lambda+log p`. For a locally constant h, the shells near 1 similarly yield `2h(1)log Lambda+O(1)`. Thus the logarithmic behavior comes from **integrating** the cutoff kernel near the identity, not equating its rank at the identity with a logarithm. This local computation is not a proof of Connes's global trace formula.

**PROVED C7 with necessary wording.** D_a is an infinite-dimensional unitary and is not trace class. For nonzero integrable h, its multiplicative average is also not Hilbert–Schmidt on all of L^2(Q_p): after `f(x) -> |x|^(1/2)f(x)`, this is convolution on Q_p^*, whose noncompact valuation direction is Z. A nonzero translation-invariant operator on this regular representation cannot be compact (translate a compactly supported vector off to disjoint annuli). The zero average is an obvious exception. This does not invalidate the flat trace at a!=1; it distinguishes that trace from an ordinary operator trace. The valid replacement is the finite **compression** theorem above. C7's rank divergence is correct; the separate claim that it equals Connes's logarithmic divergence is not.

There is also a descent obstruction worth making explicit. The tree's two code colours descend to Gamma\\T exactly when Gamma preserves colour, equivalently when the quotient is bipartite. The length-seven elements in `X^{5,11}` exchange colours and hence exchange minimal code dimensions 1 and p. Thus the local code dictionary does not by itself define a Clifford action of Gamma on one family of minimal codes over this non-bipartite graph. This is a concrete obstruction to the stronger operator interpretation, not an objection to T1 or the scalar orbit identity.

## 7. T5, Casoratian and boundary pairing

### 7.1 The exact two-dimensional algebra survives

**Claim restated (§5.1).** The symplectic pairing of the two transfer eigenvectors is the normalized Casoratian; its square is the discriminant, equivalently minus four times the Weil-form determinant.

**PROVED with endpoint precision.** For `M=((0,-1),(q,lambda))`, `v_mu=(1,-mu)`, `nu=q/mu` and `mu+nu=lambda`, direct multiplication gives `Mv_mu=mu v_mu` and

`v_mu^T J v_nu = mu-nu`.

Also `(mu^(k+1)nu^k-mu^k nu^(k+1))/q^k=mu-nu` for every k. Thus the sign matches the lane's omega convention, and Abel's identity agrees with `M^T J M=qJ`. Direct inversion yields `Q=J(M-qM^-1)/2=((q,lambda/2),(lambda/2,1))`, so `(mu-nu)^2=lambda^2-4q=-4det Q`. The record's other convention `M0=((lambda,-q),(1,0))` is related by the **symplectic** change `M=J M0 J^-1`; there is no hidden sign reversal. `independent_boundary.py` verifies these as exact symbolic identities and prints five Casoratians.

The text needs two small corrections: Q is positive definite only in the **open** band `|lambda|<2sqrt(q)`; at the endpoints it has rank one, the two eigenvectors coincide and the pairing vanishes. The square root is of the discriminant `lambda^2-4q`, not of its negative. These endpoint issues matter for the attempted extension in §5.3.

### 7.2 The boundary flat trace is valid; some normalization sentences are not

**Claim restated (§5.2 and `obs:boundary-lefschetz-two-lagrangians`).** Each of the two fixed boundary lines of a hyperbolic element contributes `p^(-ell/2)` to the half-density flat trace, and the unramified Satake pair is `{p^(1-s),p^s}`.

**PROVED after specifying the density model.** Interpret `pi_s` as acting on s-densities, using compatible local Haar coordinates on P^1(Q_p), or use the corresponding Radon–Nikodym derivative for a fixed quasi-invariant measure. There is no single PGL-invariant finite measure whose coordinate derivative may be omitted. At Re s=1/2 this is the unitary half-density model; a general s is not unitary in the displayed ordinary L^2 norm. The two fixed points are nondegenerate. With the contracting eigenvalue ratio lambda, their local fixed-point terms are

`|lambda|^s/|1-lambda| + |lambda|^(-s)/|1-lambda^-1|`

`= p^(-ell s)+p^(-ell(1-s))`.

This follows by applying the one-dimensional delta change-of-variables in each of the two coordinate charts; it is a flat trace, not an ordinary trace-class assertion. At s=1/2 it is exactly `2p^(-ell/2)`, as required. `independent_boundary.py` verifies the two terms separately for p=3,5,7 and ell=1,2,3.

The parameter `mu=p^(1-s)` correctly gives the unordered pair `{mu,p/mu}` and adjacency eigenvalue `mu+p/mu`. For the Poisson kernel with one neighbour increasing the horocycle coordinate and p neighbours decreasing it, the adjacency sum is `p^s+p^(1-s)`. But the lane reverses the two subsequent identities: the correct assignments are

`p^(-ell s)=p^(-ell)mu^ell`,

`p^(-ell(1-s))=p^(-ell)(p/mu)^ell`.

At p=5,s=3/4,ell=1 the lane's printed first assignment is already false; the script prints this counterexample. The unordered sum is unaffected. Further, `|mu|=sqrt(p)` describes the entire line Re s=1/2, not just s=1/2. On that line the trace is `2p^(-ell/2) cos(t ell log p)` for s=1/2+it, so equal positive fixed-point contributions are not a property of every unitary parameter. The two complex Satake numbers are not literally the two Q_p boundary points: they are the two inducing-character contributions at those points.

### 7.3 The proposed intertwining identification remains unproved and contains false claims

**Claim restated (§5.3 / candidate conjecture).** The label symplectic form is a scalar multiple of an intertwining pairing of Poisson boundary data, and Weil positivity is equivalent to unitarity/positivity of that intertwining pairing.

**UNVERIFIED core identification.** No map from the finite graph label space to a precisely normalized boundary-data space is constructed. General Poisson transforms restricted along a ray do not satisfy the transfer recurrence as written. For a radial adjacency eigenfunction one has `p f_(r+1)=lambda f_r-f_(r-1)` and must use `F_r=p^r f_r` to obtain `F_(r+2)=lambda F_(r+1)-pF_r`. Arbitrary nonradial restrictions need not satisfy either scalar recurrence. A Poisson kernel at s=1/2 along a ray moving away from its boundary point has ratio `p^-1/2`; substituting it into the lane's unrescaled transfer recurrence yields the nonzero defect `(p-1)^2/p`. The independent script prints this for p=3,5,7.

There is a second type mismatch: the complex-linear extension of omega is bilinear, with coefficient form `(mu-nu)(a b'-b a')`. The lane inserts conjugates and calls `(mu-nu)(a conjugate(b')-a' conjugate(b))` antisymmetric without specifying a real structure or a sesquilinear convention; on arbitrary complex coefficients it is not the same symplectic form. A pairing with a standard intertwiner cannot fix the missing map, recurrence rescaling and complex-conjugation conventions by one unspecified scalar.

**REFUTED positivity equivalence.** The complementary-series objection is substantive, not removable by saying “a positive but different pairing.” The lane explicitly uses the standard intertwiner

`I_s f(x)=integral |x-y|^(-2(1-s)) f(y) dy`.

For real 1/2<s<1, set alpha=2s-1 in (0,1). This is convolution with `|x|^(alpha-1)`. Its Fourier multiplier, in the stated conductor-zero convention, is

`Gamma_p(alpha)|xi|^(-alpha)`, where `Gamma_p(alpha)=(1-p^(alpha-1))/(1-p^(-alpha))>0`.

Derivation: at xi=1 the shells inside Z_p sum to `(1-1/p)/(1-p^(-alpha))`; the shell of radius p contributes `-p^(alpha-1)`; larger shells integrate to zero by character orthogonality. Scaling gives the asserted multiplier. Therefore the Hermitian form `integral conjugate(f) I_s f` is strictly positive on nonzero test functions. The intertwining covariance follows directly by a change of variables (and for inversion from `|x^-1-y^-1|=|x-y|/(|x||y|)`), so this is the relevant complementary-series invariant form.

At s=3/4, however, `lambda=p^(3/4)+p^(1/4)>2sqrt(p)`, so the label Weil form is indefinite. At p=5, the independent finite residue-cell Riesz Gram matrix has smallest eigenvalue `1/sqrt(5)=0.4472135955`, while `lambda=4.8390503061` and `det Q=-0.8541019662`. The script computes both forms directly. Thus positivity of this very intertwining pairing does **not** characterize the Ramanujan band. The proposed factor `(4p-lambda^2)/4` times a positive definite pairing would be negative definite outside the band, whereas Q is indefinite, another immediate inertia obstruction.

**REFUTED reducibility/singularity explanation.** Coalescence `mu=±sqrt(p)` of the two transfer roots is not reducibility of normalized unramified principal series. The usual reducibility condition for normalized GL_2 induction is that the two inducing characters have ratio `|.|^±1` (standard principal-series classification, from memory); after descent this gives the trivial/Steinberg endpoints `lambda=±(p+1)`, not `±2sqrt(p)`. Independently, the normalized Riesz intertwiner has multiplier `|xi|^(1-2s)` and becomes the identity at s=1/2. A pole in an *unnormalized* c-function at the coalescing root is not a singular normalized intertwiner or a reducible representation. The lane leaves `c(s)=(1-p^(-2s)... )` unspecified, which is not a usable normalization.

Consequently the exact Casoratian and half-density trace are retainable statements. The finite-quotient boundary map remains OPEN/UNVERIFIED, and the candidate's added equality between Weil positivity and unitarity/intertwining positivity is false as written. Merely labelling the paragraph SKETCHED does not protect its explicitly asserted supporting facts from these counterexamples.
