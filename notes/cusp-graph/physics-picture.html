A physicist's picture

Resonances, cusps, and the Lindblad rebound

A finite graph with an infinite ray attached is a cavity coupled to a single-mode waveguide. The ray is the guide — the cusp. Everything the cavity does to a wave is one complex number per momentum, the reflection coefficient R. Its poles inside the disc are bound states, its zeros are resonances, and the states with a node at the junction are invisible to it. Put the leaked quantum back and you have a Lindbladian.

Section 1

The cavity and the waveguide

Take the classic scattering problem: a particle on the half-line with a potential well near the wall, psi(x) = e^{-ikx} + R(k) e^{+ikx}. The discrete version is the one we want. Put the particle on a chain with hopping 1, and let the well be a finite weighted graph X — the core — glued at one site v_0 to a semi-infinite chain with coupling c.

R(z) = ( 1 - c^2 G(lambda)/z ) / ( c^2 G(lambda) z - 1 ), G(lambda) = <v_0|(lambda - T_X)^{-1}|v_0> equivalently R(z) = - p(z) / ptilde(z) (D1: the sign is real, the power of z is 0) p(z) = det( (1+z^2) I - z T_X - c^2 P_0 ) ptilde(z) = z^{2n} p(1/z) P_0 = |v_0><v_0|

Why is the exit one-dimensional? Because a hyperbolic cusp is a funnel whose cross-section shrinks geometrically as you go up it. In Serre's discrete model each rung Lambda_n sends q edges down and one up, so the invariant measure falls like q^{-n}. A wave going up a shrinking guide falls below cutoff in every transverse channel except the angle-independent one. One function of one variable leaves through a cusp.

What the weight c² = q+1 is doing

After the unitary g = m^{1/2} f that flattens the measure, the shrinking guide becomes an ordinary uniform chain and the whole effect of the shrinking is absorbed into one number at the junction: c^2 = q+1. Cusp = single-mode guide; c^2 = q+1 = impedance of the port. (D2 corrects the junction measure to m_0 : m_1 = q : (q+1); with the uncorrected q^{-n} the operator is not even self-adjoint. The value c^2 = q+1 survives intact.) With h cusps you get an h × h scattering matrix. The exit counts cusps, never resonances.

Section 2

The scattering lab

This is the instrument the rest of the page is built on. Choose a core, drag the coupling, and watch the roots of p move. A root inside the disc is a resonance. A root outside shows up as a pole of R at its reciprocal — a bound state. A root pinned at z = ±1 is a threshold, a half-bound state (D5: p(±1) = ptilde(±1) identically, so band-edge roots always cancel, with no cusp form present). A core eigenvector with a node at v_0 is a cusp form: it cancels out of R altogether and is drawn hollow.

The page computes p exactly, by evaluating det((1+z²)I - zT_X - c²P_0) at the (2n+1)-th roots of unity and inverting the DFT, then reducing to the Krylov space of (T_X, v_0) — whose orthocomplement is the cusp-form space. That is why Petersen (cusp multiplicity 7) comes out clean: the cusp sees only the cyclic part of the core.

Three things to try

c² crossing 1. Take the loop core with a = 0.5 and press sweep c². At c² = 1 a root sits exactly at z = 0: the product identity ∏ roots = 1 − c² forces it. A resonance at z = 0 means the holding law is supported on even times only — the degenerate, periodic case. Aperiodicity is exactly c ≠ 1 (D14), not, as one might guess, non-reality of the resonance.

A resonance leaving the disc. Keep sweeping. At c² = 1.5 a root crosses z = +1: for a moment it is a half-bound state at threshold, and past it, it is outside — and R has acquired a pole inside the disc at its reciprocal. The leaky mode has become trapped.

A dark state. Pick the cusp-form core. One eigenvector of T_X vanishes at v_0, so it never couples to the guide; it is an honest l² eigenvalue of the whole infinite graph that R cannot see, and it cancels between p and ptilde. (D4 sharpens C2(i) for the same reason: a cusp form outside the band is an l² eigenvalue but is not a pole of R.)

Section 3

Feshbach: integrate out the guide, get a complex cavity

Split with projectors P (cavity) and Q = 1-P (guide), write H|psi> = E|psi> in blocks and eliminate the Q part. What is left is the cavity plus a rank-one term at the port:

H_eff(E) = P H P + P H Q (E - Q H Q)^{-1} Q H P = T_X + Sigma(E) P_0 , Sigma = c^2 g g = <r_1|(lambda - T_ray)^{-1}|r_1> and deleting r_1 leaves the same ray, so g = 1/(lambda - g) => g^2 - lambda g + 1 = 0 => g = z or g = 1/z .

Wide-band limit. Near the band centre theta ≈ pi/2, Sigma ≈ -i c² is energy independent, so H_eff = T_X - i c² P_0 = H - (i/2) L†L with L = √2 c |out><v_0|. With no extra assumption at all we have landed on the Lindblad no-jump generator.

Breit–Wigner is a Blaschke factor

Near an isolated resonance the reflection coefficient is unimodular with the phase sweeping 2 pi — the Breit–Wigner line shape. In the disc variable the same object is a Blaschke factor, and R is -∏_i (z-z_i)/(1-z_i z) over the roots of p (D13). A factor with |z_i| < 1 winds once — one resonance, one bump. A factor with |z_i| > 1 is an inverse Blaschke factor: a pole inside, a bound state. So R is not inner; R times the Blaschke product over the bound states is, and that is the characteristic function of the Lax–Phillips contraction.

On the unit circle |R| = 1 exactly, whatever you do: unitarity forbids an absorption dip in the amplitude. The resonance is pure phase. Pull the reading circle inward and the dip appears, reaching zero when ρ = |z₀| — that is where the zero of R actually is. Connes's word for the zeta zeros, absorption spectrum rather than emission, is exactly this: not lines the cavity emits, but a frequency at which the wave is held for ~1/Γ and re-emitted with a 2 pi phase slip. And the time delay is where the primes have to live: -z d/dz log R is the cusp term of the trace formula.

Section 4

Waves, and what actually leaves

The discrete wave equation on the whole infinite graph is u_{m+1} = T u_m - u_{m-1}. On the ray it has exact d'Alembert solutions u_m(k) = F(k+m) + B(k-m): finite propagation speed one, one site per tick. Start everything on the core and watch. The wavefront moves out at speed exactly 1, the energy left on the core falls off as a sum of |z_i|^{2m}, and at late times the narrowest resonance — the one closest to the rim — is the only one you can still see.

The dark state, run live

Switch the core to the cusp-form core and the initial state to the dark state. The energy on the core does not move at all. The eigenvector has a node at v_0, so the junction never sees it; extended by zero it is an honest l² eigenfunction of the infinite graph, sitting inside the continuum. The preprint exhibits exactly this on a 3-regular graph with a funnel, eigenvalue -2 in the tempered range [-2√2, 2√2] — an embedded eigenvalue, and the cavity physicist's dark state.

Section 5

Quantum jumps and the rebound

A Lindbladian is a no-event semigroup plus a reset. Unravel it as trajectories: evolve with the contraction C (whose eigenvalues are the resonances), lose norm as the guide carries probability away, and when the detector clicks, put the cavity back into a chosen state Omega — the repump.

continuous: L_Omega(rho) = B* rho + rho B + fl(rho) Omega , fl(rho) = <j|rho|j> discrete: E_Omega(rho) = C rho C* + <j|rho|j> Omega 1 - C*C = |j><j| holding law m_Omega(m) = <j| C^{m-1} Omega C*^{m-1} |j> , m >= 1 mean holding time mu_Omega = sum_{m>=0} Tr( C^m Omega C*^m ) stationary state rho_inf = mu^{-1} sum_{m>=0} C^m Omega C*^m

The one-dimensional exit 1 - C*C = |j><j| says there is one jump operator, i.e. one detector, because there is one cusp — however many resonances there are. The model used below is the Sz.-Nagy–Foias functional model of a contraction with defect indices (1,1): Gram matrix G_nm = 1/(1 - conj(z_n) z_m), C = G^{1/2} diag(z) G^{-1/2}, j = G^{-1/2} 1. The Gram identity <k_n,k_m>(1 - conj(z_n)z_m) = conj(a_n) a_m with a_n = <j|k_n> (D12) then proves that no mode is dark to the exit: if some a_n vanished, <k_n,k_n> would too.

Three facts the demo makes visible

ρ_inf = Ω iff the moduli are equal (T4). Choose a list with one common modulus and the stationary state equals the repump, for every mode-diagonal mixture; move one modulus and it stops. Then the mean holding time is 1/(1-r²) independent of the mixture — 2.000000 for the loop at r = 0.707107, 2.303579 for K4 at r = 0.752259.

The spectrum of ρ_inf is a Gram spectrum, not the weight list. The eigenvectors are not orthogonal. Feed the loop core weights {0.188121, 0.811879} and the eigenvalues come out {0.142488, 0.857512}: the modes overlap by 0.447214. The grey bars are the weights you asked for; the coloured bars are what you get.

The vacuum traps the trajectory. Switch the vacuum on. It is invariant under C and has no exit, so once a jump lands there nothing ever leaves again: the mean holding time diverges and there is no stationary state. This is 04h's "the vacuum needs an exit", and it is why the even sector cannot simply be handed to the trivial l² spectrum of the graph.

Section 6

RH as equal linewidths

For the Riemann channel there is one mode per nontrivial zero rho = sigma + i gamma: frequency gamma/2, amplitude decay rate (1-sigma)/2. So

RH <=> every mode decays at the single rate 1/4 <=> every resonance of this one cavity has the same linewidth <=> in the disc: the resonance multiset lies on one circle |z| = r

A caution on the folklore. Equal linewidth is not equal Q: with Q_n = omega_n/Gamma = gamma_n, uniform width means Q grows linearly with frequency. What is uniform is the lifetime — every mode rings for the same time, mean holding 2 in the continuous normalisation, 1/(1-r²) in discrete time.

The width sum rule is cheap; the degeneracy is the hard part

The functional equation R(z)R(1/z) = 1 pairs rho <-> 1-rho, i.e. widths (1-sigma)/2 <-> sigma/2, whose sum is always 1/2. That is unconditional. RH says the pairing is implemented by a reflection through one line rather than a genuine exchange of two distinct widths: no pair split, every pair degenerate. In the disc, the resonance multiset is invariant under inversion in the circle |z| = r, whose fixed set is exactly that circle.

Do not oversell the criterion

Equal widths is a reformulation of RH in cavity language, not a mechanism. The ring resonator gets it free from a rotation symmetry, and the flag-spectrum theorem holds for any one-exit no-event semigroup — the resonance list is put in by hand. Worse, in the toy nothing pins the radius: the usable product identity is ∏_{|z|<1} |z_i| = |1-c²| ∏_trivial |z_t| (D16), which fixes r^{#res} only in terms of the trivial spectrum and c. Sharp consequence: if c² = q+1 and the only trivial modes are the Perron pair ±q^{-1/2}, the right-hand side is 1 — impossible for moduli below 1 — so there are no resonances at all. The pure cusp's emptiness is forced by arithmetic on the product, and any quotient with resonances must have l² spectrum beyond the Perron pair.

Section 7

The graded twist: the zeros are coherences

In the notebook the zeros enter the trace with a minus sign — a supertrace of a graded bond — so the modes carrying them are odd. An odd operator is not a population; it is a coherence between the vacuum and one excitation, i.e. a dipole: an off-diagonal element oscillating at the resonance frequency and decaying at the resonance width. It is what a classical field couples to, and it is not a probability.

That is the whole mechanism of the protection theorem. The exit flux fl(rho) = <j|rho|j> reads a population, so it vanishes identically on odd operators; the reset cannot feed them; and the odd modes evolve autonomously whatever the repump is.

Convention warning

The preprint's mu and the brief's z are the two roots of the same quadratic mu + mu^{-1} = lambda, and the two write-ups do not always pick the same root. In the elliptic-curve panels the trivial pair sits outside the unit circle at ±√q while the Weil family sits inside at q^{-1/4} — that mixture is the preprint's convention, kept here verbatim. What transfers is the invariant statement: one common modulus for the nontrivial family, not the numerical value of the modulus.

Section 9

What to keep in mind

  1. The exit counts cusps, not resonances. One cusp = one propagating mode = one jump operator = one detector, however many resonances there are. Hence the rank-one defect and the single reset map; h cusps give an h × h scattering matrix.
  2. Bound states use the incoming self-energy, resonances the outgoing one. Same square root, two branches: Sigma = c² z gives the l² eigenvalues (wave z^k, decaying up the ray), Sigma = c²/z the resonances (wave z^{-k}, growing up the ray = Gamow). The wrong branch gives growth in time instead of decay.
  3. Cusp forms are dark states. An eigenvector of the core with a node at the junction never couples to the guide and cancels between numerator and denominator of R — including inside the continuum. Band-edge roots at z = ±1 cancel too, with no cusp form present (D5), and must be counted as half a bound state or Levinson's count is wrong.
  4. The rebound is the repump. Omega is just "what state the cavity is put back into when a photon is counted". The stationary state is the time-average of the decaying orbit started from Omega; conversely, for a target stationary rho the repump is forced: Omega = Q_rho/Tr Q_rho with Q_rho = rho - C rho C* the loss, and admissibility Q_rho >= 0 is a genuine restriction (a random density fails it).
  5. Uniform width gives a uniform holding time. In discrete time with common modulus r the mean holding time is 1/(1-r²) whatever mixture you rebound into; in the continuous Riemann normalisation, exactly 2. Conversely, "stationary state = repump for every modal mixture" is the equal-width condition.
  6. Do not oversell the criterion. The flag-spectrum theorem holds for any one-exit no-event semigroup: nothing about zeta is used, the resonance list is put in by hand. The ring resonator gets equal widths free from a rotation symmetry. Equal widths is a reformulation, not a mechanism.
The open question, in physics terms

What physical reset does the arithmetic choose? Which rebound density Omega — which state the cavity is put back into after each detected quantum — is the one selected by the primes, rather than chosen by hand from a flag?

Equivalently: a distinguished isometry U carrying the Bost–Connes observables and the prime action, so that Omega is not free. In the graph round the question is finite: attach the ray to a congruence quotient of GL_2(F_q[T]) and see whether the arithmetic hands you the exit, the resonances and the rebound together.

Conventions and corrections

Sign. R(z) = - p(z)/ptilde(z), and the power of z is zero (D1). Sanity: c → 0 must give R = -1 (Dirichlet), and it does. The G-form (1 - c²G/z)/(c²Gz - 1) already contains the minus.

Which one is φ. phi = 1/R (D7). For the pure cusp 1/R(z) = q ζ_K(2s-1)/ζ_K(2s) with z = q^{s-1/2} — the function-field twin of phi(s) = xi(2s-1)/xi(2s). The poles of phi in the disc are the zeros of R, i.e. the resonances. Everything downstream must use 1/R.

μ versus z. The preprint's mu and the brief's z are the two roots of the same quadratic mu + 1/mu = lambda; the two write-ups do not always pick the same root.

Hypotheses in force. H-SIMPLE (distinct resonances) for the Gram model, the flags and the eigen-operator statements — it fails exactly for the free half-line; H-NOEDGE (p(±1) ≠ 0), without which band-edge roots cancel and the energy form degenerates; H-APERIODIC, which is just "0 is not a resonance", i.e. c ≠ 1.

How the numbers on this page are produced. p(z) by exact DFT interpolation of det((1+z²)I − zT_X − c²P_0) at the (2n+1)-th roots of unity, after a Krylov reduction of (T_X, v_0); roots by Durand–Kerner; the contraction by a complex Cholesky of the Gram matrix G_nm = 1/(1 − z̄_n z_m); Hermitian spectra by cyclic Jacobi on the real 2d × 2d embedding. No external numerical library.
