## B1 — SHARPENED: the product quotient, with the correct lattice

**PROVED (external input, not byte-cited).** Mozes (1995), *Actions of Cartan subgroups*, and Rattaggi (2004/2005), *Anti-tori in square complex groups*, §3, give the following quaternion-lattice construction: for distinct primes p,l congruent to 1 modulo 4, the projective subgroup generated by the primary integral quaternions of norms p and l acts freely and transitively on the vertices of the Bruhat–Tits product T_(p+1) × T_(l+1). Its presentation has inverse-paired generators and the quaternion reordering squares. The embeddings are into PGL₂(Q_p) × PGL₂(Q_l). This is the precise construction used here. [Rattaggi, primary source](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0411547v2).

1. **REFUTED (ambient group and “full units”).** Put B = (-1,-1)_Q and G = PB×. The arithmetic group is Γ = ⟨[S₁₃],[S₁₇]⟩ in G(Q), embedded diagonally in G(Q₁₃) × G(Q₁₇). B is a division algebra over Q, not M₂(Q). Thus the asserted rational splitting into PGL₂(Q) is false. The primary parity condition is essential: the full projective quaternion unit group contains torsion (for example [i]) and is not this simply transitive lattice.

2. **PROVED (quotient and cells).** Let Γ(5) = ker(Γ → PGL₂(F₅)). The displayed reduction formula in the worked example is multiplicative. The horizontal generators alone generate all 120 elements (also a finite breadth-first-search check); hence the reduction is onto. Nonsquare determinants prove that the image meets the other determinant class, but *alone* do not prove surjectivity. The quotient X = Γ(5)\(T₁₄ × T₁₈) has vertices Γ(5)\Γ. Its edges and square attaching maps are exactly those in §§1–3 of the worked example. The complete bipartite vertex links certify the local product structure. There are 252 based horizontal-then-vertical corners at each vertex; four corners represent each geometric square, giving 120·252/4 = 7560. The base Γ\(T₁₄ × T₁₈) has one vertex, 7 horizontal loops, 9 vertical loops and 63 squares. Calling X a “Cayley square complex” is legitimate; calling it the simply connected Cayley presentation complex of the *finite group* is not: π₁(X) = Γ(5), not the trivial group.

## B2 — SHARPENED: horizontal H¹ and an actual correspondence

1. **PROVED (which graph quotient).** Fix a vertex o₁₇ of the second tree. Let Λ₁₃ = Stab_Γ(o₁₇) = ⟨S₁₃⟩ and Λ₁₃(5) = Λ₁₃ ∩ Γ(5). Then the horizontal colour graph is Y = Λ₁₃(5)\T₁₄. In contrast, Γ(5)\T₁₄ is not the asserted graph quotient: Γ(5) is a lattice in the *product*, not the horizontal leaf stabilizer. The horizontal graph on the 120 vertices is connected, so its boundary ∂ = Dᵗ has rank 119. There are no horizontal 2-cells. Consequently

   H₁(Y;R) = ker ∂ = K,   H¹(Y;R) = C¹/im D ≅ K,   dim K = 840−120+1 = 721.

   The last isomorphism uses the positive edge inner product. It is not an identification with H¹(X).

2. **PROVED (finite correspondence).** Define a graph C whose vertices are directed vertical edges (g,b). Its edges are squares with their two directed vertical sides, oriented consistently from the lower horizontal edge to the upper one. Projection to the source endpoints gives p:C→Y; projection to target endpoints gives r:C→Y. For every horizontal edge, each map has 18 lifts, by unique square completion. Define unnormalized transport on cochains by p_*r^*. For e=(g,a), its lifts have source vertical side (g,b′) and target side (gφ(a),b), where ab=±b′a′. Therefore

   (p_*r^*f)(g,a) = Σ_(b∈S₁₇) f(gφ(b′),a′).

   This is the precise correspondence meant by T₁. Reversing vertical edges exchanges p and r, so T₁ is self-adjoint. Reversing both horizontal sides proves that its extension to *directed* horizontal edge fields commutes with horizontal reversal R. The undirected antisymmetric convention is the R=−1 sector.

3. **PROVED (cochain identity and Hecke meaning).** For a vertex function h, telescoping around a square gives

   T₁Dh(g,a) = Σ_b h(gφ(a)φ(b)) − Σ_(b′)h(gφ(b′)) = DA₁₇h(g,a).

   Taking adjoints proves invariance of K. The correspondence is the spherical double coset at 17, of degree 18, on the arithmetic horizontal graph; thus its induced operator is the unnormalized T₁₇. One can equivalently use the product-tree quotient with the 17-coordinate an edge rather than a vertex, with the two endpoint maps. This avoids an unspecified “Γ₁₇-stuff” subgroup. The 252 reordering rules give a bijection of the two kinds of length-two coloured paths, hence A₁₃A₁₇ = A₁₇A₁₃. The squares are the edge-level refinement of that equality; equality of the two matrices alone would not specify the squares.

4. **PROVED (characteristic polynomial).** C¹ = im D ⊕ K, and D identifies mean-zero vertex functions with im D. Hence χ_T = χ_(T₁)(x)(x−18)/χ_(A₁₇)(x). This is a cochain quotient identity, not a statement about the old space of *directed* edges, which is larger.

## B3 — SHARPENED: Hecke eigenvalues, corrected level and newspace

**PROVED (elementary class-set justification).** The Hurwitz order is norm-Euclidean: rounding four real coordinates to the nearest all-integral or all-half-integral tuple gives squared distance <1. Division with remainder proves class number one. Its projective units have order 12, and reduction identifies them with (O/2O)×, also of order 12 (the residue field of the local division algebra is F₄ and its radical modulo 2 has order 4). Injectivity follows by inspecting the 24 norm-one Hurwitz units modulo ±1. ## B4 — SHARPENED spectral realization; REFUTED canonical Petersson identification

### 1. What Eichler–Shimura actually identifies

**PROVED.** Under the corrected Π_- and multiplicities in B3, let W_ℓ be the direct sum of the two-dimensional weight-two Galois representations, with multiplicity m(π) and coefficient embeddings as above. Use geometric Frobenius on étale H¹, with characteristic polynomial X²−a₁₇X+17 (equivalently the usual arithmetic-Frobenius convention on the corresponding Tate-module representation). Each summand satisfies the Eichler–Shimura relation. The finite strict bound makes its two roots distinct. Thus, after extending coefficients to an algebraically closed characteristic-zero field and choosing identifications of the algebraic eigenvalues,

   (K⊕K, F₁) ≅ ⊕_(π∈Π_-) (V_π, Frob₁₇)^(⊕m(π))

as *one-operator semisimple systems*, with α+β=a₁₇ and αβ=17. These are the arithmetic-weight Satake roots; unitary-normalized Satake parameters are α/√17 and β/√17. Distinguish this field-extended spectral isomorphism from an isomorphism of real Hodge structures, integral lattices, or Galois representations: the Bass construction supplies only one operator.

**SHARPENED.** W_ℓ is realizable as a Hecke-selected part of H¹ of modular Jacobians, allowing a product and repeated factors with the stated multiplicities. The local selection at 2 must be transferred, and the reversal eigenspace at 13 selected. There is no assertion that it is the *whole* H¹ of a particular genus-721 modular curve. A polarized abelian variety factor or a selected cohomological summand need not be a Jacobian of a curve. In particular, adjoining just H⁰=1 and H²=17 produces a formal curve-shaped package, not automatically the zeta function of that modular curve.

### 2. Pairings are additional data, not consequences of the polynomial

**PROVED (external period theorem, not byte-cited).** Eichler (1957) and Shimura (1959), the weight-two Eichler–Shimura period isomorphism: for a torsion-free congruence subgroup Δ and its smooth compactified modular curve C, the maps f↦[f(z)dz] and its conjugate identify S₂(Δ)⊕overline(S₂(Δ)) with H¹(C,C), compatibly with Hecke correspondences. Under this identification the positive form on holomorphic differentials is (i/2)∫_C ω∧conj(ω); for ω=f(z)dz it equals ∫_(Δ\H)|f(z)|² dx dy, the weight-two Petersson integral in this convention. These assertions concern Betti/Hodge cohomology and do not assert a Hodge action of good-prime Frobenius. [Shimura's original paper](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jmath1948/11/4/11_4_291/_article).

**REFUTED (the claimed implication).** Jacquet–Langlands identifies automorphic representations and Hecke actions; it does not identify the doubled graph basis with integral cycles on a modular curve. Eichler–Shimura supplies a Betti Hodge structure and an étale Frobenius realization, but no canonical real operator Frob₁₇ on the complex curve's Betti H¹ preserving its Hodge decomposition. Frobenius of the reduction is not a holomorphic self-map of the characteristic-zero modular curve. Étale–Betti comparison alone does not transport Frobenius into a specified real Hodge isometry. Consequently neither Ω_B = cup nor G = Petersson follows from these theorems.

**PROVED (what is available without that claim).** Spectrally, each distinct pair α,17/α supports a nondegenerate alternating pairing scaled by 17. Choosing dual eigenbases can identify such forms over a splitting field. The choices, rational structures, integral lattices, and positive real structures have not thereby been identified. On the graph model itself,

   Ω_B = [[0,I],[-I,0]],   G = [[I,−T/2],[−T/2,17I]],
   F₁ᵗΩ_BF₁ = 17Ω_B,   F₁ᵗGF₁ = 17G,
   G>0 ⇔ 17I−T²/4>0 ⇔ ||T||<2√17.

At equality a companion block is a nontrivial Jordan block, so *no* positive invariant metric exists. This is exactly Proposition 9.3 of the supplied manuscript. It is a strict spectral criterion, stronger at the endpoint than the weak Ramanujan bound.

**PROVED (even the two finite metrics differ).** Put r_a=√(17−a²/4). On an a-eigenspace,

   J_a = (F_a−aI/2)/r_a,   J_a²=−I,

Once Ω_B and F₁ are fixed in this strict elliptic situation, this is the unique commuting positive compatible complex structure: on the α-eigenspace any commuting complex structure has eigenvalues ±i, and positivity of Ω_B fixes the sign; the conjugate eigenspace fixes the opposite sign. ### 3. The invariant metric cone, including multiplicities

**PROVED.** In a complex eigenbasis of F₁, the equation F₁*HF₁=17H reads

   (conj(λ_i)λ_j−17)H_ij=0.

Because all λ_i have modulus √17, H_ij can be nonzero exactly when λ_i=λ_j. Thus H is *block* diagonal, with an arbitrary positive Hermitian matrix on each repeated eigenvalue space. Reality relates the α and β blocks by complex conjugation; it does not diagonalize either block.

For a distinct a with multiplicity m_a, all real symmetric solutions in the original two-copy coordinates are

   H_a = [[A, −aA/2+N], [−aA/2−N, 17A]],
   Aᵗ=A,   Nᵗ=−N,

subject to positivity. Equivalently they form a copy of Herm⁺_(m_a)(C). In particular the real dimension of the full cone is Σ_a m_a² = **16539**, computed exactly from §13 as Σ_f deg(f)m_f². For example on the multiplicity-four eigenvalue 7 there are positive off-diagonal 4×4 weights. The asserted “one scalar per copy” is therefore **REFUTED** unless additional simultaneous symmetries and a preferred decomposition are imposed.

**PROVED (normalization dependence).** For a unit edge eigenvector v, use the raw companion eigenvectors e_α=(αv,v) and e_β=(βv,v). Then

   e_α*G e_α = e_β*G e_β = 2r_a²,
   e_α*(Ω_BJ)e_α = 2r_a.

So neither metric has constant weights across distinct a in this normalization. Rescaling e_α,e_β changes what “all weights equal” means. The four transitive symmetries and the exit-normalized basis of `thm:arithmetic-metric` in shard 04l are not supplied for the present 721-dimensional graph space; its specific one-ray conclusion cannot be imported.

**OPEN (a geometric ratio, with a precise conditional formula).** If a Hecke-equivariant map from K to a chosen space of weight-two forms is fixed, pullback of Petersson defines a positive Hecke-invariant coefficient metric P. One can *construct* a Petersson-based Bass certificate

   G_P = [[P,−PT/2], [−PT/2,17P]].

On a simultaneously specified line where P=p_fI, G_P=p_fG; if the graph vector has norm one and is mapped to the q-expansion-normalized f, p_f=||f||²_Petersson in the chosen integral convention. This is not a proof that G_P is the curve's Hodge metric under a Frobenius comparison. Rescaling the comparison by c_f changes p_f by |c_f|²; a unitary normalization can force p_f=1 by definition. No comparison or periods are specified in the input, so no canonical ratio G/Petersson or equality with a “symmetric ray” is determined. This settles the logical claim in the brief negatively; it leaves a concrete geometric comparison problem open.

## B5 — REFUTED as an identification; SHARPENED as a Hecke-family comparison

### 1. The correct finite dictionary

This is finite, exact, and includes accidental equality of a₁₇ among different forms. To write it in automorphic terms, decompose A(U₂K(13)) into the π with π₂^(U₂)≠0, π₁₃^(K(13))≠0, and spherical π_l for l≠2,13, then apply the component projector before the Hom in (B5.1). at a₁₇(π), counting the orbit once. The projected-space formula (B5.1) also covers any self-twist without an extra convention. By contrast, B3 uses Π_- with principal level at 5 and St at 13. These are different local selection rules.

### 2. “Odd Weil” is not Steinberg, nor the odd doubled-bond sector

[Original article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0021869377903945).

**PROVED (finite type calculation).** W_- has dimension 6. A unipotent generator acts on the six odd basis pairs by six nontrivial chirp eigenvalues ψ(x²/2), so it has no unipotent-fixed vector. Thus W_- is a finite cuspidal Weil constituent. The finite Steinberg representation has dimension 13 and is different. More importantly, the channel uses End(W_-), not W_- itself.

Here is an elementary exact character description that fixes this distinction. For g∈SL₂(F₁₃), modulo ±1, write χ(g)=Tr End(W_-)(g). The quadratic Gauss-sum identity, applied to the Fourier/chirp realization and the projection (I−P)/2, gives

- χ(1)=36;
- χ(g)=0 on regular split semisimple elements;
- χ(g)=1 on regular nonsplit semisimple elements;

   End(W_-) = 1 ⊕ τ₇ ⊕ τ₁₄,₁ ⊕ τ₁₄,₂.                  (B5.2)

The two 14-dimensional constituents are principal series induced by inverse pairs of torus characters of orders 6 and 3; τ₇ is one half of the principal series induced by the quadratic torus character. On a regular split element with torus parameter j modulo 6 their characters are respectively 2cos(πj/3), 2cos(2πj/3), and (−1)^j; on regular nonsplit elements all three vanish. Their unipotent values are 1,1,(1+ε_g√13)/2. These formulas verify (B5.2), including the dimension 1+7+14+14=36.

**PROVED (local exclusion).** The K(13)-fixed space of an unramified twist of the local Steinberg representation restricts to the irreducible 13-dimensional finite Steinberg of G⁺; a tame determinant twist does not change that restriction. ### 3. Exact polynomial comparison and zeta signs

**REFUTED (Harrow equals old/new).** Harrow containment is finite-group Fourier decomposition, which does not involve an incidence differential. The χ_T quotient is cancellation of gradients in an edge cochain complex. Both use invariant subspaces; they are not the same theorem or decomposition.

**PROVED (Bass relation, with the correct zetas).** Write Q_a(u)=1−au+17u² and P_R(u)=det(I−uΣ_-+17u²)/(Q₁₈(u)). Then the script's *ungraded* odd-Weil Hashimoto operator satisfies

## B6 — OPEN for the exact CP realization; REFUTED for the proposed shortcut

### 1. A spectral grading is not a doubled-bond grading

**PROVED (necessary CP tests).** In the notebook's definition, P is a parity on an actual bond V=V_+⊕V_-, Γ=Ad P grades End(V), and E(X)=Σ_s A_sXA_s* has homogeneous Kraus operators. Expanding a power gives

   Str(E^n) = Σ_(words w of length n) |Tr(PA_w)|² ≥ 0,
   Tr(E^n) = Σ_w |Tr(A_w)|² ≥ 0.

Moreover dim End(V)_even − dim End(V)_odd = (dim V_+−dim V_-)² ≥ 0. Thus the particular spaces of dimensions (2,1442) cannot themselves be the two full matrix sectors. There is also an immediate stronger trace obstruction to prescribing exactly E_even=1⊕17 and E_odd=F₁: at n=2, the worked example gives Tr F₁² = 290−11520 = −11230, so Tr E² = 290−11230 = **−10940**, impossible for CP. This refutes an exact sector-by-sector realization even before its dimension obstruction.

**SHARPENED (why this is not a no-go theorem for the question as quantified).** The question asks only equality of *supertraces* for n≥1. Extra identical spectra in both sectors cancel from every such supertrace, and zero eigenvalues are invisible. They can change both preceding obstructions. Indeed equality for all n≥1 fixes precisely the nonzero net divisor

   [1]+[17] − Σ_(λ∈spec F₁)[λ],

by the Vandermonde identity for distinct nonzero eigenvalues, but fixes neither the separate spectra nor the zero multiplicities. Any such CP realization must have zero net multiplicity ν(0)=(dim V_+−dim V_-)²+1440; it must also have enough common nonzero modes to make Tr E² nonnegative. Neither addition is prohibited by the definition.

The necessary inequality N_n≥0 holds for *all* n here: n=1,…,5 are the nonnegative exact values in §8 of the worked example, and for n≥6,

   N_n ≥ 1+17^n−1442·17^(n/2)>0.

Therefore negativity of N_n cannot refute the unrestricted existence claim. **OPEN:** no homogeneous Kraus family realizing this exact net divisor, and no obstruction excluding all such families with cancelling modes, is established here. The finite spectral package and the square construction alone do not supply either. This is an inverse CP realization problem, not a consequence of Jacquet–Langlands.

### 2. Why the suggested signed-walk Kraus construction fails

**REFUTED (signed transition as a Kraus transition).** Replacing a signed transition e→f by A=ε|f⟩⟨e| loses its sign:

   A|e⟩⟨e|A* = |f⟩⟨f|,   ε∈{±1}.

More generally Ad(−U)=Ad(U). Thus a scalar orientation sign in B_e is not reproduced by that permutation/rank-one Kraus prescription. One can put a sign into *relative* phases between auxiliary bond components, but then coherences and population spectra must also be computed; they cannot simply be declared to equal B_e and B_v. The cochain quotient by gradients and removal of (1−u²)^5760 are legitimate operations on a spectral divisor, not automatically CP compressions onto matrix subalgebras. This identifies the failed step in the candidate without claiming that every alternative is impossible.

**PROVED (the actual spectral identity).** With the signed linear B_e and unsigned B_v,

   N_n = Tr B_v^n − Tr B_e^n + 5760(1+(−1)^n),
   Z(u) = (1−u²)^(-5760) Z_v(u)/Z_e(u).

This is a graded spectral transfer with a designated correction. It is not, by that identity, a doubled-bond CP transfer.

### 3. What the Weil construction does realize

**PROVED (CP, and a useful precise memory construction).** Each Φ_± on M₇ or M₆ is a genuine random-unitary CPTP map. A Hashimoto map with unitary transport can also be realized as a CP *transfer with a letter register*. On V⊗C¹⁸ use Kraus operators

   A_(j,i) = U_i ⊗ |j⟩⟨i|,   j≠rev(i).

They annihilate input coherences between different letter registers. On the invariant diagonal-register algebra ⊕_i End(V) the map is exactly

   (E X)_j = Σ_(i≠rev(j)) U_i X_i U_i*,

the block convention of `weil_lps_hashimoto.py` (rev is an involution). All other eigenvalues are zero. The counting transfer has growth 17 and E/17 is CPTP. If U_i are homogeneous for a specified bond parity P, then A_(j,i) are homogeneous for P⊗I, so the same construction realizes the notebook's *graded* Hashimoto supertraces as CP supertraces, with the zero register coherences accounted for. This proves a genuine CP realization in that setting, rather than merely renaming a matrix.

**SHARPENED (which Weil grading).** The script on M₆ has the trivial doubled parity: P=−I on its bond still gives Ad P=I. It has no odd sector. The full Weil bond C⁷⊕C⁶ does give a homogeneous Kraus family with all letters even; its sector dimensions are 85 and 84. Its cross sector is not M₆. In particular its mixed-block conjugation representation need not descend from SL₂(F₁₃) to PSL₂(F₁₃), since the central −I acts there by −1. The PSL Cayley containment proved for the two diagonal matrix blocks does not by itself prove the mixed-sector Ramanujan bound. Likewise the index-two irreducible PGL examples of `thm:graded-harrow` have their own hypotheses; 17 being square modulo 13 makes this a different setup. Existence of these genuine CP constructions does not identify their net divisor with the present N_n.

**REFUTED (Hilbert–Schmidt supplies the companion metric for free).** Inverse-paired unitary letters make Σ self-adjoint for Hilbert–Schmidt; they do not force its nontrivial eigenvalues into [−2√17,2√17]. For the Bass companion,

   F₁*F₁ = [[T²+I,−17T], [−17T,289I]] ≠ 17I.

The metric G is built *using* the Hilbert–Schmidt/edge inner product and T, and is positive only after the strict band bound. It is not the Hilbert–Schmidt identity on the doubled space, nor does self-adjointness make it the exit-normalized symmetric ray. B4 gives the actual modal weights. This is exactly the distinction preserved by the notebook's `obs:kraus-dichotomy`.

## B7 — SHARPENED consequences; OPEN arithmetic analogy

**PROVED / SHARPENED.** The finite certificate establishes positivity exactly when the strict companion band condition holds. B3 supplies a classical weight-two explanation for the *weak* band bound, and the exact polynomial supplies strictness. What does not follow is that this is Deninger's positive Hodge structure or the Petersson metric transported from a modular curve: B4 identifies the missing comparison and normalization. The genus-721-sized system is a useful nontrivial metric test because multiplicity and normalization matter, but its many metrics do not settle the notebook's symmetric-ray question. The squares genuinely supply the transverse Hecke correspondence; they do not supply the ordinary cup pairing on the doubled space.

**OPEN (analogy), REFUTED (the proposed space).** The suggested pair “dilation model space, a prime transport” is a reasonable research analogy, not a proved identification with K and T₁₇. Commutation of log-p and log-q translations only gives commuting operators; it supplies neither the arithmetic quotient nor the cohomological and positivity inputs. Since log p/log q is irrational, Z log p+Z log q is dense in R, so R_+×/(p^Zq^Z) with its quotient topology is non-Hausdorff, not a solenoid or an established Deninger space for Spec Z. An adelic or inverse-limit replacement needs its own definition. In the Riemann-channel setting one would need a controlled infinite-dimensional cohomology/model space, closed operators and domains, a trace/regularized determinant, prime-period trace identities, and a positive comparison on that same realization; none follows from finite dimension or commuting dilations. Shards 04q/04r already distinguish dilation cyclicity from selection of K_S and record that the proposed zero evaluators are not a Riesz basis under RH, so a formal eigenmode-by-eigenmode finite metric cannot simply be carried over.
